# V21.2 — Reviewer authentication and trust model

**Status:** DESIGN PROPOSAL — owner decision required. This document does not authorise implementation, reviewer appointments, credential collection, production content, catalogue approval or publication.

**Design basis:** owner PASS of the Production Catalogue Curation & Qualified Reviewer Gate.
**Inspected branch:** `V21.2-development`, HEAD `5d85524e8aa12431ff83e27ec282a5d2c1cf1865`.
**Relevant implementation finding:** `src/data/catalogue-delivery.js` currently validates review-binding structure and target digest only. It does not authenticate reviewer identity, qualifications, scope, signatures, current status or revocation.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Adopt a **signed, offline-verifiable chain of trust** for catalogue approvals, with human credential verification performed by a controlled reviewer-governance process before a reviewer is authorised.

The proposed trust chain is:

1. A governance authority verifies a person's identity and relevant professional credential using authoritative sources and records the verification privately.
2. The governance authority issues a scoped reviewer credential/authorisation to that person, linked to a reviewer signing key.
3. The reviewer signs an approval attestation over the exact content digest, review scope, evidence set and decision.
4. The governance authority publishes a signed reviewer-registry and revocation snapshot. This binds the reviewer's public key to the approved scope and validity state.
5. A separate catalogue release authority validates the approvals, separation-of-duties rules, evidence completeness and release contents, then signs the immutable catalogue release manifest.
6. The offline app verifies the release signature, reviewer authorisation chain, review-attestation signature, target digest, scope and signed registry/revocation snapshot before treating the content as verified.

The app does not need to receive or store reviewer credential documents. Those remain in a restricted governance record. The public catalogue carries pseudonymous reviewer references, public keys or key references, scope IDs, signed attestations and minimal verification metadata.

**Important limitation:** offline cryptography can prove that a trusted key signed a specific approval and that the approval was authorised according to the last trusted registry snapshot the device possesses. It cannot prove that a credential has not been revoked since that snapshot while the device is disconnected. The UI and matching logic must expose this freshness limitation and fail closed where current authority cannot be established.

## 2. Trust roles and separation of duties

Keep these roles distinct. One person may hold multiple roles only where the policy explicitly allows it; high-consequence approvals require separation.

| Role | Responsibility | Must not be assumed |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewer | Assesses content within their authorised scope and signs a decision | Signing key alone does not prove qualification |
| Credential verifier | Checks identity, registration/credential, issuing authority, status and scope | A self-declared title or uploaded certificate alone is not verification |
| Reviewer registrar | Authorises reviewer identity/key/scope and maintains validity/revocation records | Must not approve the content they authored |
| Catalogue curator | Prepares proposed records and evidence bundle | Curator status is not reviewer approval |
| Independent reviewer | Performs required second review within their own scope | Must not simply countersign without examining evidence |
| Release authority | Checks all gates and signs an immutable release | Must not override missing reviewer scope or incomplete evidence |
| Trust-root custodian | Controls root signing keys, rotation and recovery | Must not be a single unreviewed point of control |

### 2.1 Reviewer scope

Authorisation is explicit and narrow. The signed registry must identify the scopes the reviewer may approve, including any exclusions or constraints. Proposed scopes remain those defined in the governance gate:

- exercise identity and execution;
- equipment identity and configuration;
- movement-demand and injury-relevance metadata;
- programme-slot identity;
- guide/instruction content.

Movement-demand/injury-relevance review requires a currently credentialed professional whose verified scope covers the actual movement/loading questions. A reviewer may decline or leave fields unknown. The app must not infer competence from a different scope.

### 2.2 Separation rules

- A curator/author cannot be the sole approver of their own proposed content.
- A reviewer cannot approve outside their active registered scope.
- A reviewer cannot approve their own reviewer credential or change their own scopes.
- A registrar cannot unilaterally issue and activate their own registrar authority.
- High-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance profiles require two independent, appropriately scoped reviews, with at least one reviewer meeting the project's designated professional qualification policy.
- A release authority cannot convert a rejected, missing, expired, revoked or out-of-scope review into an approval.
- Any emergency correction process must preserve the prior release and audit trail; it must not bypass required review.

The exact credential criteria, independence standard and registrar appointment policy require a separate governance decision. This document does not invent professional qualification thresholds.

## 3. Proposed signed data model

The following is a conceptual contract, not an approved schema or algorithm selection.

### 3.1 Reviewer authorisation credential

A signed authorisation record should bind:

- stable pseudonymous reviewer ID;
- reviewer signing-key ID and public key (or certificate reference);
- permitted review scope IDs and explicit exclusions;
- credential class/category and jurisdiction where relevant, without publishing unnecessary personal data;
- authorisation start and end;
- status and registry sequence;
- credential-verification record reference;
- registrar identity/key reference and signature;
- issue, renewal, suspension and revocation history references.

The restricted reviewer register retains the person's verified identity, authoritative credential references, verification evidence, verifier, dates, scope assessment and recheck outcome. Do not put private credential documents, home address, personal contact details or unnecessary identity data into the public catalogue.

### 3.2 Review attestation

Each reviewer's signed attestation binds:

- immutable review-record ID;
- reviewer ID and signing-key ID;
- exact scope ID;
- target type, canonical target ID and target digest;
- decision (approved, rejected, returned-for-revision);
- exact evidence references, including version and locator where available;
- review protocol/checklist version;
- limitations, excluded fields and unresolved questions;
- signed decision timestamp and attestation format/version.

The signature must cover a canonical, unambiguous serialization of all fields. The canonicalisation rules and cryptographic algorithms must be selected in a later technical specification and tested against cross-runtime/browser implementations.

A change to the target content, evidence set, review scope or review protocol that affects the approval invalidates the old attestation for the changed target. A review record is never edited in place; a new record supersedes it.

### 3.3 Signed reviewer registry and revocation snapshot

A governance-signed registry snapshot should include:

- monotonically increasing registry sequence;
- issue time and declared validity/freshness window;
- active reviewer authorisations and public-key bindings;
- scopes and constraints;
- key status and credential status;
- revocations, suspensions and superseded keys;
- reference to the prior snapshot or signed transition;
- registrar signature(s).

A device must reject rollback to a lower registry sequence once it has securely retained a higher trusted sequence. The app must not accept an unsigned or self-asserted registry. A snapshot's signature must chain to a trust root already trusted by the app.

Revocation records should distinguish reviewer-authorisation revocation from key compromise, and record effective time/reason category without exposing private personal details. If a key is compromised, approvals signed by it since the last known-good point require explicit review and possible release withdrawal.

### 3.4 Catalogue release attestation

The release authority signs a manifest that binds:

- catalogue ID, immutable release ID, version and payload digest;
- exact schema and canonicalisation versions;
- all included equipment, exercise, variation, vocabulary and guide content digests;
- every required review attestation reference;
- reviewer-registry snapshot sequence/digest used for verification;
- release-policy/checklist version;
- release issue time and validity metadata;
- release authority key ID and signature.

The existing manifest byte-integrity checks remain useful but are not a substitute for a trusted release signature. A digest proves bytes match; a signature proves a trusted key attested to those bytes, subject to trust-root integrity.

## 4. Trust roots, key custody and rotation

### 4.1 Trust root

The app needs a pre-established trust root for reviewer-governance and release-authority signatures. It must not trust a public key merely because that key appears inside the catalogue it is being asked to verify.

The root key is not a routine reviewer key. Keep it offline or in an appropriately controlled signing environment, restrict access, require multi-person authorisation for sensitive operations, and maintain a documented recovery/rotation procedure.

### 4.2 Key roles

Use separate key roles and identifiers for:
- root trust;
- reviewer registrar/registry signing;
- individual reviewer approval signing;
- catalogue release signing.

Do not reuse one key across roles. The final design must specify supported algorithms, key sizes, canonical encoding, signature envelope, key IDs, cryptographic library and browser compatibility. No algorithm is selected by this proposal.

### 4.3 Rotation and compromise

- Planned rotation publishes a signed transition from the old trusted key to the new key, with an overlap period where feasible.
- Root rotation requires a separately controlled trust-anchor update and recovery path; it cannot be bootstrapped solely by a new key embedded in an untrusted catalogue.
- Reviewer key rotation revokes/supersedes the old key and issues a new reviewer authorisation after identity re-verification.
- Suspected compromise triggers suspension/revocation, affected-approval analysis, and release withdrawal or replacement where necessary.
- Never rewrite historical attestations. Preserve their signatures and add signed revocation/supersession events.
- If the trust chain cannot be verified, the release is unverified; do not silently downgrade to trusting structural fields.

## 5. Offline verification and freshness policy

This is the most important limitation for Gym Comeback's offline-first operation.

### 5.1 What an offline device can verify

With a previously trusted root and signed snapshots, the device can verify:
- catalogue release signature and payload digest;
- reviewer attestation signature and exact target digest;
- reviewer key's authorisation and scope in the cached signed registry;
- registry/revocation snapshot signature and sequence;
- release references to the exact registry snapshot used at publication;
- schema, evidence-reference structure and required review coverage.

### 5.2 What it cannot know while disconnected

An offline device cannot know about a credential expiry, suspension, reviewer removal, key compromise or newly issued revocation that occurred after its latest signed snapshot. It also cannot reliably establish trusted current time merely from a user-controlled device clock.

Therefore distinguish these states in product logic and UI:

1. **Signature invalid / trust chain invalid:** reject the release or approval; no verified content use.
2. **Verified against current-enough signed snapshot:** signature and scope are valid and the freshness policy is satisfied.
3. **Historically verified, freshness unknown:** signatures validate against a previously trusted snapshot, but the device cannot establish whether later revocation occurred.
4. **Expired or revoked in known snapshot:** do not treat the approval as current; block new use as a reviewed candidate.
5. **Missing snapshot or unsupported algorithm/schema:** unverified; fail closed.

The app must not label state 3 as “current”, “qualified now” or “fully verified”. It must display the as-of sequence/date and explain that online refresh is required to establish current status.

### 5.3 Freshness policy recommendation

- Online publication always checks the latest trusted registry and revocation state.
- A release may be published only if all required reviewers are authorised and not revoked at the release decision point.
- The release pins the exact signed registry snapshot and its sequence/digest.
- Offline exercise descriptions and historical workout snapshots may remain viewable if their integrity checks pass, but they must not imply current reviewer status.
- Injury-aware alternative matching must fail closed when reviewer/revocation freshness is unknown or outside the policy window. No injury-aware candidate is offered based solely on a stale cached approval.
- A user must never be forced online merely to access their own historical workout data.
- The final maximum freshness window for each scope is a governance decision. Do not invent a universal number in code.
- Since device clocks are not authoritative, freshness enforcement should be based on signed trusted timestamps/online refresh policy and explicit offline state, not a local clock alone.

## 6. Release and runtime verification flow

### 6.1 At publication

1. Curator freezes a candidate payload and calculates canonical target digests.
2. Reviewers receive the exact immutable target and evidence set.
3. Each reviewer examines within scope and signs an attestation.
4. Registrar verifies that each reviewer key and scope are active for the relevant review event, with credential checks current.
5. Independent-review rules and completeness rules are checked.
6. Release authority validates all signatures, digests, scopes, evidence references, registry sequence and release policy.
7. Release authority signs the immutable release manifest.
8. The signed release, required registry snapshot and revocation state are published as immutable assets.
9. Audit record stores the exact release, checks, signer IDs and policy version.

### 6.2 In the offline app

1. Load the active release pointer and exact immutable manifest.
2. Verify manifest signature against the pre-established release trust root.
3. Verify payload byte length/digest and schema.
4. Verify registry snapshot and revocation signature against the governance trust root; reject rollback.
5. Verify each required reviewer attestation and that the reviewer key was authorised for that scope in the pinned snapshot.
6. Verify exact target digest, evidence binding and independent-review rules.
7. Derive a clear trust/freshness state; do not infer one from a text field such as `decision: "approved"`.
8. Only verified, sufficiently fresh content may participate in injury-aware alternative candidate screening. Unknown, stale or invalid state fails closed.
9. Preserve the last known-good immutable release if a candidate fails any verification step.

The implementation must be atomic with the existing release staging/activation model: failed signature or trust checks must never replace the active release.

## 7. Threat model and required mitigations

| Threat | Required mitigation | Residual limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Forged `decision: approved` field | Require valid reviewer signature, active scoped authorisation and signed release | Does not prove the reviewer made a sound judgement |
| Fake reviewer ID/scope/evidence strings | Resolve key and scope through signed registry; bind evidence and target digest | Evidence quality still needs human review |
| Content changed after review | Signature covers exact digest; changed digest invalidates approval | Correct canonicalisation is essential |
| Reviewer credential expired/revoked | Registrar rechecks; signed revocation snapshots; freshness state | Offline device cannot learn later revocation |
| Reviewer key stolen | Revoke key, analyse affected attestations, withdraw affected releases | Signatures made before compromise may need forensic assessment |
| Registry rollback | Monotonic sequence and retained highest trusted sequence | First-use devices need a trusted initial bootstrap |
| Release asset replaced | Signed manifest plus digest and immutable release ID | Compromised app shell/trust root remains a separate threat |
| Malicious catalogue publisher | Separate release key, release policy and audit trail | Release authority itself remains a trusted role |
| One insider approves own work | Separation of duties and independent review | Collusion between authorised people remains possible |
| Malicious/incorrect evidence | Provenance, exact locators, qualified review and audit | Cryptography cannot prove evidence truth |
| Compromised hosting/CDN | Verify signatures before activation; immutable cache; keep last known-good release | Availability can still be disrupted |
| Compromised app code or trust root | Controlled app release process and protected trust anchors | A malicious client can bypass its own local checks; this is not a server-enforced DRM boundary |
| Device clock manipulation | Do not rely on local clock as sole proof of current status | Offline current-time freshness cannot be guaranteed |
| Privacy leakage | Keep identity/credential documents in restricted register; publish pseudonymous IDs/minimal metadata | Review records still need access controls |

**Security boundary:** client-side verification protects against untrusted or corrupted catalogue assets when the shipped app and trust anchors are genuine. It does not make a user-controlled browser/device tamper-proof, and it cannot prevent a technically capable user from modifying their local app. Do not market it as tamper-proof or as a guarantee of medical correctness.

## 8. Failure behaviour

- Invalid signature, missing key, unknown scope, mismatched digest, missing evidence, unsupported algorithm, malformed registry or rollback: reject candidate release/approval.
- Revoked or suspended reviewer/key in the known snapshot: approval is not current; block new candidate use.
- Stale registry/revocation state: mark historically verified but freshness unknown; block injury-aware candidate use.
- Online refresh failure: retain last known-good release and historical records; do not activate candidate.
- Missing release asset after restore: retain historical workout snapshots; report catalogue verification unavailable; do not silently substitute a newer release.
- Key compromise: suspend affected approvals/releases pending review; retain audit evidence.
- No valid alternative after fail-closed screening: offer skip/remove or keep restricted, not an unreviewed guess.

## 9. Privacy, governance and operational controls

Before collecting any reviewer information, define:
- lawful purpose and consent/notice;
- minimum identity and credential data required;
- access roles, encryption and audit logging for restricted records;
- retention and deletion schedule, including credential expiry and historical approval obligations;
- secure credential verification and re-verification sources;
- incident response for compromised reviewer or signing keys;
- reviewer onboarding/offboarding and appeal/correction process;
- independent review and periodic registrar audit;
- responsibility for funding, maintenance and availability of the trust service.

Do not place private credential files in Git, public assets, the app bundle, user backups or the public catalogue. Do not ask the user to supply personal credentials as a substitute for professional review.

## 10. Technical decisions still required before implementation

A later implementation gate must decide and prove:

1. Trust-root ownership, custody, bootstrap and recovery.
2. Reviewer identity/credential verification process and scope-specific qualification policy.
3. Registrar authority, separation-of-duties and independent approval policy.
4. Signature algorithms, canonical serialization, envelope format and browser-compatible cryptographic implementation.
5. Registry format, monotonic sequence, revocation format and snapshot freshness policy.
6. Reviewer private-key custody, signing UX, key recovery and compromise response.
7. Release-authority signing workflow and audit storage.
8. Offline policy per content scope, especially injury-relevance approvals.
9. App-shell/trust-anchor update and rollback protection.
10. Privacy, data retention, access control and operational ownership.
11. Exact impact on catalogue payload schema, release manifest, cache staging/activation and tests.
12. Security test plan: forged approvals, key mismatch, scope escalation, expired/revoked reviewer, stale snapshot, rollback, digest mutation, evidence mutation, compromised key, interrupted download, cache corruption and offline restart.

No code or schema change should start until these decisions are resolved and the owner approves a bounded implementation gate. If this requires a new service, remote dependency, signing infrastructure, database/backup change or broader trust boundary, it must be explicitly presented and approved rather than folded into C1/C2.

## 11. Explicit exclusions

This proposal does not authorise:
- reviewer registration or collection of real credential data;
- generating reviewer keys or trust roots;
- choosing cryptographic algorithms or implementing signatures;
- changes to `catalogue-delivery.js`, payload schema, manifest or service worker;
- production catalogue content, IDs, approvals or publication;
- new server, API, remote service or hosted signing infrastructure;
- database schema/store/index or backup-format changes;
- C3 restriction storage/logic, Adaptive Brain, injury-aware runtime implementation;
- Equipment Manager, Exercise Library or Programme Builder UI;
- changes to `main`, Pages release candidate, Pages settings, deployment or production cutover.
