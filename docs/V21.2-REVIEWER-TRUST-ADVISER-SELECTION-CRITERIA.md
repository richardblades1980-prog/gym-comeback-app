## 1. Purpose and authority

This document defines a fair, evidence-based method for assessing potential advisers and preparing a shortlist for the professional, governance, Australian privacy/legal and independent security input required by the Reviewer Trust workstream.

It is a selection framework, not a list of actual candidates and not an engagement instruction.

**This document authorises no:**
- searching for, contacting, approaching, inviting or advertising to candidates;
- sharing project files or information with an external person or organisation;
- requesting or receiving quotations, proposals or confidential material;
- selecting, appointing, retaining or contracting an adviser;
- spending, committing funds or agreeing to recurring fees;
- collecting identity documents, credentials, personal information or sensitive records;
- choosing a provider, architecture, cryptographic product or operational service;
- changing application code, schemas, APIs, databases, backups, service workers, runtime, `main`, Pages settings, release candidate or deployment.

Any candidate research or contact must occur only after its own explicit owner approval. Any proposed engagement, scope, price and information exchange must return for separate approval before commitment.

## 2. Selection principles

1. **Relevant competence:** assess each person only for the discipline and questions within their demonstrated expertise.
2. **Australian applicability:** professional and legal advice must address the actual Australian context and clearly state jurisdictional limits.
3. **Independence:** identify financial, professional, personal, vendor and prior-project conflicts before relying on advice.
4. **Evidence over reputation:** use verifiable qualifications, current standing, relevant work and written methodology rather than marketing claims or prestige alone.
5. **Defined scope:** do not treat a broad title, membership or general experience as proof of competence for every requested question.
6. **Proportionate engagement:** buy only the specific input required; do not create an open-ended advisory relationship by default.
7. **Traceability:** link every deliverable to the stable evidence-request IDs in the Evidence Response Register.
8. **No assumed consensus:** preserve disagreement, uncertainty, limitations and advice outside scope.
9. **Privacy by design:** use public professional information for preliminary assessment; do not request identity or credential document copies at this stage.
10. **No authority by selection:** selecting an adviser does not appoint a reviewer, approve production content or confer operational authority.

## 3. Adviser workstreams and minimum suitability evidence

### 3.1 Professional scope and exercise-content review

**Purpose:** resolve ER-PRO-01 to ER-PRO-10.

Potential advisers must demonstrate:
- current, relevant Australian professional registration, accreditation or other authoritative standing where applicable to the discipline and work proposed;
- experience assessing exercise, movement, physical activity or health-related claims relevant to the proposed review categories;
- ability to define competence boundaries, exclusions and referral points;
- familiarity with evidence quality, population applicability, contraindications, uncertainty and reassessment;
- willingness to distinguish general exercise guidance from diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation or individual clinical clearance;
- ability to provide a written scope-by-scope rationale and identify questions requiring another discipline.

The exact discipline and qualification pathway must be determined from each content claim by appropriately qualified professionals. This framework does not prescribe a universal credential or imply that one profession can cover all categories.

### 3.2 Governance and operating model

**Purpose:** resolve ER-GOV-01 to ER-GOV-10.

Potential advisers must demonstrate:
- relevant governance, risk, professional-regulation or organisational-design experience;
- experience designing authority, delegation and separation-of-duties arrangements;
- practical understanding of conflicts, complaints, investigations, appeals and procedural fairness;
- ability to specify accountability, continuity, audit and emergency controls;
- independence from any provider or proposed operational arrangement they are asked to assess;
- ability to distinguish governance principles from an operationally ready scheme.

Governance input may be provided by a suitably experienced specialist or a team. Legal questions must be referred to an appropriately qualified Australian legal adviser.

### 3.3 Australian privacy and legal assessment

**Purpose:** resolve ER-LEGAL-01 to ER-LEGAL-10.

Potential advisers must demonstrate:
- admission and current entitlement to practise in the relevant Australian jurisdiction, as applicable to the proposed legal work;
- demonstrable experience in the legal domains actually engaged by the operating model, potentially including privacy, health information, consumer protection, professional regulation, records and technology/provider arrangements;
- ability to analyse the actual entity, data flows, user groups, purposes and providers rather than give generic compliance assurances;
- ability to distinguish binding legal requirements, regulator guidance, risk-management recommendations and unresolved assumptions;
- experience with privacy impact assessment, data minimisation, retention, disclosure, cross-border data and incident/breach obligations where relevant;
- appropriate professional indemnity and engagement arrangements, to be verified before any engagement.

No legal regime or conclusion is assumed by this selection framework. Applicability must be assessed against the actual facts and operating model.

### 3.4 Independent security architecture and assurance

**Purpose:** resolve ER-SEC-01 to ER-SEC-10.

Potential advisers must demonstrate:
- relevant experience in application security and threat modelling;
- practical experience with browser applications and offline-first behaviour;
- relevant cryptographic trust-chain, signature verification, key management, revocation and recovery expertise;
- understanding of service-worker/cache behaviour, backup/restore, rollback and data integrity;
- ability to test security claims and document attack paths, assumptions, residual risks and remediation;
- independence from vendors whose products or services may be considered;
- willingness to provide an evidence-based assessment rather than steer toward a preselected product.

Specialist cryptographic review may be a separate capability if the primary security assessor cannot demonstrate that competence.

## 4. Common assessment criteria

Each potential adviser may be assessed against the following criteria. Evidence must be recorded, not inferred.

| Criterion | What must be demonstrated |
|---|---|
| Discipline fit | Direct match between expertise and the assigned questions |
| Qualifications and standing | Relevant, current, independently checkable qualifications or professional status |
| Relevant experience | Comparable work, with role and contribution clearly described |
| Australian context | Appropriate jurisdictional knowledge and clear limits |
| Method and evidence quality | Transparent method, authoritative sources, reproducible reasoning |
| Independence | Disclosed interests and ability to provide impartial advice |
| Scope discipline | Clear exclusions, referral points and no claims outside competence |
| Written deliverables | Ability to answer the ER-* questions with sources, assumptions and limitations |
| Privacy/security practice | Proportionate handling of project and any later restricted information |
| Availability and continuity | Realistic capacity, delivery timeframe and named backup arrangements if relevant |
| Cost transparency | Written fee basis, assumptions, expenses, GST treatment where applicable and change-control |
| Professional safeguards | Appropriate insurance, liability terms and complaints/escalation arrangements, as relevant |

A criterion may be marked **verified**, **partially evidenced**, **not evidenced**, or **not applicable with rationale**. Missing evidence must not be treated as a positive result.

## 5. Workstream-specific weighting

A numerical score may help compare candidates within the same workstream, but it must not replace minimum requirements or professional judgement.

Suggested weighting:

| Criterion | Professional | Governance | Legal/privacy | Security |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Discipline fit and demonstrated competence | 25% | 20% | 25% | 25% |
| Qualifications / standing / authority | 20% | 10% | 20% | 10% |
| Relevant applied experience | 15% | 20% | 10% | 20% |
| Method and evidence quality | 15% | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Independence and conflicts | 10% | 15% | 10% | 10% |
| Scope discipline and written deliverables | 10% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Cost and delivery practicality | 5% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** |

Scores are comparative aids only. Do not compare raw scores across different workstreams. A high total cannot cure a missing mandatory qualification, unresolved material conflict, lack of jurisdictional authority or inability to answer the assigned questions.

Before applying the scoring table, the assessor must identify the mandatory pass/fail requirements for that particular workstream. A candidate failing a mandatory requirement is not shortlist-eligible for that scope, regardless of score.

## 6. Conflict and independence checks

Before any candidate can be recommended, the selection assessment must consider whether they:
- sell, represent, resell or receive referral benefits from a provider or product they may be asked to assess;
- have a financial interest in the project's implementation, procurement or future operations;
- have designed or implemented a system they would independently assure;
- have personal, family, employment or close professional relationships with project decision-makers or other candidates;
- have a dispute, complaint, prior decision or other interest that could reasonably affect impartiality;
- propose to assess work outside their competence or professional authority.

A disclosed conflict is not automatically disqualifying. Its materiality, manageability and any required recusal must be documented by the authorised decision-maker. An unmanageable material conflict disqualifies the candidate from the affected independent role.

Do not collect detailed personal conflict records in ordinary repository documentation. Record only a minimal assessment outcome and keep any restricted record in a separately approved process, if one is later authorised.

## 7. Shortlist methodology

If and when candidate research is separately authorised, use this sequence:

1. **Confirm scope first.** Break the input brief into bounded work packages and map each package to ER-* identifiers. Identify mandatory competence and independence requirements before looking at candidates.
2. **Define the search boundary.** Specify relevant Australian jurisdiction, acceptable professional bodies or authoritative standing sources, geographic/remote delivery constraints and any required specialist sub-discipline.
3. **Identify possible sources.** Use official regulator or professional-body directories, recognised professional networks, reputable specialist firms and independently verifiable work. Do not rely solely on sponsored rankings, testimonials or vendor referrals.
4. **Create a longlist.** Record only public professional information needed to assess relevance. Do not contact candidates or collect private personal details at this stage unless separately authorised.
5. **Apply mandatory screens.** Exclude candidates whose authority, competence, independence or scope fit cannot be adequately evidenced.
6. **Assess remaining candidates.** Apply the common criteria and workstream-specific weighting. Record evidence and uncertainty for every material score.
7. **Check coverage and gaps.** Confirm whether the proposed shortlist collectively covers every required question. Identify where separate specialist input is necessary.
8. **Prepare a shortlist recommendation.** Present the strongest suitable options, alternatives, gaps, conflicts, indicative public fee information if reliably available, and reasons for inclusion/exclusion.
9. **Return for owner decision.** No candidate is contacted, invited, selected, appointed or engaged until the owner separately approves the proposed next action.
10. **Separate engagement approval.** For each approved candidate, present exact scope, deliverables, fee/expense cap, timetable, confidentiality, information-sharing terms and exit conditions for explicit approval before commitment.

The shortlist should normally contain more than one viable option where the market and specialist field reasonably permit. If only one suitable option is found, explain the search limits and why alternatives were unavailable rather than presenting the result as competitive selection.

## 8. Deliverable and evidence acceptance

Any later adviser deliverable should:
- identify the ER-* evidence-request IDs answered;
- identify the adviser’s relevant discipline, authority and scope;
- state jurisdiction, assumptions, product context and date;
- distinguish requirements, recommendations, options and unresolved matters;
- cite authoritative sources, standards, versions and effective dates;
- disclose limitations, contrary evidence and conflicts;
- identify dependencies on other workstreams;
- state whether the adviser considers the answer within their competence;
- be suitable for independent review and owner decision.

Receipt does not equal acceptance. The Evidence Response Register must follow its evidence-quality and closure rules. No evidence item may be closed merely because an adviser has supplied an answer or because a document exists.

## 9. Cost and procurement safeguards

Any later quotation or proposal must separate:
- fixed-fee deliverables;
- hourly or day rates and estimated hours;
- disbursements and travel;
- applicable taxes;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- optional work;
- change-control and overrun approval;
- cancellation and termination terms;
- ownership/licensing of deliverables;
- confidentiality, retention and secure deletion;
- professional indemnity and liability provisions where appropriate.

No expenditure ceiling is created by this document. All fees, expenses and contractual terms require explicit owner approval before commitment.

## 10. Shortlist record template

If candidate research is later authorised, use one record per candidate with only necessary professional information:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Candidate reference | Neutral identifier, not a public personal profile |
| Workstream | Professional / governance / legal-privacy / security |
| Organisation | Public professional organisation name, if relevant |
| Discipline and scope | Exact questions the candidate may be competent to address |
| Public qualifications / standing | Source and date checked; no document copies |
| Relevant experience | Verifiable examples and candidate's stated role |
| Australian jurisdiction | Applicable scope and limitations |
| Independence | Conflict status and minimal outcome; restricted details kept elsewhere if authorised |
| Evidence links | Public sources supporting assessment |
| Mandatory screen | Pass / fail / unresolved with reason |
| Criteria assessment | Evidence and score by criterion |
| Availability | Confirmed only after authorised contact |
| Fee basis | Public information or quote only after authorised request |
| Recommendation | Shortlist / reserve / not suitable / insufficient evidence |
| Decision status | Prepared / owner review / separately approved / not approved |

Do not populate this template with invented candidates, assumed credentials, inferred availability or estimated private fees.

## 12. Non-negotiable product safeguards

All adviser work and any later recommendations must preserve these requirements:
- missing, invalid, stale, conflicting, revoked or out-of-scope trust fails closed for affected review-dependent catalogue behaviour;
- no signature or digest is represented as proof of professional competence or clinical correctness;
- offline status must not be represented as current where freshness cannot be established;
- unverified substitutions must not be represented as professionally approved;
- higher-consequence injury-aware matching remains blocked until its separate requirements are satisfied;
- historical workouts and user-owned records remain accessible and unchanged if catalogue trust is absent, stale, restricted, withdrawn or invalid;
- restricted identity, credential, conflict, complaint, investigation and security information remains separate from public catalogue data and ordinary application backups.
