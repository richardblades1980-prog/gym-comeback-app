# V21.2 — G-10 offline freshness and status meaning

**Status:** DESIGN PROPOSAL — owner decision required. This document proposes governance meanings and rules for approval freshness, revocation awareness and offline use of reviewer-trust information. It does not select cryptographic mechanisms, implement a registry or cache, define production catalogue status fields, or authorise runtime, backup or deployment changes.

**Governance basis:** G-01 through G-09 owner directions/framework approvals recorded in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. In particular, G-07 requires exact target/version binding and explicit uncertainty; G-08 requires traceable restriction/withdrawal decisions; G-09 requires privacy-protective handling of governance records.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve clear, user-safe meanings for approval status when a device is online, offline, delayed in synchronising or unable to establish whether a reviewer approval remains valid.

The central rule is that a locally cached approval proves only what the locally held, verifiable record says about a specific target and time. It does not prove that no later suspension, expiry, correction or withdrawal has occurred. The application must not turn an unknown or stale state into a current approval.

The policy must preserve the user's ability to access historical workout records even when catalogue trust information is unavailable or restricted.

## 2. Status vocabulary

Use distinct states with explicit meanings. Exact enum names and schemas belong to a later technical gate.

- **Current (verified):** the latest required authoritative status has been checked within the approved freshness policy, the approval is active, the exact target/version matches, and no known restriction or superseding decision applies.
- **Historically verified:** the approval was valid at a stated past verification time, but the system cannot establish that it remains current now. This is a historical statement only.
- **Stale:** the record was once verifiable, but its freshness interval has elapsed or a required refresh has not succeeded. Stale is not current.
- **Unknown:** required status, provenance, time, integrity or applicability cannot be established. Unknown must not be represented as approved.
- **Restricted:** an authorised precautionary or governance restriction currently limits reliance on the relevant claim, record, reviewer or scope.
- **Suspended:** reviewer authority or approval use is formally paused under the applicable governance process.
- **Withdrawn/revoked:** a final recorded decision ends reliance on the approval or authority, effective at a specified time.
- **Expired:** the approval or reviewer appointment has reached its approved end time without a valid renewal.
- **Superseded:** a later decision or target version replaces this record; the older record remains historical and traceable.
- **Rejected:** the submitted review did not satisfy the applicable requirements and was not approved.

Do not collapse these states into a single inactive/unavailable flag. A stale cache, a known withdrawal and a temporary restriction are materially different facts.

## 3. Exact binding and provenance

A status assertion must bind to the exact reviewed object and decision context, including as applicable:
- Stable target identifier and immutable target/version or content digest.
- Claim and review scope.
- Reviewer reference and authority scope at the time of decision.
- Approval/decision identifier and effective time.
- Policy and checklist version.
- Evidence/review record reference.
- Issuer or authority reference and integrity/provenance metadata.
- Last authoritative status-check time and applicable freshness rule.
- Any later restriction, suspension, expiry, withdrawal, correction or superseding decision.

A status for one exercise, claim, version, reviewer scope or jurisdiction must not be inferred for another. A changed target or material evidence/protocol change requires reassessment under G-07.

A cached record with missing or mismatched target binding, incomplete provenance, failed integrity verification or ambiguous time is unknown, not current.

## 4. Freshness and time policy

Freshness periods must be justified by the risk and change rate of each approval scope. Do not assume one universal interval is appropriate for every review scope.

The approved policy should specify:
1. The maximum age of a successful authoritative status check for each scope.
2. Which events invalidate freshness immediately, including a known restriction, suspension, expiry, withdrawal, credential-status change or material correction.
3. How a device obtains and assesses trusted time, including first-use and clock-unavailable cases.
4. What happens when a refresh fails, the authority is unavailable or the local clock is unreliable.
5. Whether any narrowly scoped offline use is permitted and under what explicit conditions.
6. How freshness policy changes are versioned and communicated to devices.

A user-controlled device clock alone must not establish that an approval remains current. If trusted time cannot be established sufficiently for the decision, use unknown status for current-authority claims.

No arbitrary grace period is approved by this proposal. Any offline interval must be separately justified by scope, user risk and revocation consequences, then expressly approved.

## 5. Offline behaviour

Offline operation should distinguish access to existing user data from reliance on reviewer-approved catalogue content.

When offline:
- Preserve access to historical workouts, personal logs and locally held user data.
- Show the time and scope of the last successful status verification where useful and safe.
- Label cached approval as historically verified only when its integrity, exact binding and verification timestamp can be established.
- If freshness has elapsed or trusted time cannot be established, show stale or unknown, never current.
- Do not invent a successful refresh or imply that a cached approval has checked for later withdrawal.
- Do not silently substitute a different exercise, approval or target version because the expected record is unavailable.
- Apply any locally known restriction or withdrawal immediately; lack of connectivity is not permission to ignore a known adverse status.
- Queue permitted status refresh work for later connectivity without changing the current displayed truth.
- Keep the user informed when catalogue or approval-dependent functions are unavailable, without blocking unrelated historical workout access.

A device that has never received a valid, verifiable status record must not bootstrap trust from an unverified cache or user-supplied clock.

## 6. Safety-critical use and injury-aware matching

For high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance decisions, unknown, stale or unverifiable required approval status must fail closed for new matching or recommendation reliance.

In particular:
- Do not present an exercise as professionally reviewed/current for injury-aware matching when its required approval status cannot be established.
- Do not infer that a previously suitable exercise remains suitable for a changed injury report, changed user condition or changed target version.
- Do not resume a paused or restricted progression solely because the device reconnects or a cached status appears unchanged.
- A restriction or withdrawal known locally must be honoured even while offline.
- If the application cannot distinguish a safe unaffected component from a restricted one, withhold the affected recommendation rather than guessing.

This trust-status rule does not itself diagnose injury, grant medical clearance or replace the separate exercise adaptation and user-consent safeguards.

## 7. Revocation, propagation and conflict handling

The governance process should define how new restrictions, suspensions, withdrawals, expiries and corrections become authoritative and how devices learn of them.

The policy should require:
- An authoritative, ordered decision history with effective times and stable references.
- A clear distinction between decision time, effective time and the time a particular device learned of the change.
- A refresh mechanism and measurable propagation objectives to be set after risk assessment.
- Immediate use of a known adverse status on any device that receives it.
- No assumption that a device which has not received an update is unaffected.
- Explicit handling of conflicting, duplicated, out-of-order or unverifiable updates.
- Reconciliation after reconnection without silently overwriting user workout history or the historical record of what the device displayed.

If an update cannot be authenticated, ordered or bound to the correct target, treat the affected current status as unknown and seek authoritative reconciliation.

## 8. User-facing language and behaviour

Status explanations should be plain and avoid overstating certainty. Examples of acceptable meanings:

- **Current:** “Approval status checked on [date/time]. This exact version was shown as active at that check.”
- **Historically verified:** “This approval was verified on [date/time]. Current status has not been checked.”
- **Stale:** “This approval information is out of date. Reconnect to check current status.”
- **Unknown:** “Current approval status could not be verified.”
- **Restricted/withdrawn:** “This approval is restricted/withdrawn. Do not rely on it for new recommendations.”

These are illustrative wording patterns, not final UI copy. Do not expose restricted complaint, identity, credential or investigation details. Do not use a green/current indicator for historical, stale or unknown status.

## 9. Historical continuity and data integrity

A trust-status failure must not:
- Delete or rewrite completed workout history.
- Remove user-entered sets, repetitions, loads, notes or dates.
- Make historical records appear as if they were performed under a different exercise or approval.
- Block export or backup of user-owned history solely because a reviewer status cannot be refreshed.
- Convert a historic approval into a current approval by changing local timestamps.

Where a later withdrawal affects a historical catalogue item, preserve the historical fact of what was recorded and the status known at the time, while clearly distinguishing that from present-day approval status. Any correction or annotation must be traceable and must not silently rewrite the user's completed activity.

## 10. Monitoring, audit and governance review

The eventual process should record enough information to reconstruct:
- Which status record and exact target/version a device used.
- The last successful authoritative check and the freshness policy applied.
- Whether the device was online or offline and whether trusted time was available.
- Which restrictions or updates were known to the device at the time.
- Why a status was classified current, historical, stale, unknown or adverse.
- When queued refresh/reconciliation succeeded or failed.

Do not log unnecessary user health or workout details in reviewer-trust telemetry. Apply G-09 access, minimisation and retention principles.

Review freshness intervals and propagation objectives when material changes occur, including changes in risk, credential verification, reviewer lifecycle, policy, service availability or observed incident patterns.

## 11. Boundaries and stop conditions

This proposal does not:
- Select exact freshness durations, offline grace periods or propagation service-level targets.
- Decide a trusted-time source or clock architecture.
- Select cryptographic algorithms, signing keys, trust roots, manifests or registry protocols.
- Define production schema fields, cache format, database migrations or service-worker behaviour.
- Authorise changes to the application runtime, backup/restore, catalogue, C3, Adaptive Brain or user data model.
- Approve production catalogue content or any individual reviewer.
- Authorise publication, deployment, changes to `main` or the Pages release candidate.

Those decisions require approved governance rules, threat/risk assessment, privacy review and their own explicit technical implementation gate. No implementation should infer unresolved governance policy.
