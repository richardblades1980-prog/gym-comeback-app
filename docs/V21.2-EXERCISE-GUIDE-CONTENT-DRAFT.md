## Editorial and safety rules

1. Keep instructions short enough to read during a workout: setup, controlled movement, and one or two key cues.
2. Use neutral coaching language. Do not prescribe a universal load, repetition target, range of motion or pain threshold.
3. State that machine layouts differ and the user should follow the machine's adjustment labels or ask gym staff to check unfamiliar equipment.
4. Do not imply that muscle illustrations prove clinical activation or that an exercise is suitable for every person.
5. Encourage controlled, comfortable movement and stopping for sharp, worsening or unusual symptoms. Do not tell users to train through pain.
6. For impact/cardio exercises, make intensity and impact options explicit. A low-impact alternative must be described as a separate execution option, not a different identity.
7. These drafts require review by a qualified exercise professional before release, especially for users with injuries, persistent pain, balance limitations or relevant medical conditions.

## Proposed guide copy

### Bodyweight and core

#### Dead Bug
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with arms pointing upward and hips and knees bent. Gently brace your trunk; keep your lower back in a comfortable, steady position.
- **Movement:** Slowly lower the opposite arm and leg toward the floor only as far as you can control, then return and alternate sides.
- **Cues:** Move slowly; keep breathing; reduce the reach if your back arches or control is lost.
- **Review note:** Do not force the limbs to the floor. A shorter range is a valid regression.

#### Bird Dog
- **Setup:** Start on hands and knees with hands under shoulders and knees under hips. Brace gently and keep your spine neutral.
- **Movement:** Reach one arm forward while extending the opposite leg back. Pause briefly without twisting, return slowly, then switch sides.
- **Cues:** Keep hips level; reach long rather than lifting high; use a smaller reach if balance is difficult.
- **Review note:** A supported single-limb reach can be used before combining arm and leg.

#### Mountain Climber
- **Setup:** Start in a high plank with hands under shoulders. Use an elevated bench or stable support if a floor plank is too demanding.
- **Movement:** Bring one knee toward the torso, return the foot, then alternate sides at a controlled pace.
- **Cues:** Keep shoulders steady and trunk braced; avoid bouncing or letting the lower back sag.
- **Review note:** Offer a slow step-in/step-back option; do not present fast running-style repetitions as the default.

#### Jumping Jack
- **Setup:** Stand upright with feet under hips and arms by your sides, leaving clear space around you.
- **Movement:** Jump feet apart while raising arms, then jump feet together while lowering arms. Land softly and repeat at a pace you can control.
- **Cues:** Keep landings quiet and knees relaxed; reduce speed or range if coordination or impact is uncomfortable.
- **Review note:** Pair with the separately named Low-impact Step Jack as an alternative; do not require jumping.

#### Low-impact Step Jack
- **Setup:** Stand upright with feet under hips and arms by your sides.
- **Movement:** Step one foot out to the side while sweeping arms upward, return to centre, then step the other foot out as arms lower or repeat the same arm pattern comfortably.
- **Cues:** Keep one foot on the floor; use a steady rhythm and a comfortable side-step width.
- **Review note:** Make clear this is the no-jump option corresponding to a jumping-jack pattern.

#### High Knees (low-impact option available)
- **Setup:** Stand tall near a stable support if balance assistance is useful.
- **Movement:** Alternate lifting each knee toward a comfortable height while moving the opposite arm. For the low-impact option, march in place without hopping.
- **Cues:** Stay tall; land softly if using a jog; choose the marching version when impact or speed is unsuitable.
- **Review note:** The source name already includes a low-impact option; retain it in the guide and preserve the source identity.

#### Bicycle Crunch
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with hands lightly supporting the head and knees bent. Lift shoulders only as far as comfortable.
- **Movement:** Bring one knee toward the torso while rotating the opposite shoulder toward it. Extend the other leg only as far as you can keep control; alternate sides.
- **Cues:** Rotate through the trunk rather than pulling the head; move slowly and keep the lower back controlled.
- **Review note:** Offer a reduced-range version with feet closer to the floor; avoid forcing neck flexion.

#### Superman / Back Extension
- **Setup:** Lie face down with forehead resting comfortably and arms reaching forward or alongside the body.
- **Movement:** Gently lift the chest and/or opposite arm and leg a small distance, pause, then lower under control.
- **Cues:** Use a small lift; keep the neck in line with the spine; avoid throwing the head back or forcing the lower back into a deep arch.
- **Review note:** The slash name combines related floor variations. Confirm whether V21.2 should keep one guide with selectable arm/leg options or separate reviewed variations.

#### Wall Sit
- **Setup:** Stand with your back against a clear wall and feet placed far enough forward to slide down without the knees being forced into an uncomfortable position.
- **Movement:** Slide down a short distance, hold while breathing normally, then push through the feet to stand.
- **Cues:** Keep feet planted and knees tracking in line with toes; use a higher position or shorter hold if needed.
- **Review note:** Avoid prescribing a fixed knee angle or hold time. A supported, shallow position is a regression.

### Free weights

#### Dumbbell Goblet Squat
- **Setup:** Hold one dumbbell close to your chest with both hands. Stand with feet in a comfortable stance.
- **Movement:** Sit hips down and back while bending the knees, then stand by pressing through the whole foot. Use only a depth you can control.
- **Cues:** Keep the dumbbell close; keep knees tracking in line with toes; avoid heels lifting or the torso collapsing.
- **Review note:** A bodyweight squat or sit-to-stand may be a simpler alternative; do not assume every user should squat deeply.

#### Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift
- **Setup:** Stand holding dumbbells in front of your thighs, knees softly bent and feet stable.
- **Movement:** Push hips backward while the dumbbells travel close to the legs. Stop when you feel a controlled hamstring stretch and can keep your back neutral; stand by bringing hips forward.
- **Cues:** This is a hip hinge, not a deep squat; keep weights close and avoid rounding the back or reaching lower than your mobility allows.
- **Review note:** Use light weights or practise the hip hinge without weights first.

#### Dumbbell Bench Press
- **Setup:** Sit on a stable flat bench with dumbbells on your thighs. Lie back with feet supported and dumbbells beside the chest; use a spotter or safe setup for challenging loads.
- **Movement:** Press dumbbells upward over the chest without clashing them or locking the elbows hard, then lower under control.
- **Cues:** Keep wrists stacked over elbows; shoulder blades supported; use a manageable range and load.
- **Review note:** Include safe dumbbell pick-up/set-down guidance in the full guide or linked safety help.

#### One-arm Dumbbell Row
- **Setup:** Support one hand on a stable bench and stagger your stance. Hold the dumbbell in the other hand with your back steady.
- **Movement:** Pull the dumbbell toward your hip, keeping the elbow close, then lower slowly until the arm is extended comfortably.
- **Cues:** Avoid rotating the torso or shrugging; keep the support stable and the neck relaxed.
- **Review note:** A chest-supported row can be considered as an alternative only if the equipment and catalogue identity are reviewed.

#### Dumbbell Shoulder Press
- **Setup:** Sit or stand with dumbbells near shoulder height, wrists stacked over elbows and trunk gently braced.
- **Movement:** Press upward within a comfortable range, then lower slowly to the starting position.
- **Cues:** Avoid leaning back, shrugging excessively or forcing the arms into a painful overhead position.
- **Review note:** The source library does not specify seated versus standing; treat posture as a configuration/variation decision if the programme needs to distinguish it.

### Resistance bands

#### Resistance Band Row
- **Setup:** Anchor the band securely at about torso height, check it for damage, and hold the ends with tension already present. Face the anchor with a stable stance or seated position.
- **Movement:** Pull hands toward the lower ribs, then return slowly without letting the band snap back.
- **Cues:** Keep shoulders down and torso steady; keep the anchor in line with the pull.
- **Review note:** The guide must explain safe anchor inspection and avoid implying that an unsecured door or movable object is safe.

#### Resistance Band Chest Press
- **Setup:** Secure the band behind you at chest height using an appropriate rated anchor, or use a purpose-made band setup. Check the band and anchor before loading.
- **Movement:** Start with hands near chest level and press forward smoothly. Return slowly while maintaining control of band tension.
- **Cues:** Keep wrists straight and shoulders comfortable; do not allow the band to recoil.
- **Review note:** Do not show a generic door-anchor setup unless the anchor instructions and door direction are explicitly verified.

#### Band Pull-apart
- **Setup:** Hold a light resistance band in front of the chest with arms extended comfortably and hands about shoulder-width apart.
- **Movement:** Pull hands apart by moving the arms outward until the band approaches the chest, then return slowly.
- **Cues:** Keep shoulders relaxed and ribs stacked over hips; do not arch the back or force the arms behind the body.
- **Review note:** Start with a light band and a small range; inspect the band for cracks or wear.

### Cardio equipment

#### Rowing Machine
- **Setup:** Adjust foot straps so they secure the feet without pressure, set light resistance, and learn the monitor/start controls before beginning.
- **Movement:** Drive with the legs first, then swing the torso slightly back and draw the handle toward the lower ribs. Recover in reverse order: arms away, torso forward, knees bend.
- **Cues:** Legs–body–arms on the drive; arms–body–legs on recovery. Keep the handle path level and avoid rounding or overreaching.
- **Review note:** This sequence is important; do not describe rowing as simply pulling with the arms. Ask gym staff for setup help if unfamiliar.

### Mobility

#### Hip / Ankle Mobility
- **Setup:** This is a category-style source label rather than one precisely defined drill. Use a stable support and select only a comfortable, controlled movement.
- **Movement:** For a hip mobility option, perform gentle standing hip circles or supported hip flexion/extension. For an ankle option, perform small ankle circles or controlled forward knee movement while the heel remains comfortably supported.
- **Cues:** Move slowly without forcing end range; keep the movement pain-free and use support for balance.
- **Review note:** **HOLD for content design.** The source label combines two body regions and does not identify a single exercise. Consider splitting into distinct Hip Mobility and Ankle Mobility entries only after catalogue identity review; do not publish the combined wording as if it were one standard drill.

#### Standing Hamstring Stretch
- **Setup:** Stand near a stable support. Place one heel slightly forward with the knee softly bent and toes pointing upward if comfortable.
- **Movement:** Hinge gently at the hips while keeping the back long until a mild stretch is felt along the back of the thigh. Return upright slowly and change sides.
- **Cues:** Keep the stretch gentle; do not bounce, round deeply or pull the toes forcefully toward you.
- **Review note:** A seated or lying alternative may be appropriate for some users but should be authored and reviewed as a separate variation.

### Machines and cables

#### Machine Chest Press
- **Setup:** Adjust the seat so the handles begin around mid-chest and the back remains supported. Confirm the machine's movement path and starting position before selecting a load.
- **Movement:** Press the handles forward smoothly, then return slowly without letting the weight stack or plates slam.
- **Cues:** Keep wrists aligned and shoulders comfortable; do not lock elbows forcefully. Follow the machine's own adjustment labels.
- **Review note:** Pin-selected and plate-loaded versions both exist. Converging handles and independent-arm operation are separate equipment/mechanics attributes; do not assume all machines share the same setup.

#### Machine Shoulder Press
- **Setup:** Adjust the seat so the handles start around shoulder level and the back is supported. Check that the handles and range suit your shoulder mobility before loading.
- **Movement:** Press upward in a controlled path, then lower slowly to the start without forcing a deep bottom position.
- **Cues:** Keep ribs down and wrists aligned; avoid leaning back or pushing through shoulder pain.
- **Review note:** Pin-selected and plate-loaded versions both exist. Handle path, independent arms and seat geometry must be recorded separately where known.

#### Leg Press Machine
- **Setup:** Identify whether the machine is a 45-degree sled, horizontal seated press or vertical press. Set the seat/back support and place feet about shoulder-width on the platform; use the machine's safety stops.
- **Movement:** Release the safety only as instructed, lower the platform within a comfortable range while keeping hips supported, then press smoothly without forcefully locking the knees. Re-engage the safety before leaving.
- **Cues:** Keep knees tracking with toes and heels supported; do not let the pelvis roll off the pad or descend farther than control allows.
- **Review note:** Orientation is a meaningful configuration difference; loading style (weight stack or plates) is independent of orientation. Do not give universal instructions for every design—machine-specific safety labels take priority.

#### Leg Extension Machine
- **Setup:** Adjust the seat and shin pad so the knee joint lines up with the machine pivot and the pad rests comfortably above the ankle. Check the machine's range limiter if fitted.
- **Movement:** Extend the knees smoothly, pause briefly if comfortable, then lower under control. Avoid dropping the weight stack.
- **Cues:** Keep hips and back supported; use a comfortable range and manageable load.
- **Review note:** The base programme says **Seated Leg Extension**. Confirm the optional-library row means the same seated knee-extension machine before treating the names as aliases.

#### Leg Curl Machine
- **Setup:** First identify the machine position: seated, prone/lying or another design. Adjust the pivot and pads according to the machine labels; secure the body against the support.
- **Movement:** Bend the knees to curl the pad through a controlled range, then return slowly without letting the stack or plates drop.
- **Cues:** Keep hips supported and avoid swinging. Do not force the end range.
- **Review note:** This is intentionally generic across machine positions. Seated and prone/lying versions need distinct variation-specific setup and instructions before being offered as interchangeable.

#### Cable Biceps Curl
- **Setup:** Attach a straight or curl handle to a low cable pulley. Stand facing the machine with a stable stance, elbows near your sides and wrists straight.
- **Movement:** Curl the handle toward the shoulders without moving the upper arms, then lower slowly until the elbows are comfortably extended.
- **Cues:** Avoid leaning back, swinging or letting the cable pull the shoulders forward; keep tension controlled.
- **Review note:** Handle type and stance can be configuration details; do not alias this to Dumbbell Biceps Curl solely because both train elbow flexion.

#### Abdominal Crunch Machine
- **Setup:** Adjust the seat and pads so the pivot/handles fit the machine's instructions and the torso can curl comfortably. Select a light starting load.
- **Movement:** Curl the ribs toward the pelvis using the abdominal muscles, then return slowly without allowing the weight to pull you abruptly backward.
- **Cues:** Keep the movement controlled; avoid yanking with the arms or pulling the neck. Use the machine's stated range.
- **Review note:** Keep this machine-specific guide separate from the base-programme label Abdominal Crunch until that programme slot's equipment/execution is confirmed.
