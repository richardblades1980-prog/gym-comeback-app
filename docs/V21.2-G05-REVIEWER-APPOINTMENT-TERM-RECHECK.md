# V21.2 — G-05 reviewer appointment, term and recheck framework

**Status:** FRAMEWORK APPROVED — owner PASS recorded for G-05 in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. This approval covers the governance framework only; operational details and prerequisites remain unresolved as stated below.

**Governance basis:** G-01 project-owner accountability, G-02 Australia-first jurisdiction, G-03 scope-based qualification framework approved in principle, and G-04 independent identity/credential verification process approved in principle.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve the principles for finite, scope-specific reviewer appointments; scheduled rechecks; event-triggered reassessment; and explicit expiry, suspension and renewal states.

The exact appointment duration and recheck interval for each credential pathway must be set only after the relevant Australian authority requirements, professional standards and risk of the review scope are assessed. No generic annual, two-year or other fixed period is proposed here.

## 2. Appointment is specific and time-bounded

Every appointment must state:

- the individual reviewer reference;
- the exact review scope and permitted tasks;
- explicit exclusions and escalation boundaries;
- jurisdiction(s) in which the authority applies;
- qualification and identity verification references;
- appointment decision-maker and approval record;
- effective date and defined end/review date;
- next required recheck date;
- conflict/independence conditions;
- current status and any restrictions.

An appointment for one scope does not imply appointment for another. A reviewer must not continue reviewing merely because they were previously trusted or because the catalogue needs work.

No appointment may be indefinite or rely on an unchanging “trusted” flag. The appointment must have an explicit end or review point. If the underlying qualification or authorisation has an earlier expiry, the reviewer authority cannot extend beyond it.

## 3. Setting the term and recheck interval

For each approved qualification pathway and review scope, determine the maximum appointment term and recheck cadence from:

1. statutory or registration-authority renewal and status requirements;
2. issuer requirements for a certification or membership, where that pathway is approved;
3. professional standards or continuing competence requirements that apply to the task;
4. the consequence of an incorrect review and the likelihood that relevant competence or authority can change;
5. practical ability to verify current standing and receive authoritative status updates.

Use the shortest applicable mandatory validity limit. Where no authoritative interval exists, the governance authority must document a reasoned, risk-based period with appropriately qualified input and a review date. It must not imply that the chosen interval is an official professional standard.

Different scopes and credential pathways may have different terms. The high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance scope should receive more stringent recheck and event-response controls, but the exact interval must be justified rather than guessed.

## 4. Event-triggered reassessment

A scheduled date is not enough. Require immediate reporting and reassessment when a material event occurs, including:

- registration, licence, certification or membership expiry;
- suspension, cancellation, restriction, condition or disciplinary status change;
- change to the reviewer's permitted scope of practice;
- change of jurisdiction or proposed expansion of review authority;
- change of identity details that affects credential matching;
- relevant complaint, concern or credible allegation;
- evidence of misrepresentation, credential fraud or impersonation;
- material change to G-03 qualification criteria;
- material change to the tasks or claims the reviewer is authorised to assess;
- loss or suspected compromise of a signing key, once signing infrastructure is separately approved.

Reviewers must be required by the eventual appointment terms to notify governance promptly of changes affecting eligibility. Failure to disclose a material change is itself a governance concern.

## 5. Interim status while a concern is checked

Use explicit lifecycle states. Do not leave a reviewer appearing active while a material eligibility concern is unresolved.

| State | Meaning | New reviews |
|---|---|---|
| Pending verification | Identity/qualification or scope assessment incomplete | Not permitted |
| Active | All required checks passed; appointment is within its term and scope | Permitted only within scope |
| Restricted | A documented limitation narrows permitted tasks | Only explicitly permitted tasks |
| Review due | Scheduled recheck point reached or due | Pause new reviews until recheck passes, unless a separately justified policy permits a narrow administrative grace period |
| Suspended | Temporary hold while a material concern, complaint or status issue is investigated | Not permitted in affected scope |
| Expired | Appointment term ended without renewal | Not permitted |
| Revoked | Governance authority removed appointment | Not permitted |
| Withdrawn | Reviewer voluntarily relinquished appointment | Not permitted |
| Superseded | Replaced by a newer appointment record | Only the current appointment may be relied upon |

A suspension or expiry does not silently erase prior reviews. Assess previously approved content under the separate withdrawal, release-impact and appeal policy. Preserve the historical record.

## 6. Renewal and re-authorisation

Renewal is a new governance decision, not automatic rollover.

Before renewal:
- repeat identity checks where required by the approved identity policy or where discrepancies/events warrant it;
- verify current credential/registration status from the authoritative source;
- assess expiry, restrictions, conditions and jurisdiction;
- confirm the G-03 scope fit remains valid;
- check relevant complaints, conflicts and prior review quality/concerns;
- confirm any required continuing competence evidence;
- issue a new finite appointment record with updated scope, exclusions and review dates.

If any mandatory check is inconclusive, the affected authority remains paused or expires. Do not use an applicant's prior service or reputation as a substitute for verification.

## 7. Historical approvals and impact assessment

When a reviewer expires, is suspended, is restricted or is revoked:

- stop new reviews in the affected scope as required by the state;
- identify affected approvals and catalogue releases;
- assess whether the reason affects prior review validity (for example, credential fraud or key compromise differs from routine expiry);
- preserve prior attestations and decision history;
- withdraw, re-review or retain prior approvals only under a documented policy decision;
- publish updated status/revocation information through the future approved trust process;
- ensure offline devices are not told that a stale snapshot proves current authority.

Routine expiry does not automatically prove that all historical work was wrong. Conversely, a later discovery of fraud or a material qualification defect may require review of earlier decisions. Do not make either assumption without assessment.

## 8. Governance and separation of duties

- The reviewer does not renew or extend their own appointment.
- A verifier checks current source evidence; an authorised governance decision-maker separately approves renewal.
- A reviewer must not decide their own complaint, suspension or appeal.
- The project owner remains accountable for policy and governance outcomes but cannot waive required professional qualification or source verification.
- Any exception or grace period must be narrowly defined, documented, time-limited, independently authorised and prohibited from bypassing mandatory registration/credential expiry or high-consequence review requirements.

## 9. Audit record

The restricted governance record should append:
- original appointment and all superseding appointment references;
- scope and exclusions at each point in time;
- verification source, date, result and verifier;
- review due date, actual recheck date and outcome;
- event-triggered notifications and investigation outcomes;
- suspension, restriction, expiry, revocation or withdrawal reason category;
- decision-maker and rationale;
- affected review/approval/release impact assessment;
- appeal and reinstatement outcome.

Do not overwrite historical states. The public catalogue should disclose only the minimum pseudonymous status and freshness information approved under G-12.

## 10. Stop conditions and dependencies

Before operational appointments, the governance package must also settle:
- final G-03 qualification pathways and authoritative status sources;
- G-04 identity/credential verification implementation;
- G-06 independence and conflicts;
- G-08 complaint, suspension, withdrawal and appeals;
- G-09 privacy, access, retention and lawful handling;
- G-10 current/offline freshness semantics.

This proposal does not authorise credential collection, live register creation, reviewer onboarding, key generation, signing services, catalogue approvals, code/schema/database/backup/runtime/UI changes, C3, injury-aware matching, Pages changes or deployment.
