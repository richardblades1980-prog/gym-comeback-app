## 1. Purpose and governing principle

This policy establishes when Gym Comeback may rely on a person's review, and when a particular review may be relied upon for a specific catalogue decision.

**Trust is scoped, evidence-based, time-bounded and revocable.** It is not a general badge attached to a person. A person's identity, qualifications and authorisation may be verified, but that alone does not approve any catalogue content. Each approval must separately demonstrate that the reviewer assessed the exact material, within the correct scope, using adequate evidence and the applicable review standard.

Technical controls (including signatures, digests, registry records and offline verification) are evidence and enforcement mechanisms for this policy. They do not decide who is qualified, replace professional judgement, or prove that a conclusion is correct.

The policy is deliberately conservative: where authority, evidence, scope, independence or current status cannot be established, the affected content is not treated as approved for the relevant use.

## 2. What the policy governs

The policy applies to people and organisations involved in:
- reviewer identity and credential verification;
- reviewer appointment, scope authorisation and re-authorisation;
- exercise identity and execution review;
- equipment identity and configuration review;
- movement-demand and injury-relevance review;
- programme-slot identity and mapping review;
- guide and instruction review;
- review approval, rejection, revision, withdrawal and appeal;
- catalogue release approval and post-release correction;
- reviewer records, audit evidence, privacy and retention.

It applies to each review event and each immutable target version. It does not convert legacy labels, historic programme slots, user-created equipment, or unreviewed catalogue rows into approved definitions.

## 3. Trust is separated into four decisions

These decisions must not be collapsed into a single “trusted reviewer” flag.

1. **Identity confidence:** has the person's identity been checked using an appropriate independent process?
2. **Qualification confidence:** has the relevant credential, registration or experience been verified with an authoritative source, and does it support the proposed review scope?
3. **Authorisation:** has the governance authority explicitly granted this person permission to review this defined scope, with stated limits and a review period?
4. **Review validity:** did this authorised reviewer actually assess this exact target and evidence set, follow the required protocol, disclose limitations and record a defensible decision?

A positive result at one level does not imply a positive result at the next. A signed review record proves only that a particular key signed a particular record, unless the identity, authorisation and key binding are separately established under the policy.

## 4. Review scopes and boundaries

Authorisation must be narrow and recorded separately for each scope:

| Scope | Permitted subject matter | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise identity and execution | Canonical identity, movement execution, meaningful variations, setup and exercise-specific instructions | Does not automatically authorise injury-relevance or professional medical conclusions |
| Equipment identity and configuration | Equipment family, loading system, mechanics, orientation, attachments and setup attributes | Does not automatically authorise exercise technique or injury clearance |
| Movement-demand and injury-relevance | Direct/secondary loading, joint actions, support, stabilisation, balance, foot contact/pressure, weight-bearing, impact, propulsion, positioning and bracing | Requires a separately defined professional qualification and scope-of-practice policy; never constitutes individual medical clearance |
| Programme-slot identity | Whether a preserved source slot maps to a reviewed definition or variation without changing historical meaning | Similar names alone are insufficient evidence |
| Guide/instruction content | Accuracy, completeness, setup, execution and cautions within the reviewer's competence | Does not authorise claims outside the reviewer's expertise |

A reviewer may hold multiple scopes only after each has been separately assessed and authorised. Scope exclusions and limitations must be explicit. If a question crosses scopes, all required scopes must be covered; one reviewer’s adjacent expertise must not be assumed to fill a gap.

The project must approve the actual qualification and verification criteria for each scope before any real reviewer is appointed. This proposal intentionally does not invent professional titles, credential thresholds, registration bodies or jurisdiction-specific legal requirements.

## 5. Reviewer eligibility and qualification verification

### 5.1 Minimum eligibility principles

Before authorisation, the governance process must establish:
- the person's identity through an independent, proportionate verification method;
- the authenticity and current status of each relevant credential or registration through its issuing authority or another authoritative source;
- the credential's relevance to the proposed review scope and actual questions;
- jurisdiction, limitations, conditions, exclusions and any restrictions on practice;
- sufficient relevant experience for the specific review task, where the scope policy requires it;
- willingness to follow the review protocol, disclose conflicts, document limitations and participate in correction/review processes.

Self-declaration, job title, marketing biography, social profile, course attendance, an uploaded certificate without independent verification, AI-generated content, or another reviewer's recommendation is not sufficient by itself to establish professional authority.

Where no authoritative verification route exists, the qualification is not treated as verified. Governance may record the limitation and decline the appointment or restrict the scope; it must not silently lower the standard.

### 5.2 Verification record

A restricted reviewer register must record, at minimum:
- stable internal reviewer reference and verified identity record;
- identity-verification method, verifier and date;
- credential type, issuing authority and jurisdiction;
- independent verification source/reference, result, verifier and date;
- authorised scopes, exclusions and rationale;
- authorisation decision-maker and date;
- validity/recheck date and any applicable expiry;
- status history: pending, active, suspended, expired, revoked or declined;
- relevant incident, appeal or re-verification references.

Sensitive identity and credential documents must remain in a restricted governance system, not in Git, public catalogue assets, app bundles or user backups.

### 5.3 Re-verification

Re-verification is required at the policy-defined interval and whenever a relevant trigger occurs, including:
- credential expiry or renewal;
- a change in registration or professional standing;
- a change in scope of practice, jurisdiction or restrictions;
- a substantiated complaint or material concern;
- a declared conflict or loss of independence;
- suspected identity or credential misrepresentation;
- a change in reviewer scope or role.

Until a required check is complete, the affected authorisation is not treated as current.

## 6. Appointment, authorisation and accountability

Reviewer appointment must be made by an authorised governance decision-maker who is not simply accepting the applicant's own assertion. The appointment record must identify the decision-maker, evidence considered, scope granted, exclusions, start date, review/recheck date and conditions.

The reviewer must:
- accept only work within their active authorised scope;
- inspect the actual target and relevant evidence, not merely approve a summary or another person's conclusion;
- disclose uncertainty, missing evidence and limits of competence;
- use the current approved checklist/protocol;
- record a reasoned decision and any field-level limitations;
- disclose conflicts before reviewing and again if circumstances change;
- protect confidential information;
- correct or withdraw an approval if a material error or new evidence is identified;
- cooperate with proportionate audit and incident investigation.

Reviewers must not delegate or share signing credentials. A reviewer remains accountable for their own attestation. Administrative staff may support evidence handling but cannot be represented as having made the professional judgement.

## 7. Independence and conflicts of interest

Independence is assessed for each review, not assumed from the reviewer's job title or external status.

A reviewer must disclose any relationship or interest that could reasonably affect, or appear to affect, impartiality, including:
- authorship, curation or material contribution to the target;
- financial interest in a product, equipment supplier, service or commercial outcome affected by the review;
- employment, ownership, family or close personal relationship with an interested party;
- prior dispute or other relationship likely to affect impartiality;
- pressure, incentive or expectation tied to a particular approval outcome.

The governance authority records the disclosure and decides whether the reviewer may proceed, must be replaced, or may proceed only with documented safeguards. A person must not be the sole approver of their own work. A reviewer must not approve their own credential, scope or authorisation.

For high-consequence movement-demand and injury-relevance metadata, require two independent reviews by appropriately authorised reviewers. At least one must satisfy the future, explicitly approved professional qualification policy for that scope. The two reviewers must independently examine the relevant evidence; a countersignature without independent assessment does not count. The exact independence standard and any permitted exceptions require owner approval before production use.

Collusion cannot be eliminated by a policy statement; separation of duties, audit, transparency and incident response reduce but do not remove that residual risk.

## 8. Evidence and review standards

### 8.1 Evidence quality

Evidence must be traceable to a source and sufficiently specific to support the claim being reviewed. Records should include source identity, version/date, exact page/section/locator where available, and the proposition the evidence supports.

Reviewers should distinguish:
- authoritative primary sources (for example, applicable official standards, equipment manufacturer documentation, professional or regulatory sources);
- reliable secondary sources that explain or corroborate primary evidence;
- direct inspection or measurement evidence, where appropriate;
- contextual or anecdotal material, which may identify a question but is not automatically proof.

The detailed source hierarchy is to be set per review scope. A source is not authoritative merely because it is popular, easy to access, repeated elsewhere or generated by AI. Conflicting sources must be recorded and resolved or left explicitly unresolved.

### 8.2 Completeness and uncertainty

Reviewers must not convert missing information into a positive assumption. Unknown, ambiguous, conflicting or out-of-scope fields remain unknown or unresolved. The review record must identify:
- what was reviewed;
- what was not reviewed;
- which evidence supports each material conclusion;
- known limitations and unresolved questions;
- whether the target is suitable for the proposed catalogue use.

For movement-demand/injury-relevance, the evidence review must consider the complete relevant movement demands, not only a primary-muscle label. This includes secondary loading, stabilisation, balance, foot contact/pressure, weight-bearing, propulsion, impact, positioning and bracing where applicable. A missing or inadequately evidenced dimension cannot be treated as cleared.

### 8.3 Decision meanings

Only these review outcomes are permitted:
- **Approved:** the exact target meets the defined review criteria for the stated scope, with no unresolved issue that prevents the intended use.
- **Rejected:** the target fails a material criterion or evidence contradicts the proposed claim.
- **Returned for revision:** the target is not ready; identified changes or evidence are required before another review.

“Approved with caveats” must not hide a failed required criterion. Where the system supports field-level outcomes, approved fields and unresolved fields must be separately represented; unresolved required fields remain unavailable for any use that depends on them.

Approval is not a guarantee of correctness, a medical diagnosis, a safety certification for every user, or permission to make claims beyond the review scope.

## 9. Review lifecycle and status control

A review approval is bound to the exact immutable target digest, scope, evidence set and protocol version. It does not transfer to a renamed, edited, reconfigured or related record.

A new review is required when:
- target content changes materially;
- the evidence set changes in a way that affects the conclusion;
- the applicable protocol or policy changes materially;
- a reviewer loses or changes the relevant authorisation;
- a credential expires, is restricted or revoked;
- a conflict is discovered;
- credible new evidence undermines the prior conclusion;
- an incident or audit identifies a material deficiency.

Review records are append-only. Corrections, withdrawals and supersession create new linked records; they do not erase the historical decision or rewrite the old attestation.

Reviewer authorisation states:
- **Pending:** verification or appointment incomplete; no review authority.
- **Active:** verified and authorised within stated scopes and current policy conditions.
- **Suspended:** temporarily not authorised while a concern or check is investigated.
- **Expired:** required validity/recheck period has elapsed without renewal.
- **Revoked:** authority withdrawn; new reviews prohibited.
- **Declined:** appointment not granted.

A status change must record who made it, when, the reason category, affected scopes/keys and any impact assessment required. A suspension or revocation does not automatically prove misconduct; the record should distinguish precautionary action from a substantiated finding.

## 10. Withdrawal, complaints, appeals and correction

A reviewer or governance officer must be able to raise a concern about an approval. Concerns must be triaged proportionately, with urgent risk-related issues able to trigger temporary suspension of affected approval use while investigated.

The process must provide:
- a recorded concern and acknowledgement;
- impartial investigation by a person not responsible for the disputed decision;
- preservation of the original evidence and audit trail;
- a reasoned outcome and affected content/release assessment;
- an opportunity for the reviewer to respond;
- an appeal to a separate authorised decision-maker where practicable;
- documented correction, withdrawal, replacement or reinstatement.

An appeal does not itself restore authority or approval. While a material concern remains unresolved, affected content must not be represented as currently approved for a use that depends on the disputed review.

If a review is withdrawn or invalidated, the catalogue governance authority assesses dependent records and releases, including whether they must be blocked, replaced or re-reviewed. Historical workout records must remain accessible; withdrawing a catalogue approval must not rewrite a user's historical activity.

## 11. Auditability and records

The audit trail must make it possible to reconstruct:
- who was authorised, by whom, for which scope and during what period;
- what identity/qualification verification was performed and from which source;
- what exact target and evidence were reviewed;
- which protocol version was applied;
- the reviewer's decision, limitations and conflicts;
- who independently checked the review and who authorised release;
- subsequent expiry, suspension, revocation, withdrawal, correction and appeal events;
- which release and registry state relied on the review.

Records must be append-only or otherwise tamper-evident, access-controlled and backed up under a defined retention policy. Auditability does not justify publishing private identity or credential data. Public catalogue traceability should use pseudonymous reviewer references and only the minimum information necessary to explain scope, decision and status.

## 12. Offline and current-status policy

The technical trust model explains how signed attestations and registry snapshots may be checked offline. This policy sets the governance meaning of those states:

- A valid signature establishes that the corresponding key signed the bound record; it does not by itself establish current professional standing.
- A reviewer is “currently authorised” only when the governance state is sufficiently fresh under the approved scope-specific freshness policy.
- An offline device with an older signed registry may report historical verification as of that snapshot, but must not claim that no later suspension, expiry or revocation exists.
- Unknown or stale reviewer status must not be silently treated as active.
- Injury-aware alternative screening must fail closed when the required review or reviewer-status freshness cannot be established.
- Historical workout data remains accessible even when catalogue trust cannot be refreshed.

The maximum freshness periods, trusted timestamp approach, first-use bootstrap and offline exceptions remain decisions for the detailed technical/governance gate. They must not be invented in implementation.

## 13. Privacy, access and retention

Before collecting real reviewer information, the project must approve:
- lawful purpose, notice and consent/other applicable basis;
- minimum necessary identity and credential data;
- restricted access roles and access logging;
- encryption and secure storage;
- retention and deletion periods, including treatment of historical approvals;
- handling of access/correction requests and incidents;
- cross-border and jurisdiction considerations where applicable;
- operational owner and funding for ongoing verification.

Credential documents and sensitive identity records must not be stored in public repositories, application assets, user backups or the public catalogue. Do not collect real reviewer data until the governance system and privacy requirements have been approved and are operational.

## 14. Policy ownership and review

A named governance owner must be responsible for maintaining this policy, approving scope-specific qualification criteria, authorising registrars, overseeing audits and ensuring that policy changes are communicated to affected reviewers and release authorities.

The policy must be reviewed on a defined schedule and after material incidents, legal/regulatory changes, changes to catalogue use, or changes to the trust architecture. The review schedule and responsible roles require owner approval.

Policy amendments must be versioned. Each review attestation and release must identify the policy/checklist version it relied upon. A policy change that materially changes approval criteria may require re-review of affected catalogue content.

## 15. Decisions still required before production reviewer onboarding

The following cannot be assumed or invented and require explicit owner/governance decisions:

1. Named policy owner and accountable governance authority.
2. Jurisdiction(s) in which reviewers may be appointed and the applicable verification sources.
3. Scope-specific qualification criteria, including the professional standard for movement-demand/injury-relevance review.
4. Identity verification method and acceptable evidence.
5. Reviewer appointment, renewal and recheck periods.
6. Independence standard, conflict thresholds and high-consequence dual-review rules.
7. Evidence hierarchy and scope-specific review checklists.
8. Complaint, suspension, revocation, appeal and incident decision rights.
9. Privacy, access, retention and lawful handling of credential records.
10. Audit ownership, funding, service continuity and succession.
11. Offline freshness policy and the meaning of “current” for each review scope.
12. Public traceability fields and restricted-register data model.

Until these decisions are made, no real person may be described as an appointed qualified reviewer for Gym Comeback production catalogue purposes.

## 16. Explicit exclusions and owner decision requested

This policy proposal does not authorise:
- appointment or onboarding of real reviewers;
- credential collection or creation of a reviewer register;
- production content, IDs, review approvals or catalogue publication;
- cryptographic algorithm selection, key generation, trust-root creation or technical implementation;
- changes to catalogue schemas, manifests, database, backup, service worker or runtime;
- C3 restriction storage/logic, Adaptive Brain or injury-aware runtime implementation;
- Equipment Manager, Exercise Library or Programme Builder UI;
- changes to `main`, the Pages release candidate, Pages settings, deployment or production cutover.

It would not appoint reviewers, approve credentials, authorise production review, create a register or keys, approve catalogue content, or authorise implementation/publication.
