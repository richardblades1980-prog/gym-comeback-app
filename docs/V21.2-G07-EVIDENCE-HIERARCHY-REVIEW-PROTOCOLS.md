# V21.2 — G-07 evidence hierarchy and review protocols

**Status:** FRAMEWORK APPROVED — owner PASS recorded for G-07 in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. This approval covers the governance framework only; operational details and prerequisites remain unresolved as stated below.

**Governance basis:** G-01 project-owner accountability; G-02 Australia-first jurisdiction; G-03 scope-specific qualification framework; G-04 independent identity/credential verification; G-05 finite appointments and rechecks; and G-06 independence/conflict principles, all approved in principle as recorded in the governance decision register.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve a common evidence-governance framework with separate, scope-specific evidence hierarchies and review protocols. Each approval must be limited to explicit claims about an exact, versioned target. Evidence must be traceable, relevant to the claim, assessed for limitations and conflicts, and sufficient for the reviewer's authorised scope.

A source is not automatically authoritative for every claim it discusses. Evidence strength is claim-specific: a manufacturer may be authoritative for its own model's dimensions or setup instructions, but not automatically for comparative effectiveness, injury risk or suitability for an individual.

## 2. Common evidence rules

Every review should:

1. **Atomise the claims.** List the exact fields or statements being assessed; do not approve an entire exercise or equipment record merely because its name appears familiar.
2. **Bind the target.** Record immutable target identifier, content digest, version/release context and the exact fields reviewed.
3. **Trace each source.** Record source title/publisher, author or responsible organisation where available, publication/update date, stable URL or document identifier, version/edition, access date and precise page/section/table/figure locator.
4. **Explain relevance.** State which claim each source supports, what it does not establish, and why it is applicable to the exact variant, equipment configuration, population or context.
5. **Assess quality and limitations.** Consider authority, method, transparency, reproducibility, recency, applicability, bias, funding and conflicts. Do not equate a source's prestige with proof of every claim.
6. **Corroborate proportionately.** Use independent corroboration for material or high-consequence claims. Repeated copies of one source are not independent evidence.
7. **Record disagreement.** Preserve conflicting sources and explain resolution; do not silently select the convenient source.
8. **Preserve uncertainty.** Unknown, ambiguous, unsupported or out-of-scope fields remain explicitly unknown. Reviewers must not fill gaps from intuition or plausible-sounding inference.
9. **Reassess material changes.** Changes to target content, variant, equipment, evidence, protocol or relevant standards trigger reassessment where they could change the conclusion.
10. **Record a bounded decision.** Approve, approve with explicit limitations, reject, or return for clarification. State the precise claims and intended use covered.

A citation alone is not evidence of correctness. A reviewer must inspect and evaluate the underlying source, not merely repeat another catalogue, search snippet, AI output or secondary summary.

## 3. Proposed evidence hierarchy by scope

The following is a framework for weighing evidence, not a rigid universal ranking. The most appropriate source depends on the claim and the review scope.

### Scope 1 — Exercise identity and execution

Relevant evidence may include:
- Original or authoritative exercise descriptions and technical materials from the recognised originator or governing body, where such an authority exists.
- Well-documented professional or educational references that define the movement and distinguish variants.
- Peer-reviewed research or established technical texts where they directly describe the movement being claimed.
- Clear, attributable demonstration material used to inspect execution details, with the exact variation and context recorded.
- Secondary descriptions only as leads for locating stronger evidence, not as sole support for disputed identity or consequential execution claims.

Review questions:
- Is this one identifiable exercise or a family of materially different variants?
- Are start/end positions, support, equipment, grip, stance, range and movement direction sufficiently specified?
- Does the evidence support the exact label and instructions, or only a similar movement?
- Could two reasonable users interpret the record as different exercises?

Do not merge near-matches by name alone. Preserve meaningful variants and unresolved identity ambiguity.

### Scope 2 — Equipment identity and configuration

Relevant evidence may include:
- Manufacturer documentation for the exact make/model and revision, including manuals, parts diagrams, labels and configuration instructions.
- Applicable technical standards or certification documentation where the specific claim falls within their scope.
- Direct inspection with dated photographs, measurements and identifiable model/configuration details.
- Reliable technical documentation from distributors or service providers when manufacturer evidence is unavailable, clearly labelled by provenance and limitations.
- User-submitted descriptions or photographs as evidence of a user's own observed equipment, not proof of a universal catalogue definition.

Review questions:
- Is the item uniquely identified, or is the label generic/ambiguous?
- Which loading system, movement mechanics, orientation, attachments and setup options are confirmed?
- Does the evidence distinguish visually similar but mechanically different machines?
- Is a configuration universal to the model or specific to one installation?

Marketing images and product claims must not establish unverified biomechanics, safety or therapeutic benefits. Unknown configuration stays unknown.

### Scope 3 — Movement demand and injury relevance

This is a high-consequence scope and must follow the separately approved professional qualification and two-independent-review requirements. The evidence framework cannot substitute for that professional judgement.

Relevant evidence may include:
- Applicable professional standards, clinical or rehabilitation guidance and consensus statements from recognised bodies.
- Peer-reviewed biomechanical, clinical or exercise-science research directly relevant to the movement, loading condition and claim.
- High-quality systematic reviews where their population, methods and movement context are applicable.
- Validated measurement or laboratory evidence where available and appropriately interpreted.
- Detailed movement analysis and professional reasoning that explicitly distinguishes observed mechanics from inferred tissue loading.

Review questions must consider the complete relevant demand profile, not only a primary-muscle label:
- Joint actions, positions and ranges.
- Direct and secondary muscle/tissue loading, including stabilisation.
- Weight-bearing, foot pressure, balance and support demands.
- Propulsion, braking, landing, impact and deceleration.
- Isometric bracing, grip, trunk and postural demands.
- Equipment setup, load path, tempo and common execution variants.
- Whether evidence concerns the movement itself, a specific population, a clinical context or an individual outcome.

Do not convert population-level research or movement classification into individual medical clearance. Do not infer that a movement is suitable for a person with a reported restriction merely because the restricted area is not listed as a primary muscle. Where evidence is insufficient or conflicting, classify the relevant demand as unknown and fail closed for injury-aware candidate matching.

### Scope 4 — Programme-slot identity and mapping

Relevant evidence may include:
- Original programme source files, authored programme documents and version history.
- Original slot identifiers, labels, ordering, grouping, progression context and programme notes.
- Traceable author clarification or other primary records, where available.
- Secondary notes only where their relationship to the original programme is documented.

Review questions:
- Does the proposed mapping preserve the exact historical slot and its intended role?
- Is the match supported by primary source evidence, or only by similar wording?
- Does the mapping change exercise identity, equipment assumptions, order, progression or training intent?
- Can the mapping be stated as confirmed, partial, ambiguous or unresolved?

Never rewrite historical slot identity or silently substitute a current library exercise because its name looks similar. Preserve original identifiers and labels; unresolved slots remain unassigned.

### Scope 5 — Guide and instruction content

Relevant evidence may include:
- Current authoritative standards or professional guidance relevant to the exact instruction.
- Primary equipment manuals for equipment-specific setup and operation.
- Peer-reviewed or established educational sources for technical explanations where directly applicable.
- Approved source material from the other scopes, with links to the exact reviewed version.
- Attributable instructional demonstrations used as supplementary evidence, not as a substitute for review of written claims.

Review questions:
- Is each instruction accurate, understandable and appropriate to the stated audience and scope?
- Are warnings, limitations, setup assumptions and variant distinctions explicit?
- Does the guide make unsupported medical, rehabilitation, injury-prevention or guaranteed-outcome claims?
- Can the reader identify when to stop and seek appropriate qualified guidance?

A guide cannot broaden the scope of the underlying approved exercise or equipment record. Medical or rehabilitation claims require the relevant professional review; general exercise instruction must not be presented as diagnosis or treatment.

## 4. Evidence age, version and applicability

No universal evidence-expiry interval is proposed. Reviewers should consider:
- Whether the source has been superseded, corrected, withdrawn or materially updated.
- Whether equipment models, manufacturer instructions, standards or professional guidance have changed.
- Whether new research materially changes a claim or reveals a prior limitation.
- Whether the source still applies to the exact exercise variant, configuration and intended use.
- Whether the source's date affects the reliability of the claim.

A source does not become invalid solely because it is old; nor does a recent publication become strong evidence solely because it is new. Record the reason for relying on older evidence or excluding newer evidence where that choice could affect the decision.

## 5. Conflicting or insufficient evidence

When sources disagree:
1. Identify the exact point of disagreement and whether sources address the same claim and context.
2. Compare authority, method, population, version, applicability, independence and conflicts.
3. Seek independent corroboration or appropriately scoped professional input where needed.
4. Record the rationale, remaining uncertainty and any restricted claim wording.
5. Reject or leave the field unknown if the conflict cannot be resolved to the required standard.

Do not average incompatible claims or select a preferred answer without explanation. A reviewer must not resolve a question outside their authorised scope.

## 6. Minimum review record and checklist structure

Each review record should capture, at minimum:
- Exact target identifier, digest, version and reviewed fields.
- Review scope and checklist/protocol version.
- Reviewer authorisation reference and confirmation that it was active for that scope at review time.
- Assignment-specific conflict disclosure and independence assessment reference.
- Evidence references with source/version/date/locator and claim linkage.
- Relevant limitations, uncertainty and conflicting evidence.
- Decision for each claim or field, including any conditions or exclusions.
- Reviewer rationale, date/time and attestation binding the decision to the exact target.
- Required second-review reference for high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance content.
- Reassessment triggers, supersession or correction links where applicable.

The detailed checklists must be developed and reviewed by people qualified for their respective scopes. This proposal does not invent professional thresholds, clinical criteria or technical standards.

## 7. Independence and high-consequence review

G-06 remains applicable to all evidence assessment. Reviewers must disclose relevant interests in evidence sources, manufacturers, products, programmes, employers and prior authorship.

For high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance content:
- Two independent appropriately scoped reviews are required.
- At least one reviewer must meet the final approved professional qualification standard for the relevant decision.
- Each reviewer must independently inspect the evidence and record their own reasoning before seeing or relying on the other's conclusion.
- A second signature, copied rationale or countersignature is not an independent review.
- Disagreement or unresolved uncertainty prevents a positive approval until resolved under the approved process.

## 8. Corrections, monitoring and maintenance

Maintain an append-only history of source corrections, withdrawn papers, changed manufacturer documentation, standards updates, reviewer corrections, complaints and reassessment decisions.

A credible new concern about a high-consequence claim should trigger prompt triage. Where reliance could create material risk, restrict or suspend that claim pending review rather than leaving it presented as verified. Preserve historical approvals and explain whether they were superseded, restricted or withdrawn; do not silently overwrite the audit trail.

Checklist changes must be versioned. A material protocol change requires an impact assessment of existing approvals and a documented decision about which records need re-review.

## 9. Boundaries and stop conditions

This proposal does not:
- Select specific Australian professional bodies, credentials, clinical standards or experience thresholds.
- Declare any source type automatically sufficient for all claims.
- Approve any exercise, equipment definition, programme mapping, movement-demand profile or guide.
- Create reviewer identities, appointments, credentials, attestations, keys or a live register.
- Select cryptographic formats, schemas, APIs, databases or implementation architecture.
- Authorise catalogue publication, C3, Adaptive Brain, UI, backup/runtime changes, main, Pages settings or deployment.

Those decisions require their own evidence, professional input and explicit approval gates. G-09 privacy safeguards remain a prerequisite before collecting real reviewer or conflict records.
