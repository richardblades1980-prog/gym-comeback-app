# V21.2 — Reviewer Trust Technical Gate Design

**Status:** DESIGN DIRECTION APPROVED — owner PASS recorded. Approval is limited to this logical trust-boundary design and its direction for the next design/review stage; no architecture option has been selected and no implementation is authorised.  
**Authorisation basis:** Owner PASS of the consolidated governance readiness audit, commit `950b2e089e5bfdde43573e58c8ef951d985670cb`.  
**Design branch:** `V21.2-development`  
**Source baseline inspected:** `950b2e089e5bfdde43573e58c8ef951d985670cb`  
**Purpose:** define a machine-enforceable reviewer-authentication and approval trust boundary for a later, separately approved implementation gate.

This document is an approved design direction only. It does not authorise application code changes, schema or backup changes, key generation/custody, credential or personal-data collection, reviewer appointments, production content approvals, public publication, injury-aware matching changes, deployment or changes to `main`/Pages.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Adopt a **separate, signed trust chain** for reviewer authorisation, review attestations, governance status and catalogue release authority. Keep content-integrity digests and reviewer-authentication signatures as distinct controls.

The current platform already has useful foundations:
- immutable catalogue release delivery and exact target-digest checks in `src/data/catalogue-delivery.js`;
- canonical SHA-256 digest helpers and domain separation in `src/modules/exercise-guide-crypto.js`;
- exact guide, asset-set, assignment and review metadata validation in `src/modules/exercise-guide-registry.js`;
- programme-snapshot and local-asset validation in `src/modules/exercise-guide-runtime.js`;
- offline catalogue and guide asset delivery boundaries.

These controls establish content integrity, provenance and binding. They do **not** prove the identity, professional qualification, active scope, independence or current authority of the person named in review metadata. A digest of a review record is not a reviewer signature. A signature from an untrusted key is not proof of qualification. A valid signature against a stale registry is not proof of current authority.

**Recommended trust flow:**

1. An authorised governance process verifies identity and qualification using approved Australian sources and records the result in a restricted governance system.
2. A separately authorised registrar grants a finite, narrow reviewer scope and binds that authorisation to a reviewer public key.
3. The reviewer signs a decision over an exact, immutable target digest, review scope, evidence-set reference and decision.
4. Independent reviewers separately sign their own decisions where G-06/G-07 require dual review.
5. A governance authority publishes signed reviewer-status and revocation snapshots.
6. A distinct catalogue release authority checks the full evidence and trust chain, then signs the immutable catalogue release.
7. The app verifies the chain against a trust root it already trusts, derives a freshness state, and exposes only the minimum safe public projection.
8. Offline use may report historical verification against a known signed snapshot; it must not claim current status when freshness or trusted time is unknown.

This is a logical architecture, not an approved production schema or selected cryptographic/provider design.

## 2. Trust objectives and invariants

The design must enforce these invariants regardless of eventual provider or architecture:

1. **No self-asserted authority:** reviewer IDs, role strings, qualification claims and public keys in catalogue data are not trusted unless linked to a valid signed authorisation chain.
2. **Narrow scope:** authority is explicit per review scope, jurisdiction, claim class and any exclusions. No adjacent-scope inference.
3. **Exact target binding:** every attestation binds the canonical target identifier, immutable version and digest of the exact reviewed content and evidence references.
4. **Independent review:** required reviewers each assess the evidence and sign separately; a copied or countersigned result is not an independent review.
5. **Separation of duties:** credential verifier, registrar, reviewer, independent reviewer, release authority, trust-root custodian and auditor have distinct duties. No person can appoint themselves, approve their own credential, review their own work and release it unilaterally.
6. **Finite authority:** authorisation has an approved validity/recheck lifecycle and is affected by expiry, restriction, suspension, withdrawal, credential change and scope change.
7. **Revocation-aware:** a signature proves what a key signed; a current status decision also requires a sufficiently fresh authoritative status snapshot.
8. **Fail closed:** absent, malformed, untrusted, stale, out-of-scope, conflicting or unverifiable required data cannot become an approved/current result.
9. **Privacy by separation:** identity documents, credential evidence, conflict declarations, complaints and case files stay in restricted governance systems; public catalogue data contains only the approved minimal projection.
10. **Historical continuity:** trust withdrawal must not delete or rewrite a user's historical workout record, programme snapshot or the fact that a prior catalogue release existed.
11. **No medical clearance:** reviewer approval of a bounded catalogue claim is not individual diagnosis, treatment advice, rehabilitation prescription or clearance for a user to exercise.
12. **No AI qualification authority:** AI may assist drafting, consistency checks or evidence organisation only under an approved process; it cannot establish a person's identity, qualification, scope, independence or professional approval.

## 3. Logical trust architecture

### 3.1 Trust domains

Keep four data/trust domains distinct:

| Domain | Purpose | Trust treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Public catalogue/release | Exercise/equipment/guide content, safe approval traceability, immutable release identity | Public data; authenticity verified through signed release and approved trust chain |
| Signed trust metadata | Reviewer authorisations, public-key references, status/revocation snapshots, signed review attestations and release attestations | Public or distributable cryptographic metadata; signed, versioned and privacy-reviewed |
| Restricted governance records | Verified identity, credential source outcomes, private evidence, conflicts, case/appeal records, registrar decisions, audit and retention metadata | Restricted, least privilege, purpose-limited; never embedded in public app assets or user backups |
| User-owned application data | Profiles, equipment inventory, workouts, injury/user-entered health context, programme history and backups | Remains separate from reviewer trust; never used as a governance credential store |

A shared identifier does not make records shareable. Public projections must be deliberately generated from restricted authoritative records after disclosure review.

### 3.2 Candidate signed objects (logical contracts only)

The following objects describe the minimum logical responsibilities. They are **not** final JSON schemas, field names or API contracts.

**A. Reviewer authorisation credential**

Must bind, at minimum:
- stable pseudonymous reviewer reference;
- reviewer public-key identifier and key material or verifiable key reference;
- explicitly approved scope IDs, jurisdiction and exclusions/limitations;
- authorisation status and monotonically increasing governance sequence;
- issue time, start/effective time, expiry/recheck time and policy version;
- restricted verification-record reference (not identity documents);
- issuing registrar reference, registrar authority and signature;
- lifecycle linkage for renewal, restriction, suspension, revocation and supersession.

It must not expose legal name, date of birth, home address, personal contact details, credential-document copies, unnecessary registration identifiers or private case information.

**B. Review attestation**

Must bind:
- pseudonymous reviewer reference and reviewer key ID;
- exact target type, stable ID, version and canonical digest;
- exact review scope and jurisdiction;
- decision and any bounded limitations/conditions;
- evidence-set digest/reference and checklist/policy version;
- review timestamp and signing-format version;
- any required conflict assessment reference/outcome in a privacy-safe form;
- signature made by the reviewer key.

A review attestation cannot itself establish that the reviewer was qualified or authorised. That is established by validating the linked authorisation and status at the relevant decision time.

**C. Governance status/revocation snapshot**

Must bind:
- governance authority identity and trust-chain reference;
- monotonically increasing sequence number;
- issue time and trusted-time evidence;
- authorisation additions, changes, restrictions, suspensions, expiry and revocations;
- references to the policy/qualification versions governing those changes;
- validity/freshness metadata and signature.

A snapshot must not claim that a credential is current indefinitely. Missing updates, stale snapshots or uncertain time produce a non-current state. The exact sequence, timestamp and freshness rules remain to be approved.

**D. Catalogue release attestation**

Must bind:
- catalogue identity, immutable release ID/version and exact payload digest;
- exact manifest and schema versions;
- the set of included review attestations and their target digests;
- proof that required scope, independent-review, evidence and lifecycle checks passed;
- release authority identity/key reference and signature;
- the governance snapshot sequence(s) used for the release decision.

A release signature authenticates the release authority's statement about the release. It does not replace the underlying reviewer attestations or make an unsupported claim valid.

**E. Public traceability projection**

Must be generated from the authoritative restricted decision and signed public trust metadata. It may expose only fields later approved under G-12, such as pseudonymous reviewer reference, exact target/version, scope/jurisdiction, decision/lifecycle, policy version and honest freshness information. Every field and linked evidence excerpt requires privacy/disclosure review. No public projection may contain restricted identity, credential, conflict, complaint, investigation, health or security information.

## 4. Proposed verification sequence

The verifier should process the trust chain in a deterministic order and return structured reasons, not a single opaque “trusted” flag.

1. Validate the immutable release envelope and exact payload byte digest before parsing or materialising content.
2. Validate release schema, catalogue identity, release ID/version and supported algorithms/formats.
3. Verify the release authority signature against a previously trusted release-authority credential and trust root.
4. Verify the included governance snapshot signature, authority, sequence, time evidence and policy version.
5. Verify each reviewer authorisation signature and its chain to the governance trust root.
6. Verify that the reviewer authorisation covers the exact scope, jurisdiction, target class and review time, and that it was not expired, suspended, restricted or revoked according to the status information available.
7. Verify each reviewer attestation signature and exact target/version/digest/evidence binding.
8. Verify required independent reviews, conflict outcomes, role separation and the approved professional qualification policy version for the high-consequence scope.
9. Verify that the catalogue release authority checked all mandatory evidence and did not waive a failed or unknown gate.
10. Derive the current/historically verified/stale/unknown/adverse state using approved freshness and trusted-time policy.
11. Only then make the approved content available for the use permitted by its exact scope and status.

Any failed mandatory check must make the affected content unavailable for the dependent safety-critical use. Error handling must preserve workout controls and historical data. Do not substitute another exercise, guide or approval automatically when trust fails.

## 5. Trust-root bootstrap, keys and cryptographic design questions

### 5.1 Trust-root bootstrap

The application must never trust a public key solely because that key appears in the catalogue, manifest, registry or payload it is being asked to verify.

Evaluate at least these alternatives in a later architecture decision:
- a root public key or root-key set pinned in a trusted application release;
- a separately signed, versioned trust-root bundle distributed through a controlled update channel;
- a staged root-rotation mechanism where an existing trusted root authorises its successor, with an independently recoverable emergency path.

For each option, assess first install, offline first use, application update, rollback, root compromise, root loss, key rotation, recovery and long-term support. No option is selected here. Do not generate or store real keys as part of this design stage.

### 5.2 Key separation and custody

Use distinct cryptographic identities for reviewer signing, governance/registrar signing, catalogue release signing and root trust. Do not reuse one private key for multiple roles.

A future key-custody design must address:
- generation and proof of control;
- protected storage and least-privilege access;
- multi-person control for high-impact root operations;
- backup and disaster recovery without uncontrolled key copies;
- rotation, expiry, suspension, compromise and revocation;
- audit logs and independent assurance;
- incident containment and emergency release/revocation;
- staff/provider departure and service exit.

Evaluate browser-compatible signature algorithms and supported platform APIs against the target devices and offline requirements. The existing Web Crypto SHA-256 code is a content digest facility; it is not a digital-signature/key-management implementation. Do not assume that a particular signature algorithm, key format, hardware module, provider or custody product is selected.

### 5.3 Canonicalisation and domain separation

Retain strict allowlisted canonical payloads, explicit schema/format versions and domain separation for digest and signature inputs. Signatures must cover the exact immutable semantic payload, not mutable UI labels or loosely serialised objects. Reject unknown critical fields, duplicate identifiers, malformed encodings, unsupported algorithms and ambiguous canonical forms. Digest, signature and trust-policy versions must be explicit and independently upgradeable.

## 6. Offline freshness, revocation and rollback

The app must distinguish at least:
- **Current (verified):** the complete trust chain validates, the exact target/scope is authorised, and the applicable approved freshness policy is satisfied using trusted time/status.
- **Historically verified — freshness unknown:** the signature chain validates against a previously trusted snapshot, but a later suspension, expiry or revocation cannot be ruled out.
- **Stale:** a required freshness interval has elapsed or a required refresh failed.
- **Unknown/unavailable:** trust root, time, registry, signature, required evidence or status cannot be established.
- **Restricted/suspended/revoked/expired/withdrawn:** an authoritative adverse lifecycle state applies.
- **Invalid:** cryptographic, schema, binding, provenance or policy validation failed.

Requirements:
- A user-controlled device clock alone must not establish current authority.
- A signed snapshot must be sequence-bound and rollback-resistant. A device must not silently accept a lower sequence after securely retaining a higher trusted sequence.
- An offline snapshot may prove only the state represented by that snapshot at its trusted issue time. It cannot prove no later revocation exists.
- Unknown, stale, invalid or adverse trust must fail closed for high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance matching and any other use that requires current reviewer authority.
- No universal freshness interval or offline grace period is chosen. G-10 requires scope-specific risk justification and explicit owner approval.
- If the device cannot safely retain trusted sequence state, its security limitations must be explicit; do not claim rollback protection that the platform cannot guarantee.
- Online refresh failures must not silently downgrade to current status.
- Cache and service-worker lifecycle must not delete trust state in a way that silently resets anti-rollback protection.
- A lost/reset device must re-establish trust through an approved bootstrap/recovery process rather than trusting an arbitrary cached registry.
- Historical workout records and display snapshots remain accessible even if current catalogue approval becomes unavailable.

## 7. Role and separation-of-duties model

| Role | Proposed responsibility | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Accountable policy owner | Own policy, approve governance direction and ensure resources/oversight | Cannot substitute for professional qualification or override missing specialist approval |
| Credential verifier | Independently check identity/credential against authoritative sources and record outcome | Cannot appoint themselves or make unsupported scope assumptions |
| Reviewer registrar | Grant/recheck/restrict/revoke narrowly defined reviewer authority and issue signed authorisation | Cannot approve the reviewer's own work or credential |
| Reviewer | Assess evidence within active scope and sign their own exact-target decision | Cannot share keys, self-approve or decide outside scope |
| Independent reviewer | Independently assess high-consequence content and sign separately | Cannot merely countersign another review |
| Catalogue curator | Prepare source material, evidence bundle and candidate content | Cannot represent curation as professional approval |
| Release authority | Verify all required approvals and sign immutable release | Cannot waive missing, expired, out-of-scope or adverse approval |
| Trust-root custodian | Operate root-key lifecycle and recovery under multi-person controls | Cannot be a single unreviewed point of control |
| Investigator/appeal authority | Handle complaints, restrictions, investigation and independent appeal under G-08 | Cannot collapse precautionary restriction into a finding or self-review |
| Independent auditor | Test process, controls, records and public-status accuracy | Cannot audit their own operational decisions without independent assurance |

Exact role appointments, delegation, independence tests and operational service levels remain governance decisions. This table is a separation model, not an appointment.

## 8. Restricted records, privacy and publication

Before any real information is collected, the actual responsible entity must complete the G-09 privacy/legal assessment and approve:
- purpose and lawful handling basis;
- collection notice and data minimisation;
- identity/credential evidence alternatives and source references;
- role-based access and access logging;
- correction, disclosure, retention, legal hold and disposal schedule by record class;
- incident and breach response;
- provider, hosting location and cross-border assessment;
- service continuity, export, deletion and exit controls.

Prefer retaining a verified outcome, source, date, verifier and necessary evidence reference over retaining document images where that is sufficient and lawful. Do not put restricted records in Git, app assets, public catalogue, public issue trackers, normal user backups or client-side storage.

The public projection must be built from approved fields only. Redaction is not a substitute for a lawful disclosure basis. Withdrawn or superseded decisions need accurate public lifecycle treatment without exposing allegations or private investigation material. Public copies and third-party caches may not be fully retractable; explain that limitation honestly.

User workout, injury and health context is a separate protected user-data domain. It must not flow into reviewer credential records, public traceability, reviewer telemetry or trust metadata except where a separately assessed lawful necessity is explicitly approved. Do not let reviewer-trust outages corrupt or lock historical user activity.

## 9. Threat model and required mitigations

| Threat / failure | Required design response |
|---|---|
| Forged reviewer ID or role string | Resolve to signed authorisation chained to trusted root; reject self-asserted identity |
| Unqualified or wrong-scope reviewer | Verify scope-specific credential policy and active scope at decision time; fail closed |
| Reviewer credential expires or is revoked | Signed status/revocation update; freshness state; block current-authority claims |
| Reviewer key is stolen/shared | Key custody, incident response, revoke key, assess affected approvals and releases |
| Reviewer signs another person's work | Individual key control, audit and independent review; no shared credentials |
| Reviewer self-approval or conflict | Role separation and conflict decision; reject prohibited combinations |
| Target changes after review | Exact canonical digest/version binding; new version requires new review |
| Evidence changed or unavailable | Bind evidence references/digests; unresolved mandatory evidence prevents approval |
| Compromised release key | Revoke release authority, trusted root recovery, superseding release and impact assessment |
| Trust-root substitution | Bootstrap from pre-established trusted root; never trust a key introduced by the payload |
| Old signed snapshot replay | Monotonic sequence, retained high-water mark and explicit recovery policy |
| Clock manipulation | Trusted time evidence; uncertain time means unknown/stale |
| Offline revocation gap | Historical-only state; no false current claim; fail closed for high-consequence use |
| Registry or network outage | Preserve app and user history; mark trust unavailable; no downgrade or bypass |
| Privacy leak via public projection | Allowlist, field-by-field disclosure review, separate restricted source and output audit |
| Cache/service-worker deletion | Separate namespaces and test trust-state retention/anti-rollback semantics |
| Backup/restore transplant | Do not restore governance authority from an ordinary user backup; re-establish trust through approved bootstrap |
| AI-generated or inferred credential claims | No authority from AI inference; independent human verification required |

## 11. Required verification and assurance plan

A future implementation gate must include unit, integration, browser, offline, backup/restore and independent security review. At minimum, test:

**Cryptography and binding**
- valid signature chain; invalid signature; wrong key; unsupported algorithm; malformed key/signature; canonicalisation ambiguity;
- tampered target, version, scope, evidence digest, decision, policy version and release manifest;
- a valid digest without a valid signature never grants authority;
- a valid signature from an untrusted key never grants authority.

**Identity, scope and lifecycle**
- forged reviewer reference; unknown/revoked key; credential expired before/after review; scope mismatch; jurisdiction mismatch; registrar not authorised;
- suspended, restricted, withdrawn and superseded authorisation;
- reviewer, registrar, curator and release-authority role conflicts;
- required two independent reviewers missing, duplicated or not independent;
- appeal/reinstatement and historical audit append-only behaviour.

**Freshness and offline**
- trusted time unavailable, device clock changed, stale snapshot, sequence rollback, snapshot replay, revocation while offline, long offline duration, refresh failure and root rotation;
- first install online/offline; app update; cache deletion; device reset; backup restore to a new device;
- no state falsely shown as current; high-consequence candidate matching fails closed whenever required freshness is unknown.

**Privacy and continuity**
- restricted identity/credential/case fields never enter public payload, app assets, logs or user backup;
- public projection only includes allowlisted, approved fields;
- retention/disposal and access controls tested in the actual selected service;
- governance outage, withdrawal or catalogue failure does not delete, rewrite or block historical workout records and user-owned data.

**Release and operations**
- release signer cannot bypass missing review gates;
- key compromise and emergency revocation/recovery;
- audit trail, monitoring, incident response, continuity, provider exit and restoration;
- exact source/bundle parity and full browser/offline validation on the final implementation commit.

Passing automated tests alone is not professional qualification, legal compliance, security assurance or owner approval. Independent specialist review remains mandatory for the relevant claims and security boundary.

This proposal does not authorise:
- changes to application runtime, catalogue schema/payload, manifest, database, backup, migration, service worker, C3, Adaptive Brain or UI;
- creation, generation, custody or use of real private signing keys or trust roots;
- connection to or selection of a credential-verification, identity, registry, hosting or key-management provider;
- collection of real identity, credential, conflict, complaint, appeal or other personal information;
- reviewer recruitment, appointment, authorisation or professional credential decisions;
- production catalogue, exercise, equipment, guide, movement-demand or injury-related approvals;
- public reviewer publication, a live reviewer register or complaints/appeals service;

If a required governance or technical fact is unknown, the affected approval must remain unavailable. No implementation may weaken the existing fail-closed safety boundary or compromise historical user workout data to make the trust service appear operational.
