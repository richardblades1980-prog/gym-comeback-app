# V21.2 — G-03 qualification standards by review scope

**Status:** FRAMEWORK APPROVED — owner PASS recorded for G-03 in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. This approval covers the governance framework only; operational details and prerequisites remain unresolved as stated below.

**Governance boundary:** Australia is the initial reviewer-governance jurisdiction. G-01 project-owner accountability and G-02 Australia-first direction are recorded in the governance decision register. No reviewer credentials are being collected and no production catalogue content is approved by this proposal.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve the **scope-by-scope qualification framework and its safeguards** as the method for developing final Australian qualification criteria with appropriately qualified professional input.

Do not treat this as approval of any particular profession, registration, degree, certificate, membership, experience threshold or individual reviewer. Those details remain open until authoritative sources and professional input have been assessed.

## 2. Core rule

A reviewer is eligible only for a specifically defined scope where independently verified evidence demonstrates current competence relevant to the actual decisions in that scope.

Qualification is not a general badge. A credential or professional title does not automatically establish competence for every review task. A reviewer may be authorised for one scope and excluded from another. Each review must still be bound to the exact target, evidence and protocol.

Assess four distinct questions:

1. **Identity:** is this person who they claim to be?
2. **Qualification:** does verified education, registration, credential and/or relevant experience establish competence for this specific scope?
3. **Current standing and limits:** is the qualification current, unrestricted or appropriately understood, and valid for the work and jurisdiction?
4. **Task authorisation:** has governance explicitly authorised this person for this scope, with exclusions and a review period?

A positive answer to one question does not imply a positive answer to the others.

## 3. Scope-by-scope framework

| Review scope | Competence that must be demonstrated | Qualification evidence to assess (not pre-approved) | Scope limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exercise identity and execution | Ability to distinguish exercise variants, equipment/setup dependencies, movement execution and meaningful naming differences | Relevant formal study, recognised training, demonstrable domain experience, structured assessment and references appropriate to the actual task | Cannot approve clinical suitability, injury safety or claims outside exercise identification/execution |
| Equipment identity and configuration | Ability to distinguish equipment types, mechanisms, orientation, loading systems, setup and configuration attributes | Relevant technical/product knowledge, manufacturer or engineering documentation, hands-on experience and documented assessment | Cannot infer exercise safety or therapeutic suitability from equipment identity alone |
| Movement-demand and injury relevance | Competence to assess movement/loading demands, relevant anatomy and biomechanics, limitations of evidence, and the boundary between general movement description and individual clinical advice | Current, independently verifiable Australian professional qualification/registration or other formally recognised competence whose scope demonstrably covers the specific assessment; authoritative status/scope checks and independent professional input required | Highest-consequence scope. No generic fitness or product credential is assumed sufficient. No diagnosis, individual medical clearance, guaranteed safety or treatment advice |
| Programme-slot identity and mapping | Ability to interpret historical programme labels, intended exercise identity, context and evidence without rewriting historical meaning | Relevant programme/exercise domain expertise, source provenance, demonstrated mapping method and independent checking | Similar names are not aliases. Must preserve original slot IDs, labels and historical records; uncertain mappings remain unresolved |
| Guide and instruction content | Ability to assess accuracy, completeness, clarity, setup, execution cues, contraindications/limitations and audience suitability for the specific guide | Relevant instructional/domain expertise; where a guide makes clinical, rehabilitation or injury-related claims, appropriately scoped professional qualification and review are additionally required | General technique guidance must not drift into diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation prescription or promises of safety |

This table defines the competence questions, not credential answers. The final criteria may subdivide a scope where the task requires different expertise. A reviewer authorised for exercise identity, for example, is not thereby authorised for injury relevance.

## 4. Qualification assessment dimensions

For each scope, the final matrix must explicitly define:

- accepted qualification or competence pathways, supported by authoritative evidence;
- issuing or registering authority and the authoritative verification source;
- identity linkage between the person and the qualification;
- current status, expiry, restrictions, conditions and relevant scope of practice;
- jurisdiction and any state/territory-specific limitation;
- relevant experience only where justified, with a defensible way to assess it;
- required continuing professional development or revalidation only where the relevant authority or professional standard supports it;
- permitted decisions, prohibited claims and escalation/referral boundaries;
- recheck triggers and expiry/re-authorisation requirements;
- evidence required where no statutory register exists, without treating self-attestation as independent verification.

Do not invent a universal years-of-experience threshold, CPD hours, credential level or renewal period. These may differ by profession, authority and task.

## 5. Higher-consequence movement-demand and injury relevance

This scope needs the strongest qualification threshold and review controls.

The reviewer must be able to assess the full relevant demand profile, not merely a primary-muscle label. Depending on the movement, this may include secondary loading, joint actions, stabilisation, balance, foot pressure, weight-bearing, propulsion, impact, landing, bracing, grip, positioning and equipment setup.

Required governance safeguards:

- final qualification criteria must be developed with appropriately qualified Australian professionals and checked against authoritative registration/credential sources;
- the scope must explicitly state what the reviewer may assess and what requires a different discipline;
- two independent appropriately scoped reviews remain required for high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance metadata, under the approved trust policy;
- at least one of those reviewers must meet the final approved professional qualification standard for the specific assessment;
- reviewers must identify uncertainty, evidence gaps, exclusions and conflicts rather than converting them into positive claims;
- approval describes the reviewed metadata only; it is not individual medical clearance, diagnosis, treatment advice or a guarantee that an exercise is safe for a particular person.

If the final standard cannot be established or verified, the content remains unreviewed for injury-aware matching.

## 6. Cross-scope and multidisciplinary decisions

Some catalogue decisions span more than one discipline. Do not stretch one reviewer’s authority to cover the entire question.

- Split the review into independently reviewable claims where possible.
- Assign each claim to a reviewer with the corresponding scope.
- Where claims interact, require a documented synthesis review by people whose combined scopes cover the interaction.
- If an issue cannot be split safely or the required expertise is unavailable, record it as unresolved and do not approve the affected claim.
- A reviewer may recommend that another specialist is required but may not self-expand their authorisation.

## 7. Independence, verification and records

The eventual qualification register and verification procedure must capture, in a restricted system:

- reviewer identity and identity-verification outcome;
- exact qualification/registration and issuing authority;
- authoritative source checked, date, verifier and result;
- jurisdiction, scope assessment, permitted tasks and exclusions;
- current status, expiry/recheck date and restrictions;
- evidence supporting any experience-based pathway;
- conflicts, conditions, suspension or revocation;
- authorisation decision, decision-maker and audit history.

Public catalogue data should expose only a pseudonymous reviewer reference and minimum approval traceability. Do not store credential documents or unnecessary personal identity data in Git, public assets, app bundles, catalogue payloads or user backups.

No credential collection or live register creation is authorised until G-04 identity/credential verification and G-09 privacy/access/retention decisions are approved and safeguards exist.

## 8. Australian professional input and authoritative verification

Before final credential criteria are proposed:

1. Identify the exact assessment tasks and claims within each scope.
2. Obtain input from appropriately qualified Australian professionals for those tasks, especially movement-demand/injury relevance.
3. Identify the relevant statutory registration authority, credential issuer or other authoritative body for each proposed pathway.
4. Confirm the authority's public verification method, current-status information, restrictions and scope information.
5. Obtain jurisdiction-specific legal/regulatory advice where the distinction between protected titles, registration, membership and permitted practice is material.
6. Record disagreement and unresolved interpretation; do not substitute informal web profiles, marketing claims, self-declarations or AI inference for authoritative verification.

This proposal intentionally does not name a profession or assert that a particular Australian qualification is sufficient. Those choices need task-specific evidence and qualified review.

## 10. Open questions for the final qualification matrix

- Which precise tasks and claims are included in each scope?
- Should any scope be split into narrower specialist sub-scopes?
- Which Australian authorities or issuers can authoritatively verify each proposed qualification pathway?
- What evidence is acceptable for technical or domain expertise where no statutory registration applies?
- What exclusions and escalation rules are mandatory?
- What recheck interval is supported by the relevant authority or professional standard?
- Which decisions require multidisciplinary review beyond the existing high-consequence dual-review rule?
