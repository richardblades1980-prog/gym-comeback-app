**Status:** DECISION DESIGN — G-01 and G-02 owner directions recorded; G-03 to G-11 frameworks approved in principle; G-12 remains open. This register turns the approved Reviewer Trust Policy into a controlled set of governance decisions. It does not make those decisions on the owner's behalf and does not authorise reviewer onboarding, credential collection, production review, code, schema, service or publication.

**Policy approval basis:** owner PASS of `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-POLICY.md`.

## 1. Recommended decision sequence

Do not attempt to decide every operational detail in one step. Resolve the decisions in dependency order:

1. **Accountability and operating boundary:** who owns the policy, who can make governance decisions, and which jurisdictions the service intends to support.
2. **Scope qualification standards:** what evidence and qualifications are acceptable for each review scope, especially movement-demand/injury-relevance.
3. **Reviewer lifecycle and independence:** appointment, rechecks, conflicts, dual review, suspension and appeal authority.
4. **Evidence and approval standards:** what a reviewer must inspect and record before approving each type of content.
5. **Privacy and records operations:** what personal data is necessary, who may access it, how it is protected and retained.
6. **Freshness and offline meaning:** when a cached approval may be described as current, historically verified or unknown.
7. **Operational readiness:** who funds, maintains and audits the process, and what happens if the service or governance authority is unavailable.

Only after these decisions are resolved should a separate technical implementation gate specify systems and controls.

## 2. Decision register

G-01 and G-02 are **DECIDED IN PRINCIPLE**; G-03 through G-11 frameworks are **APPROVED IN PRINCIPLE** as recorded below. G-12 remains **OPEN**. Recommendations below are proposed defaults to help discussion; they are not adopted policy until the owner explicitly approves them.

### G-01 — Accountable policy owner and governance authority

**Decision:** identify the accountable owner of this policy and the body/person authorised to appoint reviewers, approve scope criteria, suspend/revoke authority, resolve appeals and approve release governance.

**Owner decision (accepted):** project-owner governance. The project owner retains final accountability for the Gym Comeback trust policy, governance process and release-governance decisions.

**Mandatory separation:** the project owner does not thereby become a professionally qualified reviewer, credential verifier or specialist approver. Professional qualifications and scope must be independently verified; specialist movement-demand/injury-relevance decisions must be made by appropriately qualified reviewers. The project owner cannot override missing, expired, out-of-scope, rejected or otherwise invalid professional approval. Reviewer appointment, curation, independent review and release approval must remain separated so no person can unilaterally appoint themselves, approve their own work and release it.

**Still open within G-01:** deputy/succession arrangements, decision records, escalation route and the detailed delegation matrix. These must be settled before operational onboarding.

### G-02 — Intended operating jurisdictions

**Decision:** identify the countries/regions in which reviewers may be appointed and whose users/content the catalogue is intended to serve.

**Owner decision (accepted):** Australia is the initial reviewer-governance jurisdiction. The initial reviewer appointment and credential-verification process is to be designed for Australia; this does not itself select Australian credential categories or approve any reviewer.

**Boundary:** do not extend reviewer authority or represent the governance process as covering another country/region until that jurisdiction's credential-verification sources, professional rules, privacy obligations and review criteria have been assessed and separately approved. A credential valid in one jurisdiction does not automatically authorise a reviewer in another.

**Still open within G-02:** treatment of overseas credentials for work within the Australian scope, state/territory-specific differences where relevant, and the process for approving any later jurisdiction expansion.

### G-03 — Qualification standard by review scope

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G03-QUALIFICATION-STANDARDS-FRAMEWORK.md` is approved as the method for developing final Australian qualification criteria with appropriately qualified professional input. This does not select or approve any credential category, profession, experience threshold or individual reviewer.

**Decision:** approve an evidence-based qualification matrix for each of the five review scopes.

**Recommended direction:** require demonstrable competence directly relevant to the specific decision. For movement-demand/injury-relevance, use a current, independently verified professional credential and scope of practice that demonstrably covers the movement/loading assessment being made. Use independent domain expertise where a question spans disciplines.

**Must resolve:** acceptable credential categories, issuing authorities, minimum relevant experience (if any), verification source, status/restriction checks, jurisdiction limits, and which claims a reviewer may or may not approve.

**Important:** this requires input from appropriately qualified professionals and, where relevant, jurisdiction-specific regulatory/legal advice. Do not choose titles or credential thresholds by guesswork or use AI as the qualifying authority.

### G-04 — Identity and credential verification method

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G04-IDENTITY-CREDENTIAL-VERIFICATION.md` is approved as the independent, source-based verification method with explicit fail-closed outcomes. This does not authorise collection of real identity or credential data, selection of a provider or reviewer appointment.

**Decision:** approve how identity and qualifications are independently checked and documented.

**Recommended direction:** verify directly against authoritative issuing/registration sources wherever possible; record the source, date, verifier, result and scope assessment. Treat unavailable or inconclusive verification as not verified.

**Must resolve:** acceptable identity evidence, alternative verification when authoritative records are unavailable, who performs checks, how discrepancies are handled, and how rechecks are triggered.

**Privacy condition:** do not collect real credentials until a restricted, access-controlled record process and privacy basis are approved.

### G-05 — Appointment, term and recheck cadence

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G05-REVIEWER-APPOINTMENT-TERM-RECHECK.md` is approved as the lifecycle framework: finite scope-specific appointments, event-triggered reassessment, explicit lifecycle states and renewal as a new decision. Exact terms and intervals remain to be justified by scope and credential pathway.

**Decision:** define how long authorisation lasts and when it must be reviewed.

**Recommended direction:** authorisation should have a finite review date, and must be rechecked earlier when credential status, scope, jurisdiction or a material concern changes. Do not use a never-expiring “trusted” flag.

**Must resolve:** appointment term, recheck interval, reminder/expiry process, grace periods (if any), and whether scope-specific periods differ.

**Cannot assume:** a universal validity period is appropriate for every profession or jurisdiction.

### G-06 — Independence and conflicts

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G06-INDEPENDENCE-CONFLICTS.md` is approved as the framework for assignment-specific conflict disclosure, assessment, recusal, proportionate safeguards and independent dual-review principles. This does not appoint reviewers, collect real declarations or approve production reviews.

**Decision:** define material conflicts, prohibited relationships, disclosure requirements and safeguards.

**Recommended direction:** no sole self-approval; mandatory disclosure before each review; remove a reviewer where a material conflict cannot be managed transparently. For high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance records, require two independent appropriately scoped reviews, including at least one reviewer meeting the approved professional qualification standard.

**Must resolve:** financial-interest thresholds, permitted mitigations, definition of independence, how dual reviews are kept independent, and any narrowly defined emergency process.

**Cannot assume:** two signatures mean independent review if both reviewers rely on the same unchecked conclusion.

### G-07 — Evidence hierarchy and review protocols

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G07-EVIDENCE-HIERARCHY-REVIEW-PROTOCOLS.md` is approved as the evidence-governance framework: claim-specific, scope-specific evidence assessment; exact target/version binding; traceable sources and locators; quality, relevance and limitation assessment; proportionate independent corroboration; explicit conflict and uncertainty handling; bounded decisions; minimum review records; and reassessment/maintenance principles. This approval is limited to governance principles and scope-specific checklist development. It does not approve production catalogue content, specific professional standards or credentials, reviewer appointments, real credential/conflict collection, or technical implementation.

**Decision:** approve evidence standards and checklists separately for each review scope.

**Approved boundary:** the five review scopes remain distinct. High-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance reviews require two independent appropriately scoped reviews, including at least one reviewer meeting the final approved professional qualification standard. Evidence does not provide individual medical clearance; unknown or unresolved material remains unknown and injury-aware matching fails closed where required.

**Decision:** approve evidence standards and checklists separately for each review scope.

**Recommended direction:** require traceable source/version/locator and an explanation of which claim it supports. Prefer authoritative primary evidence; corroborate where appropriate; record conflicts and uncertainty. A missing or ambiguous required field remains unknown.

**Must resolve:** source hierarchy by scope, treatment of manufacturer material, standards, peer-reviewed research, direct inspection, conflicting evidence, evidence age/relevance and checklist maintenance.

**Safety boundary:** injury-relevance review must consider the complete relevant loading demands, not merely primary muscle labels. An approval is not individual medical clearance.

### G-08 — Decisions, withdrawal and appeals

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G08-DECISIONS-WITHDRAWAL-APPEALS.md` is approved as the governance framework for distinct decision outcomes; concern intake and proportionate triage; precautionary temporary restrictions; independent investigation; reasoned final decisions; independent appeals and reconsideration; evidence-based reinstatement; proportionate notifications; and append-only decision/audit history. Approval includes procedural fairness, protection for good-faith reporting, separation of functions where practicable, explicit authority/scope, and preservation of historical user workout data. This does not appoint decision-makers or investigators, establish a live complaints/appeals service, authorise personal-data collection, approve production catalogue content or technical implementation.

**Decision:** define who can accept/reject reviews, suspend reviewers, withdraw approvals, investigate concerns and hear appeals.

**Approved boundary:** temporary restrictions are precautionary, not findings of fault; appeals do not automatically stay a safety restriction or reinstate authority; no automatic reinstatement occurs merely through passage of time. Original evidence and decisions remain traceable, with corrections and superseding outcomes appended rather than silently overwritten. High-consequence unresolved uncertainty remains fail-closed.

**Still to resolve before operations:** delegated decision authority, response/appeal timeframes, standing and evidence-access rules, external referral criteria, privacy/retention and operational procedures. These require the relevant later governance decisions and appropriate Australian professional/legal input.

### G-09 — Privacy, access and retention

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G09-PRIVACY-ACCESS-RETENTION.md` is approved as the privacy-governance framework: purpose limitation and data minimisation; separation of public traceability from restricted reviewer/case records and user workout/health data; least-privilege role principles; controlled collection and disclosure; record-class-specific retention and disposal; access/correction routes; security and incident governance; provider/cross-border assessment; and a documented pre-operation privacy assessment. This does not establish legal compliance, select a provider, set final retention periods, authorise real personal/credential data collection, create a live register or complaints service, or authorise technical implementation.

**Decision:** approve privacy, access, retention and incident-governance principles before any real identity, credential, conflict, complaint or appeal information is collected.

**Approved boundary:** minimise personal data; prefer authoritative verification outcomes and source metadata over retaining document copies; keep restricted governance records separate from public catalogue assets and user workout/health records; publish pseudonymous reviewer traceability only; define record-class-specific retention and secure disposal before collection. Appropriate Australian privacy/legal advice and a completed assessment remain prerequisites to operation.

**Still to resolve before operations:** applicable legal basis and notices, responsible entity/roles, exact access permissions, retention schedule, security controls, provider and cross-border assessment, correction/disclosure process, breach response and independent Australian privacy/legal review.

### G-10 — Meaning of current status and offline freshness

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G10-OFFLINE-FRESHNESS-STATUS.md` is approved as the governance framework for distinct current, historically verified, stale, unknown and adverse states; exact target/version and provenance binding; scope-specific freshness governance; trusted-time uncertainty; offline behaviour; safety-critical fail-closed handling; revocation propagation/conflict handling; user-facing explanations; and historical workout continuity. This does not set final freshness intervals or offline grace periods, select trusted-time or revocation technology, approve a production status schema, or authorise technical implementation.

**Decision:** define when an approval can be described as current, historically verified, stale or unknown, and what offline devices may safely claim.

**Approved boundary:** online decisions use the latest authoritative status available. Offline devices may state only what their locally held, verifiable record establishes at a known time. Unknown/stale status must never be described as current; high-consequence injury-aware matching fails closed when required status/freshness cannot be established. Historical workouts remain accessible and unchanged.

**Still to resolve before implementation:** freshness periods by scope, trusted-time evidence, first-use bootstrap, revocation propagation objectives, any narrowly justified offline interval, and treatment of devices offline for extended periods. A user-controlled device clock or cached approval does not prove current professional standing or absence of a later withdrawal; exact intervals and technical mechanisms require separate risk assessment and approval.

### G-11 — Audit, continuity and funding

**Owner decision (PASS):** `docs/V21.2-G11-AUDIT-CONTINUITY-FUNDING.md` is approved as the governance framework for responsibility/delegation separation, operational readiness and resourcing, funding controls, independent audit and assurance, risk/corrective action, continuity and succession, outage/pause/exit rules, and owner oversight reporting. This does not appoint people, commit expenditure, select providers, establish a live service or authorise technical implementation.

**Decision:** identify who maintains the reviewer process, audits it, funds verification/storage/services and responds to incidents, and how the process remains safe during disruption or exit.

**Approved boundary:** require named accountable and operational roles, adequate recurring resources, independent risk-based audit, tested continuity/exit plans and transparent owner reporting before production reliance. If governance capacity, funding or a required service is unavailable, pause or narrow affected approvals rather than silently lowering review requirements.

**Still to resolve before operations:** operational owner/deputy, responsibility matrix, realistic budget and funding authority, provider(s), audit competence and cadence, service/incident responsibilities, succession, recovery and exit/transfer arrangements.

### G-12 — Public traceability and restricted data contract

**Decision:** define what users can see about an approval and what remains restricted.

**Recommended direction:** public record shows a pseudonymous reviewer reference, approved scope, decision, target/version binding, policy/checklist version, evidence references suitable for publication and a clear freshness state. Private identity and credential evidence remain restricted.

**Must resolve:** exact public fields, evidence redaction, reviewer privacy, user-facing explanation, accessibility and handling of withdrawn or superseded approvals.

## 3. Cross-cutting policy rules (recommended as non-negotiable)

These rules should remain invariant regardless of the selected provider or technical architecture:

- No reviewer authority without independently verified identity and scope-relevant qualification.
- No approval outside the reviewer's active, explicitly granted scope.
- No approval without review of the exact target and traceable evidence.
- No self-approval as the sole approval.
- High-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance content requires two independent appropriately scoped reviews under the approved professional qualification standard.
- Unknown, missing, conflicting or out-of-scope information is not converted into a positive claim.
- Material target/evidence/protocol changes require reassessment where they affect the conclusion.
- Suspension, expiry, revocation and withdrawal are explicit states, not silent edits.
- Stale or unknown offline authority is never represented as current.
- Catalogue trust failure must not erase or block access to historical user workout records.
- No cryptographic signature is represented as proof of professional competence, factual correctness or medical safety.

## 4. Required governance outputs before technical implementation

The governance stage is complete only when it produces approved, versioned artefacts:

1. Named accountable policy owner and decision authority. **G-01 direction selected:** project-owner accountability with mandatory independent professional review and no owner override of missing specialist approval; operational delegation and succession remain open.
2. Jurisdiction statement and credential-verification source list. **G-02 direction selected:** Australia is the initial reviewer-governance jurisdiction; overseas credential equivalence and later jurisdiction expansion remain subject to separate assessment and approval.
3. Scope-by-scope qualification and exclusions matrix.
4. Identity/credential verification and recheck procedure.
5. Appointment, independence, conflict and dual-review procedure.
6. Scope-specific evidence standards and review checklists.
7. Complaint, suspension, revocation, withdrawal and appeal procedure.
8. Privacy impact/data-handling and retention decision.
9. Freshness/offline status policy.
10. Audit, incident, funding and continuity plan.
11. Public-versus-restricted reviewer data specification.
12. Owner sign-off on the complete governance package.

A technical implementation gate can then translate these approved outputs into schemas, signing, registry, release validation, offline behaviour and security tests. The technical gate must not silently decide any unresolved governance item.

## 5. Explicit stop conditions

Do not:
- appoint or advertise real qualified reviewers;
- collect credentials or create a live reviewer register;
- mark production catalogue records as qualified-review approved;
- generate signing keys or establish trust roots;
- select cryptographic algorithms or build a signing service;
- change catalogue schema, manifest, database, backup, service worker or runtime;
- implement injury-aware matching or C3;
- publish production catalogue releases;
- change `main`, the Pages release candidate, Pages settings or deployment.

These remain prohibited until their own explicit approval gates pass.
