# V21.2 — G-08 decisions, withdrawal and appeals

**Status:** FRAMEWORK APPROVED — owner PASS recorded for G-08 in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. This approval covers the governance framework only; operational details and prerequisites remain unresolved as stated below.

**Governance basis:** G-01 project-owner accountability; G-03 scope-specific qualification framework; G-04 independent identity/credential verification framework; G-05 finite appointments and rechecks; G-06 independence/conflicts; and G-07 evidence hierarchy and review protocols, as approved in principle in the governance decision register.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve a fair, traceable and safety-conscious process for accepting or rejecting review decisions, responding to concerns, restricting potentially unsafe or unsupported claims, suspending reviewer authority, withdrawing approvals, correcting records, reinstating approvals and hearing appeals.

The process must separate the initial review from subsequent investigation and appeal wherever practicable. It must preserve the original evidence and decision history, protect people who raise concerns in good faith, provide affected parties a meaningful opportunity to respond where safe and appropriate, and fail closed when a credible unresolved concern affects a high-consequence claim.

## 2. Core governance principles

Every decision under this process should:

1. **Identify authority.** The person making a decision must have an expressly delegated role and the relevant scope. Project-owner accountability does not make the owner a professional reviewer or permit override of missing specialist approval.
2. **Separate functions.** A person should not investigate their own disputed review, decide an appeal against their own decision, or unilaterally investigate and finally determine a complaint about themselves.
3. **Preserve the record.** Retain the original target, evidence, review, decision, reasons and timestamps. Corrections and later decisions are appended and linked; prior records are not silently overwritten.
4. **Act proportionately.** Choose the least restrictive action that adequately controls the credible risk, without allowing commercial, schedule or reputational pressure to delay necessary safety action.
5. **Protect procedural fairness.** Give affected people a clear explanation of the issue and a reasonable opportunity to provide relevant information, except where advance notice would create a material safety, evidence-preservation, privacy or interference risk.
6. **Keep scope boundaries.** A decision-maker must not determine professional, clinical, legal or technical questions outside their competence and delegated authority.
7. **Give reasons.** Record the evidence considered, findings, uncertainty, decision, scope, conditions, effective time and review/appeal route.
8. **Protect good-faith reporting.** Do not retaliate against a person for raising a concern honestly. Deliberately fabricated or abusive reports may be handled separately and fairly; inability to substantiate a concern is not proof of bad faith.
9. **Maintain user-data continuity.** A catalogue trust restriction must not erase, corrupt or block access to historical user workout records.
10. **Communicate accurately.** Do not describe a restricted, suspended, expired, withdrawn, disputed or unknown approval as current or verified.

## 3. Decision classes and outcomes

Use distinct outcomes so that a process action is not confused with a final finding:

- **Accept:** the submitted review meets the applicable approved scope, evidence and process requirements for the exact target and claims.
- **Reject:** the review does not meet those requirements; reasons and any remediable deficiencies are recorded.
- **Return for clarification:** material information is missing or ambiguous and can reasonably be supplied without treating the claim as approved.
- **Restrict:** temporarily limit the affected claim, use or reliance while a concern is assessed. Restriction is a precautionary status, not a final finding of misconduct or error.
- **Suspend reviewer authority:** temporarily pause some or all of a reviewer's authorisation where a credible concern affects their eligibility, independence, competence, conduct or ability to perform the role. Scope and duration must be explicit and reviewed.
- **Withdraw approval:** end reliance on a previously accepted claim or review because it is no longer supportable, applicable, valid or authorised. Preserve the historic decision and explain the reason and effective time.
- **Correct or supersede:** append a corrected decision or record that identifies what changed, why, who authorised it and which earlier record it supersedes.
- **Reinstate:** restore a restricted approval or reviewer authority only after the required evidence, eligibility and independent decision checks are satisfied. Reinstatement is a new recorded decision, not automatic expiry of a precaution.
- **Close with no change:** conclude that the concern does not justify a change, with a reasoned record. This does not prevent reconsideration if materially new evidence emerges.

Do not use one generic inactive status for restriction, suspension, expiry, withdrawal and rejection; their meanings and consequences differ.

## 4. Intake and triage of concerns

A concern may arise from a user, reviewer, professional body, source publisher, manufacturer, project participant, audit, new evidence or another credible channel.

The intake record should capture only information necessary to understand and assess the concern:

- A unique concern reference and received date/time.
- The affected target, claim, review, reviewer reference or approval, if known.
- A concise description of the concern and its potential consequence.
- Supporting sources or evidence and their provenance, where available.
- Any immediate risk, affected scope, known exposure and requested correction.
- A safe contact route if a response is needed; anonymous reports may still be triaged where sufficiently specific.

Triage should determine:
1. Whether the report is within the governance process and which scope it concerns.
2. Whether there is a credible potential for material harm, misleading reliance, privacy exposure, conflict, credential/authority failure or evidence-integrity failure.
3. Whether an immediate restriction or preservation action is needed.
4. Who is authorised and suitably independent to investigate.
5. Whether specialist professional, privacy, legal or technical advice is needed.
6. Whether affected users or other parties require a proportionate notice.

Do not reject a concern merely because it is anonymous, inconvenient, or initially incomplete. Seek clarification where possible. Do not collect unnecessary sensitive personal information.

## 5. Temporary restriction and urgent action

Where credible information indicates that continued reliance could create material risk, the authorised decision-maker should be able to apply a temporary, narrowly scoped restriction while investigation proceeds.

The restriction decision should state:
- The precise target, claim, scope or reviewer authority affected.
- The risk or uncertainty prompting action.
- Why the selected restriction is proportionate.
- Who authorised it and when it takes effect.
- What remains available and what must no longer be represented as approved.
- The evidence or action required for reassessment.
- A review point so the restriction is not left indefinite without reconsideration.

For high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance concerns, unresolved material uncertainty prevents positive approval. If a claim cannot be reliably isolated from unsafe reliance, restrict the relevant broader record or use until the boundary is established.

Urgent action may precede a full response from an affected reviewer or author when delay would materially increase risk or compromise evidence. Reasons must be recorded, and a meaningful opportunity to respond should follow as soon as it is safe and practicable.

A temporary restriction is precautionary and must not be presented as a final finding of fault.

## 6. Investigation and findings

Investigations should be assigned to a person or panel with relevant competence, authority and no material conflict. The investigator should:

- Define the question, scope, applicable policy/checklist version and standard of review.
- Preserve relevant source material, target versions, review records, correspondence and audit history.
- Distinguish established facts, disputed facts, professional opinions and unresolved uncertainty.
- Give affected parties a fair opportunity to respond to material adverse information, subject to justified safety, privacy and evidence-preservation limits.
- Obtain independent specialist input where the issue falls outside the investigator's competence.
- Assess whether other approvals, targets or users may be affected by the same cause.
- Record findings and recommended actions without exceeding delegated authority.

A finding that a review was incorrect does not automatically establish reviewer misconduct. A good-faith error, a reasonable disagreement, a process breach, a conflict, deliberate misrepresentation and credential failure are different matters and should be classified separately.

## 7. Final decision and available actions

The authorised decision-maker should consider the investigation record and decide one or more proportionate outcomes:

- No change, with reasons.
- Correct or clarify a claim or review record.
- Return the review for fresh assessment by an appropriately authorised reviewer.
- Require a new independent review, including the full dual-review requirement where high-consequence scope applies.
- Restrict or withdraw the affected approval.
- Suspend, narrow, not renew or revoke reviewer authority under the applicable lifecycle rules.
- Refer a matter to an appropriate professional, regulatory, privacy, legal or other authority where warranted and lawful.
- Identify related records requiring reassessment.
- Notify affected parties and, where necessary, users in clear non-alarmist language.

The decision must state whether it is final within the internal process, what remains unresolved, its effective time, the records affected and how an appeal may be lodged.

The project owner retains policy accountability but cannot replace a missing or adverse specialist professional determination with an owner preference.

## 8. Appeals and reconsideration

An affected reviewer or other directly affected party should be able to request review of a material final decision, subject to clearly stated standing and scope rules.

An appeal should:
- Identify the decision being challenged and the specific alleged error.
- Be submitted within a reasonable period to be set after operational and legal review.
- Explain whether the challenge concerns factual error, process unfairness, misapplied policy, new material evidence, disproportionate action or authority/conflict failure.
- Be decided by a person or panel not materially involved in the original decision or investigation and appropriately qualified for the disputed scope.
- Consider the original record and any admissible new evidence, with a reasoned outcome.
- Be documented and linked to, but not overwrite, the original decision.

Possible appeal outcomes are: affirm, vary, set aside and remit for a fresh decision, or return for additional investigation. The appeal decision should explain its scope and whether any restriction remains in force.

An appeal does not automatically stay a precautionary restriction or reinstate a reviewer. The appeal decision-maker may vary an interim restriction if the evidence and risk justify it.

A separate reconsideration route may be available where genuinely material new evidence emerges after the appeal period or process. Reconsideration is not a way to repeatedly relitigate the same issue without new grounds.

## 9. Reinstatement and return to active status

No approval or reviewer authority should automatically return to active status merely because time has passed or a complaint has closed.

Before reinstatement, establish as applicable:
- The concern or cause has been resolved or adequately controlled.
- Required evidence, source corrections or professional reassessments are complete.
- Identity, credential, scope, appointment and conflict requirements remain valid.
- Any required fresh independent review is complete.
- The decision is made by an authorised person independent of the original disputed decision where practicable.
- Conditions, limitations, monitoring or recheck dates are explicit.

If these requirements cannot be established, maintain the appropriate restricted, suspended, withdrawn or unknown state.

## 10. Notifications, transparency and confidentiality

Communications should be timely, factual, proportionate and limited to people who need the information.

Where relevant:
- Acknowledge receipt and explain the process without promising a particular outcome.
- Notify affected reviewers or authors of material allegations and decisions, subject to justified exceptions.
- Tell affected users when a change materially alters whether they should rely on a claim.
- Explain whether an approval is restricted, withdrawn, corrected or superseded and what the status means.
- Avoid publishing personal, credential, health or confidential investigation details.
- Provide a public-facing explanation of a material catalogue correction or withdrawal without disclosing restricted reviewer records.
- Correct inaccurate public status promptly and preserve the prior public history as a superseded record.

Exact notice periods, disclosure rights, retention rules and privacy exceptions require the separate G-09 privacy decision and appropriate Australian legal advice.

## 11. Minimum decision and audit record

For each intake, restriction, investigation, final decision, appeal and reinstatement, retain as applicable:

- Concern/decision identifier and linked target, claim, reviewer and approval references.
- Received, decision and effective timestamps.
- Applicable policy, checklist and target versions.
- Intake summary, evidence references and provenance.
- Triage and risk rationale.
- Assigned investigator and decision-maker authority, scope and conflict assessment.
- Notices, responses and relevant procedural steps.
- Findings, unresolved issues and specialist advice references.
- Decision, reasons, conditions, affected records and user impact.
- Appeal grounds, independent appeal assignment and outcome.
- Correction, supersession, reassessment and reinstatement links.

The record should be append-only in meaning: later entries may correct or supersede earlier conclusions but must not erase the audit history. Access and retention must be designed under G-09 before real personal or credential data is collected.

## 12. Boundaries and stop conditions

This proposal does not:
- Set legally binding response or appeal deadlines.
- Decide Australian statutory reporting obligations or professional disciplinary procedures.
- Select a complaints provider, investigator, appeal panel or external adjudicator.
- Authorise real reviewer appointments, credential/conflict collection or a live complaints register.
- Approve production catalogue content or any individual review outcome.
- Select databases, access-control architecture, encryption, signing, schemas or APIs.
- Authorise runtime, backup, catalogue, C3, Adaptive Brain, main, Pages or deployment changes.

Operational timelines, privacy handling, statutory duties and external referral criteria require appropriate Australian professional/legal input and the separate G-09 safeguards. No live personal records should be created under this design proposal.
