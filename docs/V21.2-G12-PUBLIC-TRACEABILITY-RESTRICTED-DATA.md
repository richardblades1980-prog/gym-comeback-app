# V21.2 — G-12 public traceability and restricted data contract

**Status:** DESIGN PROPOSAL — owner decision required. This document proposes the governance boundary between information that may be shown publicly to explain reviewer approval and information that must remain restricted. It does not define a production schema, publish reviewer data, authorise collection of personal information, or approve technical implementation.

**Governance basis:** G-01 through G-11 owner directions/framework approvals recorded in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. In particular, G-07 requires exact target/version and evidence traceability; G-08 requires transparent, append-only decisions; G-09 requires privacy and data minimisation; G-10 requires truthful freshness states; and G-11 requires auditability and continuity.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve the principles that determine:
- What a user or public observer may see to understand why a catalogue claim is presented as reviewed.
- What information remains restricted to authorised governance roles.
- How public status relates to the authoritative restricted record.
- How corrections, expiry, restriction, withdrawal and supersession are represented without erasing history.
- How privacy, safety, accessibility and offline clarity are maintained.

The public record should provide meaningful traceability without exposing reviewer identity documents, private credentials, contact details, conflict narratives, complaints or investigation material. Public transparency must not become a substitute for the underlying evidence or imply individual medical clearance.

## 2. Public traceability principles

A public-facing approval explanation should be:
1. **Pseudonymous:** identify the reviewer through a stable public reference, not a legal name or personal contact detail, unless a separate lawful and explicit publication decision is made.
2. **Scope-limited:** state the approved review scope and jurisdiction precisely. Do not imply that a reviewer is qualified for every exercise, discipline or country.
3. **Target-bound:** identify the exact catalogue target and version to which the decision applies.
4. **Decision-specific:** show the decision outcome and its effective date/time, distinguishing accepted, rejected, restricted, suspended, withdrawn, expired and superseded states.
5. **Policy-traceable:** identify the policy/checklist version used and a public-safe review/approval reference.
6. **Evidence-aware:** provide public-safe source references or a concise explanation of the evidence basis where publication is appropriate, without disclosing restricted source material.
7. **Freshness-honest:** state whether the status is current, historically verified, stale or unknown, and when it was last authoritatively checked where meaningful.
8. **Understandable:** explain what the approval does and does not mean in plain language, including that it is not individual medical clearance.
9. **Accessible:** present status and meaning in text, not colour alone; support assistive technologies and clear language.
10. **Consistent:** public status must be derived from the authoritative governance decision history, not manually improvised or inferred from a cached label.

Public traceability should answer: what exact item was reviewed, for what scope, under which policy, what decision was made, when was it made, and what is known about its current status?

## 3. Proposed public information set

The following are candidate public fields for owner review, not an approved schema.

### A. Record identity and target

- Public approval reference.
- Stable catalogue item/exercise reference.
- Exact target version or content digest, using a safe public representation.
- Claim(s) or review scope covered.
- Jurisdiction covered, where relevant.
- Link to the applicable public policy/checklist version.

### B. Decision and lifecycle

- Decision outcome and effective date/time.
- Current lifecycle state: active/current, historically verified, stale, unknown, restricted, suspended, withdrawn, expired or superseded as applicable.
- Public-safe reason category for restriction, withdrawal, expiry or supersession.
- Reference to a superseding or corrected public record where one exists.
- Last authoritative status-check time and freshness meaning.

Do not publish a confidential complaint narrative or imply misconduct from a precautionary restriction. A public-safe status explanation should distinguish a temporary precaution from a final finding.

### C. Reviewer traceability

- Pseudonymous reviewer reference.
- Narrowly approved scope and jurisdiction.
- Verification/appointment status at the time of the decision, expressed without exposing private credential details.
- Whether the required independent review process was satisfied, where this can be disclosed safely.

Do not expose reviewer legal name, home/work contact details, identity documents, registration account numbers, credential scans, private declarations, conflict details or personal circumstances under this proposal.

### D. Evidence and rationale

- Public-safe source citations or identifiers.
- A concise explanation of which claim the evidence supports.
- Material limitations or uncertainty that users need to understand.
- Review protocol/checklist version.

Evidence references must not disclose licensed, confidential, security-sensitive or personal material without authority. Redaction and publication review are required before any evidence excerpt or attachment is exposed.

## 4. Restricted information contract

The following information remains restricted by default and may be accessed only for a documented governance purpose by an authorised role under G-09:

- Legal identity, date of birth, identity-document contents and identity verification artefacts.
- Personal contact details and private correspondence.
- Credential copies, registration identifiers, account details and source-check artefacts not intended for publication.
- Full conflict declarations, financial interests and recusal narratives.
- Complaints, allegations, investigation files, witness details, responses and appeal submissions.
- Sensitive professional, employment, health or other personal information.
- Private reviewer performance notes and non-public appointment deliberations.
- Security logs, authentication information, access tokens, secrets, signing keys and infrastructure details.
- Internal risk assessments, incident response details and provider security reports, except for an authorised disclosure.
- User workout, injury or health-related information, which is not part of the reviewer public record and must not be exposed through this process.

A public record should contain a reference or safe summary only where necessary; it must not embed or mirror the restricted record.

## 5. Public/restricted linkage and authoritative source

The eventual system should distinguish the authoritative restricted governance record from the public projection of approved fields.

Governance requirements:
- Each public approval reference maps to one authoritative decision record and exact target/version.
- Public fields are generated or approved from recorded governance decisions; they are not independently editable facts.
- Changes to the authoritative record produce a traceable public update with an effective time.
- Public and offline copies clearly state their last verification time and freshness status.
- A public record that cannot be reconciled with the authoritative source is stale or unknown, not current.
- Restricted fields never flow into public outputs merely because they share an underlying record or identifier.
- Redaction is performed and checked before publication; access to the private source does not imply permission to publish it.
- Public corrections and superseding decisions retain a safe historical link without exposing restricted content.

The technical mechanism for this separation is not selected here. It must be designed under a separate privacy/security and technical implementation gate.

## 6. Corrections, restrictions and historical traceability

The public view must accurately reflect lifecycle events without silently erasing the past.

- **Correction:** publish the corrected statement with a reason category, effective time and link to the superseded public version where safe. Preserve the prior record as historical, clearly marked as corrected/superseded.
- **Restriction:** show that reliance is temporarily limited and that the restriction is precautionary where appropriate. Do not state or imply a final finding unless one has been made.
- **Suspension:** identify the affected approval/reviewer scope and status without disclosing private allegations or investigation material.
- **Withdrawal/revocation:** show that the approval is no longer current and provide a public-safe reason category and effective time where disclosure is lawful and appropriate.
- **Expiry:** state that the approval or authority period ended and has not been shown as renewed.
- **Supersession:** link to the new target/version or decision while preserving the historical reference.
- **Appeal:** state only the public-safe status of the decision and any effect on current reliance. Do not expose appeal submissions or confidential deliberations.

A later decision must not rewrite the historical fact that a prior version or approval existed. Equally, historical traceability must not mislead users into treating a withdrawn or superseded approval as current.

## 7. Public explanation and user safety

The user-facing explanation should communicate:
- What was reviewed and the exact scope.
- Which version the decision covers.
- What the approval does and does not establish.
- Whether the status is current or only historical.
- Whether any restriction, expiry, withdrawal or superseding decision affects present reliance.
- What the user can do if the status is stale or unknown.

Avoid:
- “Certified safe” or equivalent absolute claims.
- Implying the reviewer has assessed an individual user's health or granted medical clearance.
- Suggesting a public approval is a recommendation for every person or every injury.
- Hiding uncertainty behind a green badge, generic “verified” label or inaccessible colour-only indicator.
- Revealing private complaint or credential information in order to explain a status change.

A suitable plain-language statement may say: “This exact catalogue version was reviewed for the stated scope under the listed policy. The status shown reflects the latest check available at [time]. It is not individual medical clearance.” Final UI copy requires user testing and accessibility review.

## 8. Public availability, offline copies and removal

Public information may be cached or copied by users and third parties. The policy should acknowledge that a correction or withdrawal cannot guarantee erasure of every previously downloaded copy.

Require:
- Clear publication date and last authoritative check time.
- A stable route to the latest public status where connectivity exists.
- Explicit stale/unknown language for offline copies beyond the approved freshness period.
- A process to publish corrections and withdrawals promptly after authorised decisions.
- A method to prevent newly issued records from repeating a withdrawn or superseded approval.
- No false claim that a public deletion removes all copies or changes already completed user workout history.
- A privacy/legal assessment before publishing personal or potentially identifying information.

If a public record must be removed for privacy or legal reasons, retain only the minimum restricted audit history justified under G-09 and applicable law. The public view may use a neutral withdrawal/removal notice without exposing the underlying private reason.

## 9. Accessibility, comprehension and feedback

The public view should:
- Use text labels and clear explanations in addition to icons or colour.
- Identify timestamps with timezone or an unambiguous time convention.
- Avoid unexplained professional acronyms.
- Distinguish the reviewer, the review scope, the catalogue item and the approval decision.
- Make the difference between historical approval and current status easy to understand.
- Offer a safe route to report an apparent error or raise a concern, subject to the future G-08/G-09 operating safeguards.
- Avoid requiring a user to disclose injury or health information to view public trust status.

Accessibility and comprehension testing should include users relying on assistive technology and users with limited technical knowledge. Exact standards and testing plans require a later design decision.

## 10. Publication governance and quality control

Before public release of any trust record:
1. Confirm the decision is authorised and final for the stated publication purpose.
2. Confirm exact target/version, scope, jurisdiction and policy/checklist binding.
3. Confirm reviewer reference and scope are valid for the decision time.
4. Confirm required independent reviews are recorded.
5. Confirm public fields match the authoritative restricted record.
6. Review every field and evidence link for personal, confidential, licensed or security-sensitive information.
7. Confirm lifecycle and freshness wording is accurate.
8. Record who authorised publication and when.
9. Provide a correction/withdrawal path and monitor public status consistency.
10. Preserve the restricted audit history under G-09 and G-11.

A publication check is not a new professional review and cannot cure missing evidence, qualification or authority.

## 11. Boundaries and stop conditions

This proposal does not:
- Approve exact production field names, JSON schemas, API contracts or database structures.
- Authorise publication of any real reviewer identity, credential, conflict or case information.
- Authorise a live reviewer register or production catalogue trust record.
- Decide the lawful basis for publishing any personal information.
- Approve evidence excerpts, licensed materials or third-party content for republication.
- Select a hosting, registry, signing, cryptographic or caching architecture.
- Authorise changes to catalogue, manifest, database, backup, service worker, runtime, C3 or Adaptive Brain.
- Authorise production release, deployment, changes to `main`, Pages settings or the Pages release candidate.

Any actual publication requires G-09 safeguards, G-08 decision authority, approved evidence/qualification processes, a completed privacy/disclosure review and separate technical/publication approval.
