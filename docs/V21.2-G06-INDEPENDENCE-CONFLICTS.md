# V21.2 — G-06 reviewer independence and conflicts of interest

**Status:** FRAMEWORK APPROVED — owner PASS recorded for G-06 in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. This approval covers the governance framework only; operational details and prerequisites remain unresolved as stated below.

**Governance basis:** G-01 project-owner accountability; G-02 Australia-first jurisdiction; G-03 qualification framework; G-04 independent verification; and G-05 finite appointments/rechecks, all approved in principle as recorded in the governance decision register.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve a consistent process requiring reviewers to disclose relevant conflicts before each review, assess independence for the exact decision, and recuse or apply documented safeguards where a conflict could impair impartial judgement or undermine user trust.

A reviewer’s qualification and authorisation do not make them independent for every assignment. Independence must be assessed for each review and each material relationship.

## 2. Core principles

- Reviewers must disclose actual, potential and reasonably perceived conflicts before receiving or deciding the review assignment.
- Conflict assessment is specific to the reviewer, target, evidence, commercial context and decision.
- A material unmanaged conflict disqualifies the reviewer from that review.
- A disclosure is not itself a cure. The governance decision-maker must record whether the conflict is acceptable, mitigable or requires recusal.
- The person whose work or commercial interest is being reviewed must not control the independent review outcome.
- No sole self-approval is permitted.
- High-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance content retains the requirement for two independent appropriately scoped reviews, including at least one reviewer meeting the approved professional qualification standard.
- Independence must be substantive: two signatures are not independent review if one reviewer merely adopts the other's conclusion without separate assessment.

## 3. Conflict categories to assess

The following are examples for assessment, not an automatic finding that every relationship is disqualifying:

### Financial and commercial interests
- direct or indirect financial interest in the exercise, equipment, manufacturer, supplier, programme, guide or claim being reviewed;
- payment, commission, sponsorship, referral benefit, equity, ownership or other material benefit linked to approval;
- paid consultancy, endorsement or commercial partnership involving the subject;
- a financial dependency that could reasonably affect the reviewer’s judgement.

### Personal and organisational relationships
- family, close personal relationship or significant personal dispute with the author, curator, supplier or decision-maker;
- employment, management, supervisory, client, contractor or business relationship with a person or organisation whose work is under review;
- shared ownership, partnership, board membership or other governance relationship;
- competitive or adversarial relationship that could reasonably distort judgement.

### Prior involvement and authorship
- authoring, designing, curating, editing or materially contributing to the exact content or evidence being reviewed;
- having made the disputed decision earlier in the same approval chain;
- reviewing one's own prior work, or work where the reviewer has a strong reputational interest in defending an earlier conclusion.

### Evidence and advocacy
- supplying or commissioning evidence that is central to the conclusion;
- acting as an advocate, expert witness or paid representative for an interested party in the same matter;
- having a material relationship with a source whose claims are being assessed.

### Other relevant circumstances
- gifts, hospitality or benefits beyond any threshold later established by policy;
- pressure, threats, incentives or instructions affecting the outcome;
- any other circumstance a reasonable independent reviewer would want the governance authority to know.

The final policy should define materiality and any quantitative thresholds with appropriate professional/legal input. Do not invent dollar limits or assume that a small payment is always immaterial.

## 4. Disclosure and assessment workflow

For each review assignment:

1. **Assignment notice:** provide the reviewer with the target, author/curator identity where appropriate, evidence sources, commercial context and intended use needed to identify conflicts.
2. **Reviewer declaration:** require a dated declaration covering actual, potential and perceived conflicts, including relevant prior involvement.
3. **Assessment:** an authorised person who is not conflicted determines whether the disclosure is immaterial, manageable with safeguards, or disqualifying.
4. **Record:** retain the declaration outcome, decision rationale, safeguards, recusal or reassignment in the restricted audit record.
5. **Reconfirmation:** require updated disclosure if a new conflict emerges during review or before release.
6. **Release check:** confirm required reviewers remained eligible and independent throughout the relevant review event.

A generic annual declaration is not enough on its own. It may supplement, but cannot replace, assignment-specific disclosure.

## 5. Mitigation versus mandatory recusal

Possible safeguards, only where the conflict is genuinely manageable, may include:
- removing the conflicted person from a particular evidence source or decision step;
- assigning an additional independent reviewer;
- requiring separate evidence gathering and written reasoning;
- separating commercial, curatorial and approval responsibilities;
- transferring the decision to an unconflicted authority.

Safeguards must not be used to disguise a conflict that undermines the review's credibility. Recusal is required where:
- the reviewer authored or materially controlled the exact content and cannot provide an independent assessment;
- a material financial or personal interest could reasonably influence the outcome and cannot be adequately neutralised;
- the reviewer is subject to pressure or has a direct stake in approval;
- independence cannot be demonstrated or the conflict cannot be assessed reliably.

The exact boundaries should be calibrated with professional and legal input where needed.

## 6. Independence for dual review

For high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance metadata, two reviewers must each:
- hold current authorisation for the relevant scope;
- disclose conflicts independently;
- receive the exact target and evidence set;
- conduct their own assessment using the approved protocol;
- record their own rationale, limitations and decision;
- identify disagreements rather than silently harmonising conclusions.

Do not count as independent:
- two signatures on a single shared assessment written by one reviewer;
- a reviewer merely countersigning another person's conclusion;
- two reviewers where one directs or controls the other's decision on that item;
- a second reviewer whose relevant conflict is undisclosed or unmanaged.

Shared employment, common professional networks or use of the same source do not automatically invalidate independence, but must be assessed for actual dependence, control, collusion risk and whether each reviewer reached an independent judgement. Where independence remains uncertain, obtain another reviewer or leave the content unapproved.

## 7. Project-owner and governance conflicts

The project owner remains accountable for policy and governance, but must not:
- approve their own authored or materially curated content as the sole reviewer;
- substitute their judgement for required professional approval;
- direct reviewers to reach a preferred conclusion;
- override a recusal, missing qualification or failed independence requirement;
- decide a complaint or appeal where personally conflicted.

Where the project owner has a material interest in a particular review, the matter must be delegated to an unconflicted authorised decision-maker under a delegation policy approved before operational use. The exact deputy/panel arrangement remains open under G-01.

## 8. Failure and enforcement

- No declaration: no assignment or approval.
- Undisclosed conflict discovered before release: pause the review, remove or reassess the affected reviewer, and obtain an independent review.
- Undisclosed conflict discovered after release: assess the affected approval and release; suspend reliance where the conflict could materially undermine the conclusion.
- Suspected deliberate concealment: consider suspension of reviewer authority and investigation under G-08.
- Disagreement between independent reviewers: do not resolve by majority vote or project-owner override; record the disagreement and escalate to appropriately scoped independent assessment.
- No suitable independent reviewer available: defer the approval. Do not lower the standard to meet a release date.

Preserve original declarations and decisions. Add corrections and outcomes as append-only records.

## 9. Restricted records and privacy

The future restricted governance record should retain only necessary information:
- assignment and target reference;
- declaration date and outcome;
- conflict category and sufficient rationale to audit the decision;
- safeguards, recusal, reassignment or additional review;
- decision-maker and conflict-of-interest assessment;
- subsequent changes and release-impact assessment.

Do not publish private relationship details, financial information or personal allegations in the catalogue. G-09 must determine lawful collection, access, retention, correction and deletion controls before real declarations are collected.

## 10. Open policy details

The following remain for later decisions and qualified input:
- materiality definitions and any financial thresholds;
- which conflicts are always disqualifying versus potentially mitigable;
- exact organisational independence tests for dual reviewers;
- permitted safeguards and who may authorise them;
- project-owner recusal and deputy/panel delegation under G-01;
- investigation and appeal procedures under G-08;
- declaration data, access and retention under G-09.

## 11. Stop conditions

This proposal does not authorise:
- reviewer recruitment, appointment or real conflict declarations;
- credential or personal-data collection;
- production catalogue review or approval;
- a live reviewer register, signing keys or governance service;
- catalogue schema, database, backup, service worker, runtime or UI changes;
- C3, injury-aware matching implementation, main/Pages changes or deployment.
