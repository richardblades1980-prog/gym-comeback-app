## 1. Purpose and status

This brief turns the unresolved professional, governance, Australian privacy/legal and independent-security questions into a structured evidence request. It is intended to help the project owner obtain informed advice and later prepare a source-grounded architecture decision record.

It is **not** a request to choose an architecture now. It does not assert that any particular qualification, legal basis, privacy obligation, security control, provider or cryptographic option is correct. Those conclusions must come from appropriately qualified people reviewing the actual proposed operating model, product scope and evidence.

The project owner has approved governance principles and the architecture-comparison approach only. The owner has **not** approved operational governance, final technical architecture or implementation.

### Explicit limits

This document authorises no:
- contacting, recruiting, appointing, advertising for or contracting professionals or reviewers;
- collection, upload or processing of real identity documents, credentials, conflict declarations or other personal information;
- creation of a live reviewer register, credential-verification service or public register;
- provider selection, procurement, account creation, connection or data transfer;
- generation, import, custody or use of signing keys or trust roots;
- production exercise-guide or catalogue approval;
- code, schema, API, database, backup, service-worker, runtime or test changes;
- publication, release, deployment, Pages configuration change, or change to `main` or the Pages release candidate.

Any external engagement, information collection, procurement, operational setup, architecture selection and implementation requires its own explicit approval and applicable safeguards.

## 2. Project context supplied to advisers

The intended product includes gym and hiking functionality, an exercise library and adaptive programme behaviour. Exercise guidance and substitutions can affect physical activity decisions. Some content may have higher consequence where it makes claims about movement demands, injury relevance, contraindications, progression or recovery.

The project must distinguish:
- editorial preparation from qualified professional review;
- a reviewer's identity from their qualification and authorised scope;
- content integrity from reviewer authentication;
- a cryptographically valid signature from professional competence or clinical correctness;
- historical/offline verification from a claim that authority is currently valid;
- current catalogue trust from access to a user's historical workout records.

The existing codebase has content-digest, catalogue-delivery, registry and runtime validation foundations. These provide integrity and binding checks; they do not currently establish a complete authenticated reviewer trust chain. Existing browser/offline/backup tests are not evidence that professional credential verification, live revocation, privacy operations or an end-to-end signed trust chain have been implemented.

**Safety and data continuity requirement:** missing, stale, withdrawn or unverifiable catalogue trust must not erase, corrupt or block access to historical user workout records. The system must not present unverified substitutions as professionally approved.

## 4. Professional input — qualification, scope and content review

**Required expertise:** appropriately qualified Australian professionals whose current registration, accreditation, experience and scope are relevant to the specific content claims being assessed. The appropriate disciplines and regulators must be determined from the actual claim and intended use; this brief does not prescribe a credential pathway.

### Questions for professional advisers

1. For each proposed review category, what professional discipline(s) are competent to assess the relevant claims, and why?
2. Which authoritative registration, accreditation, licensing or professional-body sources establish current standing in Australia?
3. What evidence is sufficient to verify identity, current status, relevant qualification, experience and any restrictions?
4. What activities fall inside and outside each reviewer's competence and lawful/professional scope?
5. Which content claims require specialist review rather than general exercise-industry review?
6. What criteria distinguish a routine exercise description from higher-consequence content involving injury relevance, contraindications, rehabilitation implications, movement demand or progression?
7. What review standard should apply to each category, including evidence quality, currency, population, limitations and contraindications?
8. When is one review sufficient, and when are two genuinely independent reviews required? What independence criteria apply?
9. What changes to content, evidence, intended population or review protocol require reassessment?
10. What wording and user-facing limitations are appropriate so the app does not imply diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation or individual clinical clearance?
11. What should happen when evidence conflicts, is weak, is outdated or does not cover the intended user group?
12. What events should trigger a reviewer's scope restriction, recheck, suspension or removal, and which authority can determine that?

### Evidence requested

- A scope-by-scope qualification and exclusions matrix, with rationale.
- Official Australian registration/accreditation verification sources and the exact fields/statuses they expose.
- A description of what evidence is and is not sufficient to verify competence for each scope.
- Review standards/checklists for each content-risk category.
- Criteria for independence and dual review, including how conflicts are assessed.
- Content-change and evidence-change triggers for reassessment.
- Clear limits on what professional approval does and does not mean.
- Source links, publication/effective dates, jurisdiction and any known gaps.

### Required output

A versioned **Professional Scope and Evidence Standard** with named accountable discipline(s) for each content category, exclusions, evidence requirements, review depth, independence rules, reassessment triggers and unresolved questions.

Do not request or collect an individual's identity or credential documents as part of this design exercise. Any later verification process needs separate legal/privacy approval, a defined authority and an approved secure handling method.

## 5. Governance input — authority, independence and operations

**Required expertise:** governance, professional-regulation and organisational risk expertise appropriate to the eventual operating model. Legal advice may be required for authority, liability, complaints and disclosure questions.

### Questions for governance advisers and the owner

1. Which entity or role is accountable for the reviewer-governance scheme and its public representations?
2. Who is authorised to verify credentials, appoint a reviewer, define scope, renew authority, restrict scope, suspend or revoke it?
3. What evidence and independent checks must exist before appointment or renewal?
4. How are registrar, credential verifier, reviewer, independent reviewer, release authority, root custodian and auditor duties separated?
5. Which actions require two-person or multi-party approval?
6. What constitutes a material conflict of interest, and how is it disclosed, assessed, mitigated, monitored and recorded?
7. What independence tests prevent self-appointment, self-verification, self-review or unilateral release?
8. What delegation, deputy, absence, incapacity and succession arrangements are acceptable?
9. Who receives complaints, investigates them, determines interim restrictions and communicates outcomes?
10. What review, appeal or reconsideration route exists, who hears it, and how is independence maintained?
11. What events trigger emergency suspension, credential recheck, key revocation or content withdrawal?
12. What audit trail is necessary, who can inspect it, and how are errors corrected without silently rewriting history?
13. What operational capacity, funding, insurance, continuity and incident response are needed to sustain the scheme?
14. Which decisions must remain with the project owner, and which require independent professional or governance authority?

### Evidence requested

- A responsibility and separation-of-duties matrix.
- Appointment, renewal, scope-change, suspension, revocation and exit lifecycle.
- Conflict definitions, thresholds, mitigation and escalation process.
- Complaints, investigation, appeal and withdrawal process.
- Delegation, succession, continuity and emergency decision rules.
- Audit, incident, funding and operational-capacity requirements.
- Explicit unresolved matters and the authority needed to resolve them.

### Required output

A versioned **Governance Operating Model** specifying authority, roles, separation of duties, lifecycle controls, conflicts, complaints/appeals, continuity, audit and funding dependencies. A framework approval alone is not operational readiness.

## 6. Australian privacy and legal input

**Required expertise:** an Australian privacy/legal adviser competent to assess the actual entity, data flows, user base, providers and operating model. This brief is not legal advice and does not assume which privacy laws or regimes apply.

### Questions for the adviser

1. Which legal entities and individuals would be responsible for each collection, use, disclosure, hosting and publication activity?
2. Which Australian privacy, health-information, consumer, professional-regulation, records, surveillance, cybersecurity or other laws may apply, and on what facts?
3. Does the proposed reviewer-governance activity involve personal information, sensitive information or health information? Which fields and purposes create that classification?
4. What lawful basis, consent or other authority is required for each purpose and data flow?
5. What collection notices, privacy policy disclosures, purpose limits and user rights are required?
6. What information may be collected from reviewers, credential authorities, complainants and users, and what is unnecessary?
7. What access, correction, deletion, complaint, disclosure and breach-response rights or duties apply?
8. What retention and destruction periods are required or defensible for identity evidence, credential checks, conflict declarations, review attestations, audit logs and complaints?
9. What limits apply to public disclosure of reviewer names, qualifications, status, scope, evidence references and decision history?
10. What contractual, security, location, subcontractor and cross-border transfer requirements apply to hosting, identity verification, key management, analytics, support and backups?
11. What privacy impact assessment, security assessment, records schedule or regulator consultation is required before operation?
12. How should historical attestations and public records be handled when a reviewer leaves, is suspended, is revoked or requests correction/deletion?
13. What liability, disclaimers, consumer representations and professional responsibility issues arise from describing content as reviewed, verified, approved or safe?
14. What incident and breach notification obligations apply, and who is responsible for decisions and notices?

### Evidence requested

- A written applicability analysis with assumptions and jurisdiction.
- A data inventory and purpose-by-purpose lawful handling analysis.
- A role/controller/service-provider map for the actual entity structure.
- Required notices, consent or other authority and user-rights process.
- A retention/deletion schedule with legal basis for each record class.
- Public-versus-restricted disclosure assessment.
- Provider, hosting-region, subcontractor and cross-border requirements.
- Privacy impact assessment requirements and breach/incident responsibilities.
- Specific advice on exercise, injury and health-related information boundaries.

### Required output

A versioned **Australian Privacy and Legal Requirements Assessment** for the proposed operating model. It must distinguish confirmed legal requirements, risk-management recommendations, assumptions and questions requiring further advice. No real personal or credential data should be collected before the relevant legal/privacy controls and secure process are approved.

## 7. Independent security input — trust architecture and threat assurance

**Required expertise:** an independent security architect or security assessor with experience in browser applications, offline-first systems, cryptographic trust chains, key management, release integrity and privacy-sensitive governance systems. The assessor should be independent of any proposed provider and should disclose relevant conflicts.

### Questions for the assessor

1. What assets, trust boundaries, adversaries and abuse cases must the threat model include?
2. What attacks arise from compromised app releases, malicious catalogue payloads, stolen reviewer keys, registrar compromise, root compromise, insider collusion, rollback, replay, device reset and offline staleness?
3. What trust-root bootstrap and recovery models meet the approved governance constraints?
4. What separation and custody controls are proportionate for reviewer, registrar, release and root keys?
5. Which standards-based signature/envelope formats and algorithms are suitable for the supported browser/device matrix, and what migration path is needed?
6. How should canonical payloads, domain separation, key identifiers, versioning, signature validation and algorithm agility be specified?
7. How should signed reviewer authorisations, exact-target review attestations, status snapshots and catalogue releases be chained and independently verified?
8. What anti-rollback, trusted-time and freshness model is achievable on supported offline devices, including devices that remain offline for extended periods?
9. How should suspension, expiry, revocation, root rotation, emergency recovery and stale cached state behave?
10. What is the minimum safe offline claim, and how must the UI distinguish historically verified, currently verified, stale, unknown and invalid?
11. How should trust state interact with service-worker caches, app updates, backup/restore, device migration and reinstall?
12. What security controls protect restricted identity, credential, conflict, complaint and audit records?
13. What testing, monitoring, incident response, key ceremonies, recovery exercises and independent assurance are required before production?
14. What failure modes must leave historical workout data available and unchanged while disabling only the untrusted catalogue/review function?

### Evidence requested

- A documented threat model and trust-boundary diagram.
- Security requirements and attack/abuse-case register.
- Comparative evaluation of bootstrap, key custody, signature format, registry/revocation, trusted time, hosting and offline models.
- Browser/device compatibility evidence and cryptographic interoperability tests.
- Key lifecycle, role separation, root rotation, compromise and recovery procedures.
- Freshness, anti-rollback and offline-state semantics with explicit limitations.
- Service-worker/cache/backup/restore security analysis.
- Independent review findings, severity, remediation and retest criteria.
- A test plan covering tampering, stale data, revoked authority, missing trust, rollback, reset, offline operation and data preservation.

### Required output

A versioned **Independent Security Architecture Assessment** that recommends options only within the professional, governance and legal constraints already established. It must state residual risks, assumptions, rejected alternatives, operational dependencies and conditions that would invalidate the recommendation.

## 8. Cross-disciplinary decision questions

These questions must be resolved jointly because no single discipline can answer them alone:

| Decision topic | Professional input | Governance input | Legal/privacy input | Security input |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reviewer identity and qualification | What proves competence for this scope? | Who verifies and grants authority? | What may be collected and retained? | How is proof protected and bound to an identity? |
| Scope and independence | What work is within competence? | Who assigns scope and handles conflicts? | What representations/liability arise? | How are scope and role enforced in signed records? |
| Review evidence and attestations | What evidence supports the conclusion? | Who approves and audits the decision? | What evidence can be disclosed? | How is exact target/evidence binding protected? |
| Suspension and revocation | What professional events require action? | Who decides and appeals? | What records/disclosures must persist? | How quickly can status propagate and how is offline staleness shown? |
| Public traceability | What qualifications/status may be meaningful? | Who authorises publication? | What disclosure is lawful and proportionate? | How is public data separated from restricted records? |
| Offline trust | What claim can safely be made when status is old? | What freshness risk is acceptable? | What user disclosure is required? | What anti-rollback and trusted-time guarantees are feasible? |
| Continuity and recovery | How is review authority preserved safely? | Who succeeds a missing authority? | What records must be retained? | How are roots/keys recovered without weakening trust? |

If any row has unresolved material inputs, the corresponding architecture choice remains blocked or explicitly conditional.

## 12. Subsequent gates — separate owner decisions

1. **Input acquisition gate:** separately authorise any external engagement, adviser selection, terms, information exchange or costs. This brief itself does not authorise contact.
2. **Evidence sufficiency gate:** review returned evidence, resolve conflicts and mark remaining blockers. Do not select architecture while material prerequisites remain unresolved.
3. **Final architecture decision gate:** present a source-linked architecture decision record and obtain an explicit owner decision. This may approve a design direction only.
4. **Implementation proposal gate:** prepare a separate, scoped implementation plan, threat-informed tests, migration/rollback and data-preservation plan. Obtain explicit approval before code or operational changes.
5. **Independent verification gate:** independently verify the exact implementation and operational controls before any production trust claims.
6. **Production/operations gate:** separately approve live providers, key ceremonies, personal-data handling, reviewer appointments, production content approvals, publication and deployment.

No stage inherits permission for the next stage.

## 13. Non-negotiable product and data safeguards

- Missing, invalid, stale, conflicting, revoked or out-of-scope trust must fail closed for the affected review-dependent catalogue behaviour.
- The app must never describe a digest as a reviewer signature or a signature as proof of professional competence.
- An offline device must not claim current reviewer authority when it cannot establish freshness.
- Manual substitutions must not be represented as catalogue-verified or professionally approved unless the full approved trust chain supports that claim.
- Injury-aware matching and other higher-consequence recommendations remain blocked until their separate professional, governance and technical requirements are satisfied.
- Historical workouts and user-owned records must remain accessible and unchanged if catalogue trust is absent, stale, restricted, withdrawn or invalid.
- Restricted identity, credential, conflict, complaint, investigation and security information must not leak into public catalogue data or ordinary application backups.
- No provider, cryptographic algorithm, trust root, freshness interval, retention period or legal conclusion is selected by this brief.
