# Adaptive Brain — historical rules audit and V21.2 design boundary

**Audit date:** 19 November 2026  
**Audited branch:** `V21.2-development`  
**Audited HEAD:** `cd782a72aacc1b80186e6dd27c870a57fc42e9c1`  
**Status:** discovery and design evidence only; no adaptive behaviour is approved or implemented by this document.

## 1. Executive finding

The archived material describes two related but distinct systems. They should not be collapsed into one opaque “readiness score”:

1. **Daily readiness and recovery context** — a dated self-report about energy, fatigue, soreness and symptoms, intended to guide the overall training approach for that day.
2. **Exercise-specific progression review** — a comparison of the same exercise across previous sessions, using repetitions, load, reps in reserve (RIR) and symptom response to decide whether to build consistency, hold, review a lower-performance session or consider progression.

V21.2 currently implements neither live recommendation loop. It has the workout data, programme progression and descriptive history foundations needed to design them safely.

Historical thresholds are records of prior product decisions, not validated clinical rules and not automatic approval to reuse them.

## 2. Historical rules recovered

### V10 — Smart Progression

The V10 release note records:
- RIR for the final set and a symptom response of better/no symptoms, same/manageable or worse/aggravated.
- Repetitions are prioritised before load.
- A small load increase is considered only when all three sets reach 12 reps, RIR is at least 2, and symptoms are stable or better.
- Worsening symptoms override progression and lead to hold/reduce or movement modification.
- Suggestions are advisory; values and workout plans are not automatically changed.

The archived V10 package was described as prepared for beta testing, not as a fully deployed or device-validated rule set.

### V19.4 — Exercise-by-exercise Adaptive Training Review

The V19.4 release note refines the review:
- Review one selected workout day exercise by exercise.
- Use up to the three most recent saved sessions for the same exercise and day.
- Consider reps, total reps, load, RIR and symptom/body-response feedback.
- Distinguish building consistency, maintaining load, reviewing a lower-performance session and considering progression after repeated strong sessions.
- Show a needs-data state when history is absent rather than inventing advice.
- The progression trigger is documented as two qualifying sessions, each reaching 3 sets of 12 or more reps, at least 2 RIR and stable/better symptoms.
- Worsening symptom feedback always blocks progression.
- Recommendations remain explanatory and advisory.

This is the strongest historical source for exercise-specific comparison, but it still does not define a complete reduction algorithm or validated rules for every exercise configuration.

### V20 — Daily readiness foundation

The V20 note records a separate daily check-in:
- Energy: 1–5.
- General fatigue: 1–5.
- Muscle soreness: 1–5.
- Pain/symptoms: 0–10.

Its historical guidance is:
- Symptoms 7–10: pause aggravating activity and seek prompt qualified advice.
- Symptoms 4–6: ease back and review symptoms; avoid movements that increase them.
- Low energy, high fatigue or high soreness: recovery focus; consider an easier session or more recovery.
- Average check-in: steady pace and familiar work, without pushing progression.
- Favourable check-in: follow the established planned session with controlled technique.

The V20 note explicitly says the check-in is not a medical assessment, diagnosis or medical clearance; a favourable score is not permission to train through pain. It also says V20 does not use a multi-day recovery trend or automatically modify a plan.

These bands must be reviewed before reuse. They are not a diagnosis, clinical triage protocol or proof that a particular activity is safe.

### V11 — Injury-aware alternatives

V11 separately records a 7/10 pain prompt before showing alternatives, and caution-ranking based on the selected restriction area, target muscles and equipment. That rule belongs to the older exercise-substitution flow. It must not silently become the threshold or behaviour for the V20 daily readiness system.

### V20.7 — Historical linked-set carry-forward

V20.7 describes linked values flowing from Set 1 into later sets, with a manually changed later set potentially becoming the source for subsequent linked sets.

That cascade is **superseded for V21.2** by the owner's explicit Set 1-only rule. V21.2 has one automatic source per exercise: Set 1. A later manual edit is independently user-owned and does not flow into following sets.

## 3. Current V21.2 source and data reality

Audited source files and contracts:

- `src/modules/gym-progression.js` manages the 4/8/12-week plan, 2–5 planned days, sequential unlocks, manual unlocks, history ordering and descriptive exercise-history summaries. It is not an adaptive recommendation engine.
- `src/modules/gym-workout.js` implements persisted workouts, previous-week carry-forward by immutable programme slot and set number, Set 1 autofill, per-field ownership markers (`first-set` / `user`), set locking, rest and completion. It does not capture RIR or exercise symptom-response feedback.
- `v21.2/app.js` shows descriptive completed-workout history and a notice when a schedule item already has readiness data. It does not expose a live readiness check-in or Adaptive Brain proposal/apply interface.
- `src/progress/assessment-manager.js` manages immutable body-measurement assessments, not daily training readiness.
- `src/data/database.js` already defines a `readiness` store indexed by date and a `progression` store indexed by module. The Gym plan and unlock settings currently live in `preferences`.
- `src/data/backup.js` includes readiness, progression, preferences, sessions, exercise records and set records in its existing full backup store sets. The backup writer reads complete store records and restore writes those records back.
- `src/data/shared-migration.js` projects the legacy `gym-comeback-readiness` date-keyed values into the existing readiness store while retaining the original key/raw value for auditability.

The existing readiness store is a credible candidate for daily check-in records. Its exact V20-compatible record contract should be verified before reuse. The exercise-specific RIR and symptom-response location is not yet decided. Do not create a new store or change the IndexedDB version by assumption.

The current five-day programme retains 32 distinct source slot IDs. Those IDs identify programme positions, not verified canonical exercise definitions. Historical comparisons must use exact source/programme context and must not invent cross-slot identity from similar display names. Manual substitutions must not be treated as performance on the original movement.

## 4. Current owner-approved behaviour that governs any future advice

The following V21.2 rules are current and take precedence over older archived behaviour:

- A recommendation must notify the user and explain why it is being made.
- Show the exact before/after proposal for the affected value(s).
- Provide explicit **Apply / OK** and **Keep current values / Dismiss** actions.
- Merely displaying a proposal never changes a workout record.
- Applying may change Set 1 and only later fields still marked as automatic Set 1 defaults.
- User-owned fields remain untouched.
- Started or completed sets remain untouched.
- Set 1 remains the only source for within-exercise auto-population. Editing Set 2 does not cascade to Set 3.
- Worsening symptom feedback blocks progression advice.
- No decrease thresholds or additional clinical rules may be invented.


## Owner requirement — persistent injury-aware exercise alternatives (2026-11-19)

A user-reported injury or affected body area must change the Adaptive Brain's exercise recommendations across the programme, not merely add a warning to the individual exercise card.

**Example: a user reports a pulled calf muscle.**

1. Record an active, user-reported restriction for the affected area. The app must not diagnose the injury or infer a recovery date.
2. Review current and upcoming programme slots against reviewed exercise metadata. Consider direct muscle involvement and secondary loading such as stabilisation, balance, foot pressure, propulsion, impact and setup/bracing. A movement is not suitable merely because "calf" is not listed as its primary muscle.
3. Offer an appropriate reviewed alternative only when its movement and equipment requirements are sufficiently known and it does not materially load the restricted area to the best of the reviewed information.
4. Give the user explicit choices: **Swap** the exercise, **Remove/skip** it from the workout, or **Keep it restricted** and leave it out for now. The original programme slot and history remain intact.
5. Keep the restriction active across future sessions until the user explicitly updates or clears it. A favourable daily readiness check-in must never silently clear an injury restriction.
6. When the user says they are ready to resume the original programme/exercise, request an explicit confirmation and current symptom update. This is a user-controlled app state only; it is not medical clearance. Ongoing, worsening or concerning symptoms should prompt the app to advise the user to stop aggravating activity and seek appropriate qualified assessment.
7. If no sufficiently reviewed alternative exists, do not invent one or guess. Offer **Remove/skip** or keep the affected exercise restricted until reviewed information is available.

**No zero-effect promise:** the app must not claim an alternative cannot affect the injured area. It can describe known movement demands and aim to avoid or minimise loading, but cannot establish medical safety for an individual.

### Required exercise-library metadata

The library must support more than primary target muscles and equipment labels. Candidate fields for reviewed curation include:
- primary and secondary muscles / body regions;
- stabilisation and balance demands;
- joint actions and movement pattern;
- standing/seated/lying position and foot-contact requirements;
- impact, propulsion and weight-bearing characteristics;
- equipment requirements and relevant machine configuration;
- known limitations, cautions and the evidence/reviewer status for those attributes.

These attributes must be grounded in reviewed exercise information. Do not create injury-exclusion rules from an unverified exercise name, image, fuzzy match or AI-generated guess.

### Approval and preservation rules

- The injury restriction is a safety constraint, not a progression suggestion. It takes precedence over progression, workout completion convenience and a favourable readiness score.
- A swap or removal requires explicit user approval. Applying it must not overwrite the protected source programme slot, old session history or unrelated user-owned set values.
- Make the scope visible: current session versus active restriction across future sessions. Do not silently turn a one-session swap into a permanent programme edit.
- Reassess the restriction only through an explicit user action. Never clear it automatically because time has passed or a workout was completed.

## 5. Decisions still required before implementation

1. **Separate daily and exercise-specific inputs.** Confirm which fields belong to a date-level readiness check-in and which belong to an individual performed exercise/session. Decide whether RIR is captured for the last set as in V10/V19.4, and how symptom feedback is recorded without conflating general readiness with a local movement response.
2. **Reconcile historical thresholds with V21.2 configuration.** The historical 3 × 12 and 2+ RIR trigger assumes three sets. V21.2 allows configurable set counts and different progression focuses. Do not silently apply the historical trigger to 1, 2, 4 or more sets, timed work, or a manual progression focus.
3. **Define the reduction/recovery path.** The archives say hold/reduce, ease back or recovery focus, but do not establish a complete validated algorithm for a load, repetition, target-duration or set-count reduction. This is a design gap, not permission to invent numbers.
4. **Set symptom precedence.** Specify which input wins when daily readiness is favourable but an exercise response is worse, or vice versa. Worsening local symptoms must not be neutralised by a favourable daily score.
5. **Define injury and alternative eligibility.** Decide the body-area restriction model, affected-side detail, direct/secondary loading taxonomy, review evidence required before an alternative can be offered, and the exact user-controlled clear/resume flow. The persistent injury requirement above is mandatory; a missing or unreviewed movement-impact classification must fail closed to skip/remove rather than guess.
6. **Define history eligibility.** Confirm completed versus incomplete sessions, number and recency of records, same-day duplicates, set-count changes, missing values, manual substitutions, programme-source changes and dated records.
7. **Define stale-proposal handling.** A proposal must be tied to the exact values and set ownership state shown to the user. If relevant values change before Apply, the app must not apply a stale proposal over newer user input.
8. **Define units and exercise modes.** Weight units, bodyweight/no-load exercises, repetition targets, timed-hold target duration and actual measured duration must remain distinct. Actual duration is not a Set 1 target default.

## 6. Recommended architectural direction

Keep the existing `gym-progression.js` focused on programme sequencing and descriptive history. If the user approves implementation, add a separately named Adaptive Brain service (for example, `gym-adaptive.js`) with its own decision functions and tests rather than turning programme unlock logic into a combined coaching engine.

Treat daily readiness and exercise-specific progression as separate inputs with explicit precedence. Reuse the existing readiness store only after confirming its field contract and backup/migration behaviour. Choose the exercise-response storage shape only after the ownership, history and backup requirements above are decided.

Any persisted exercise-response fields or new recommendation records require the applicable evolution gate, backup/restore coverage and historical compatibility tests even if they can technically fit inside an existing IndexedDB store.

## 7. Required validation before a live recommendation can be enabled

At minimum, tests should cover:
- No history and insufficient history produce needs-data/no-progression output.
- Only the exact same source programme slot and context are compared; substitutions do not contaminate original-exercise trends.
- Incomplete sessions, missing values, changed set counts, timed exercises and invalid units are handled explicitly.
- Worsening symptoms block progression regardless of otherwise strong performance or favourable daily readiness.
- An active body-area restriction blocks proposals for movements with known direct or secondary loading of that area; unresolved movement metadata does not qualify an alternative.
- Dismiss leaves all persisted session, exercise and set values unchanged.
- Apply changes only the exact approved proposal, and only eligible Set 1 / automatic fields.
- User-owned values and started/completed sets are unchanged.
- A stale proposal is rejected or refreshed if its source values/ownership have changed.
- The `first-set` and `user` field-origin metadata survives backup and restore.
- Readiness and response data survive reload, cold restart, backup and restore, and any supported legacy migration.
- Browser and backup workflows pass on the exact final commit SHA.

## 8. Scope boundary

This audit does not implement readiness capture, recommendation logic, automatic increases/decreases, clinical thresholds, a new data store, a schema upgrade, a backup-format change, an exercise-identity assignment, a merge or a deployment.

**Recommended next step:** complete the equipment and exercise-library selector audit/design first, including reviewed movement-demand metadata and the injury-aware alternative contract above. Then prepare the bounded Adaptive Brain decision/design gate. Do not implement the recommendation engine until both foundations and the decision rules are explicitly reviewed.


## Post-audit timed-set carry-forward correction — 2026-11-19

A focused source review found the programme-week prefill was copying the actual measured `duration` field while omitting `targetDuration`. This contradicted the approved V21.2 rule that a timed exercise may carry its target forward but must not carry its prior actual measured time as a new target.

- Corrected `findPreviousWeekValues` to recognise `targetDuration` as a carry-forward value.
- Corrected the exact-slot / same-set copy list to include `targetDuration` and exclude actual `duration`.
- Retained within-exercise Set 1 autofill for `targetDuration`; actual measured duration remains independent.
- Added a regression proving the next week receives the target, leaves actual duration blank, and does not alter the prior session.
- Canonical and offline engine mirrors are identical. No schema, backup format, cache, migration or existing record was changed.

Implementation and test commit: `69d337c9cc30044637f01f4ea7d59e51c58c9d36`. Exact-SHA browser and backup workflow results must be checked before this correction is considered validated.
