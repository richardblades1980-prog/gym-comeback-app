## 1. Executive finding

The technical catalogue foundation is ready for controlled curation, but production content is not ready to be created or published.

The live source has a structural review-binding validator. It requires a review-record ID, reviewer-scope ID, approved decision, target SHA-256 digest and non-empty evidence references. It recomputes the target digest. It does **not** authenticate the reviewer, verify qualifications, establish that the reviewer holds the claimed scope, check whether that scope is current, or verify that the cited evidence was actually reviewed.

Therefore, a payload containing `decision: "approved"` and plausible IDs/evidence strings is not proof of qualified approval. A digest proves which content was bound to a review record; it does not prove who reviewed it or whether they were qualified.

**Release rule:** no production catalogue item may be marked approved, and no production catalogue release may be published, until both (a) content curation is complete and (b) a trusted, auditable reviewer-qualification and approval process has been established and evidenced.

## 3. Separate review scopes — no universal reviewer badge

A reviewer must be approved for the specific decision being made. One qualification must not automatically confer authority over every catalogue field.

Proposed review scopes:

1. **Exercise identity and execution:** canonical identity, whether labels describe the same movement, meaningful variations, setup, execution instructions and guide accuracy.
2. **Equipment identity and configuration:** equipment family boundaries, loading system, movement mechanics, orientation, attachments, fit/setup attributes and equipment requirement alternatives.
3. **Movement-demand and injury-relevance metadata:** direct and secondary involvement, joint actions, support, stabilisation, balance, foot contact/pressure, weight-bearing, impact, propulsion, positioning and bracing. This scope requires a suitably qualified professional whose current registration/credential and scope of practice cover the specific review.
4. **Programme-slot identity:** mapping a preserved source slot to a reviewed definition/variation, including confirmation that the mapping does not rewrite historical meaning.
5. **Guide/instruction content:** accuracy and completeness of user-facing instructions, cautions and setup steps within the reviewer's competence.

A reviewer may hold one or more scopes, but each approval must name the scope actually exercised. The project must define and document acceptable credential types and verification methods for each scope before inviting production review. Do not invent a credential threshold or treat a course completion badge, job title, self-declaration or AI output as proof of professional authority.

For injury-relevance metadata, the project should require review by a currently credentialed professional with appropriate expertise for the movement/loading questions being approved. If the reviewer cannot assess a field within their scope, that field remains incomplete/unknown and must not be represented as cleared.

## 4. Trusted reviewer register and evidence

### 4.1 Controlled reviewer register

Maintain a controlled reviewer register outside the public catalogue payload. Each reviewer entry should contain, at minimum:

- stable reviewer identity/reference;
- identity-verification method and verification date;
- credential/registration type and issuing authority;
- registration or credential reference, held in the restricted register rather than public catalogue content;
- independent verification source and verification date;
- approved review scopes and any explicit exclusions;
- jurisdiction and scope limitations where relevant;
- credential status, expiry/recheck date and revocation/suspension state;
- who authorised the reviewer and when.

Access to credential evidence must be restricted to those who need to verify it. Public catalogue data should expose only the minimum reviewer reference, scope, decision and review-record linkage needed for traceability; it must not publish unnecessary personal or credential information.

### 4.2 Approval record

Each review event should be immutable and linked to the exact reviewed target. The controlled record should include:

- unique review-record ID;
- reviewer identity resolved through the controlled register;
- exact reviewer scope;
- exact target type, stable target ID and target digest;
- exact evidence set, including source/version/page or equivalent locator where available;
- review protocol/checklist version;
- decision: approved, rejected, or returned for revision;
- decision timestamp and reviewer attestation;
- any limitations, unresolved fields or required follow-up;
- supersession/revocation link if the content or approval is later withdrawn.

A review approval applies only to the exact digest and declared scope. Any content edit that changes the digest invalidates that approval for the changed target. An approval must not silently transfer to a new exercise variation, equipment configuration, guide or programme-slot mapping.

### 4.3 Authentication is a separate technical requirement

The existing catalogue loader performs structural and digest validation only. It does not consult a trusted reviewer register. The design must not claim otherwise.

Before production publication, a separate trust-boundary decision must select and validate a machine-enforceable way to establish that review bindings came from authorised reviewers and that the approval scope was valid at approval time. Options to evaluate include a controlled release-signing/attestation process or a tightly controlled approval service with verifiable output. The selected approach must account for key/credential rotation, revocation, audit history, offline verification and failure behaviour.

A private spreadsheet or manually maintained list may support human governance, but by itself it does not make the current client-side `validateReviewBinding` an authenticated reviewer validator. If the chosen design remains human-only, the product and release process must explicitly state that limitation and must not present the payload as cryptographically/authentically reviewer-verified.

This trust mechanism is **not** authorised by this design document. It requires a separate scoped technical gate before implementation. The existing C1/G2 exclusions around publisher authentication and digital signatures are not silently reopened here.

## 5. Curation workflow

Use the following lifecycle for each candidate. A row may remain blocked indefinitely; completion percentages must never pressure reviewers to guess.

1. **Source captured:** preserve exact legacy label, source location, legacy metadata and original programme-slot references.
2. **Identity triage:** decide whether the row is one exercise/equipment concept, a family with explicit variations, or multiple distinct concepts. Record unresolved questions; do not mint IDs yet.
3. **Evidence assembled:** collect reliable source material for identity, execution, equipment configuration and relevant movement demands. Record exact references and limitations.
4. **Draft prepared:** a curator prepares a proposed record and identifies every field requiring expert review. AI may help organise evidence or flag inconsistencies but cannot approve, attest or fill unknowns as facts.
5. **Scoped review:** each reviewer reviews only fields within their approved scope and records a decision against the exact digest.
6. **Independent check:** a second appropriately scoped reviewer checks high-consequence movement-demand/injury-relevance content and any material identity/configuration ambiguity. The project must define the required independence rule in the reviewer-process gate.
7. **Release readiness:** validate every reference, digest, reviewer scope, evidence locator, required field, equipment relationship and programme mapping. Any failed or missing check blocks publication.
8. **Immutable release:** only after all required approvals and trust checks pass may canonical IDs and approved records enter a versioned release. Changes create a new reviewed digest/release; never edit a published release in place.
9. **Ongoing maintenance:** monitor credential expiry/revocation, source corrections and safety-relevant feedback. Affected approvals are suspended or superseded; the prior audit trail remains.

## 6. Minimum content review requirements

### 6.1 Exercise definitions and variations

For every exercise/variation, review and record:

- canonical identity and clear distinction from similar movements;
- whether it is a definition, meaningful variation or separate exercise;
- exact execution/setup and support points;
- body position, laterality, range and movement pattern;
- primary, secondary and other relevant muscle/body-region involvement, with clear semantics;
- joint actions, stabilisation and balance demands;
- foot contact/pressure, ankle/calf contribution, weight-bearing, impact/landing and propulsion;
- bracing and other relevant secondary loading;
- equipment requirements using explicit AND/OR group semantics and supported configurations;
- guide availability and any cautions/limitations;
- evidence provenance, review scope and completeness.

An empty metadata array means “reviewed absent” only when the field is explicitly in scope, the complete profile is present, and an authorised reviewer has approved that exact content. Missing data means unknown.

### 6.2 Equipment definitions

For every proposed equipment definition, review:

- whether the concept is distinct or should be a configuration of another definition;
- canonical name, family and scope boundaries;
- loading/resistance system independently from movement mechanics;
- orientation/body position independently from loading system;
- supported attachments, seat/pad/pivot/handle and fit/setup dimensions;
- source evidence and known limitations.

The owner's Gym has both pin-selected and plate-loaded equipment. This does not identify the loading system, geometry or mechanics of any individual machine. Individual inventory configuration remains user-confirmed; it must not be inferred from the general Gym description, name, picture or another machine.

### 6.3 Original programme slots

Keep all 32 original slot IDs and source labels unchanged. For each slot, record one of:

- exact reviewed match;
- reviewed mapping to a distinct canonical definition/variation, with rationale;
- intentionally unresolved / no safe identity match.

A mapping requires its own evidence and review. Similar wording alone is insufficient. The ten non-exact optional-library labels need explicit identity work; repeated labels remain separate slot targets. Historical session and set records must continue to display their original snapshots.

### 6.4 Guides

The 15 existing exercise-specific legacy descriptions and 27 generic-guide gaps are source material only. They must be reviewed and rewritten/approved as needed. A legacy description, generic fallback, illustration or generated text is not a reviewed guide. A guide's approval is separate from approval of exercise identity or movement-demand metadata.

## 7. Injury-aware matching: fail closed

The user's reported restriction is not a diagnosis and does not permit the app to infer healing or a safe return date.

For a reported calf restriction, for example, the reviewer must assess more than the primary-muscle list: secondary calf involvement, foot pressure, balance/stabilisation, propulsion, landing/impact, weight-bearing, position and bracing can all matter. The app must not claim that a suggested alternative has zero effect on the restricted area or constitutes medical clearance.

Candidate states remain:

- **Known conflict:** exclude the exercise as an alternative.
- **Unknown/incomplete:** fail closed; do not suggest it.
- **Reviewed candidate:** may be offered for the user's consideration with clear limitations, not a safety guarantee.

If no reviewed candidate exists, offer skip/remove or keep restricted rather than guessing. Persistent user restrictions, their lifecycle and backup coverage remain a separate C3 gate and are not part of this work.

## 8. Proposed execution order

1. **Approve this governance design** — no code or production content.
2. **Define the trust/authentication gate** — specify how reviewer identity, scope, approval, revocation and evidence are verifiably enforced; assess whether a new trust-boundary gate is needed.
3. **Define the reviewer qualification policy** — scope-specific criteria, verification sources, independence and re-review rules.
4. **Run a small pilot on a few representative records** — one straightforward bodyweight movement, one ambiguous movement, one machine definition and one movement-demand profile. Pilot records remain private/non-production until the trust gate passes.
5. **Resolve all 44 equipment candidates** — split/merge/retain decisions with evidence before issuing IDs.
6. **Curate all 42 exercise candidates and their meaningful variations** — including reviewed equipment requirements, movement-demand profiles and guides.
7. **Resolve all 32 programme slots** — exact match, reviewed mapping or intentionally unresolved.
8. **Run independent review and release-readiness checks** — no partial/unknown production approval.
9. **Only then consider the production catalogue release and later UI gates.**

The Equipment Manager and Exercise Library screens should consume reviewed catalogue content; they should not be used as a substitute for curation or reviewer governance. C3 injury restrictions remain separate.

## 9. Explicit exclusions and safety boundaries

This proposal does not authorise:

- production catalogue payloads, canonical IDs, equipment definitions or exercise/variation records;
- any approval or reviewer credential claims;
- programme-slot assignments, aliases or guide publication;
- changes to `catalogue-delivery.js`, its review-binding validator or payload schema;
- a reviewer service, signing keys, digital signatures, remote publishing or new trust infrastructure;
- database schema/store/index changes or backup-format changes;
- C3 restriction storage/logic, Adaptive Brain changes or injury-aware runtime recommendations;
- Equipment Manager, Exercise Library or Programme Builder UI;
- changes to `main`, the Pages release candidate, Pages settings, deployment or production cutover.
