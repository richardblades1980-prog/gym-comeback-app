# V21.2 — G-11 audit, continuity and funding

**Status:** DESIGN PROPOSAL — owner decision required. This document proposes governance principles for maintaining, auditing, funding and sustaining a future reviewer-trust process. It does not appoint an auditor or operator, commit funds, select a provider, create an operational service, or authorise technical implementation.

**Governance basis:** G-01 through G-10 owner directions/framework approvals recorded in `docs/V21.2-REVIEWER-TRUST-GOVERNANCE-DECISION-REGISTER.md`. In particular, accountability remains with the project owner; specialist review and decision functions must remain appropriately independent; G-08 requires traceable decisions; G-09 requires privacy-protective records handling; and G-10 requires unknown or stale trust status never be presented as current.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve the governance principles required to keep the reviewer-trust process accountable, independently auditable, funded, recoverable and safe to pause or exit.

The project must not rely on an unfunded, unstaffed or unavailable governance process while continuing to imply that approvals are current. A service outage, loss of a provider, unavailable reviewer, exhausted budget or loss of key custody must not silently weaken verification, review or safety requirements.

The policy owner remains accountable for ensuring that the process has a named operational owner, adequate resources, independent audit arrangements, incident response and a documented continuity/exit plan before production reliance.

## 2. Accountability and separation of responsibilities

Before operation, approve a responsibility and delegation matrix that identifies, for each activity:
- The accountable policy owner.
- The operational process owner and deputy/succession contact.
- The person or role responsible for reviewer identity/credential verification.
- Reviewer appointment and scope-authorisation authority.
- Review decision and independent dual-review responsibilities.
- Complaint/investigation and appeal responsibilities.
- Privacy and security responsibilities.
- Audit responsibility and independence safeguards.
- Service/provider management and financial approval.
- Incident command, communications and service suspension authority.

The matrix should identify who performs, approves, is consulted on and is informed about each activity. Avoid assigning incompatible duties to one person where independence is material.

Minimum separation principles:
1. A reviewer must not be the sole approver of their own work.
2. A person should not investigate their own disputed decision or determine an appeal against their own decision.
3. A person who administers access or infrastructure does not thereby gain authority to make professional review decisions.
4. The project owner retains policy accountability but cannot override missing or adverse specialist professional approval.
5. An auditor should not audit work for which they were materially responsible without independent safeguards and transparent limitations.
6. No one person should be able to appoint themselves, verify themselves, approve their own work, alter the record and remove the audit history.

Where a small team makes full separation impracticable, document the specific conflict, compensating independent review, scope and limits. Do not describe a conflicted arrangement as independent.

## 3. Operational readiness and minimum resourcing

Before production reliance, prepare an operating plan that identifies the minimum people, competence, time, tools and funding required for each approved scope and function.

The plan should cover:
- Workload and realistic review capacity by scope.
- Independent specialist availability, including the high-consequence dual-review requirement.
- Credential verification and scheduled/event-triggered rechecks.
- Conflict management and case investigation capacity.
- Privacy, security, record management and audit effort.
- User communication, corrections and withdrawal handling.
- Provider costs, renewal dates and contingency reserves.
- Training, handover, leave cover and succession.
- Monitoring of overdue reviews, expired authority and unresolved incidents.

Do not accept review volumes that cannot be independently and competently assessed. A backlog, budget pressure or launch deadline is not a reason to waive qualification, evidence or dual-review requirements.

If capacity is insufficient, narrow the supported scope, pause new approvals or suspend reliance on affected records until capacity is restored. Preserve historical workout data.

## 4. Funding and financial controls

The operating model should identify the expected cost categories and who authorises expenditure. It should not depend on undisclosed or uncommitted volunteer labour for safety-critical tasks.

Consider and document:
- Credential/identity verification fees and rechecks.
- Professional reviewer and independent second-review costs.
- Privacy/legal advice and specialist consultation.
- Audit and security assessment costs.
- Hosting, storage, backup, monitoring and provider support.
- Incident response, data export, migration and service exit.
- Training, documentation and continuity cover.

Before operation, establish:
- A named budget owner and approval limits.
- A realistic recurring budget and renewal calendar.
- Funding for independent audit, not only routine operations.
- A contingency for provider failure, incident response and orderly service exit.
- Transparent handling of sponsorship, donations or commercial relationships.
- A prohibition on financial sponsors influencing reviewer findings, appointment outcomes, evidence conclusions or appeal decisions.

If funding becomes inadequate, record the impact and trigger a controlled scope reduction or pause. Do not conceal an unfunded control or continue to claim that it is operating.

This proposal does not select a funding model, commit the project to expenditure or authorise commercial partnerships.

## 5. Independent audit and assurance

The future process should have a documented audit programme proportionate to risk. Audit scope and frequency must be set after the operating model and risk assessment are known; no arbitrary universal cadence is adopted here.

Audit should assess both design and actual operation, including:
- Correct reviewer scope, credential verification and recheck status.
- Independence, conflict declarations and dual-review integrity.
- Evidence quality, target/version binding and decision rationale.
- Timeliness and correctness of restrictions, suspensions, withdrawals and appeals.
- Public status accuracy and offline freshness claims.
- Access controls, privileged actions and privacy handling.
- Retention, disposal and incident response.
- Backup/restore, service continuity and provider exit readiness.
- Whether historical user workout data remains intact and accessible.
- Whether unresolved risks, exceptions and corrective actions are visible to the owner.

Auditors should have appropriate competence, defined access, confidentiality obligations and independence from the work being audited. Use read-only, time-bounded access where practicable and apply G-09 data minimisation.

Audit reports should distinguish:
- Scope and period examined.
- Evidence sampled and limitations.
- Findings and risk severity.
- Confirmed nonconformities versus recommendations.
- Responsible action owner and due date.
- Closure evidence and independent verification where required.

An audit is not a substitute for a qualified professional review of an individual exercise or claim. A clean audit sample does not prove every approval is correct or medically safe.

## 6. Risk, incidents and corrective action

Maintain a proportionate risk and corrective-action process that records:
- The risk or incident, affected scope and potential user impact.
- Severity, likelihood and uncertainty.
- Immediate containment or restrictions.
- Accountable owner and required specialist input.
- Root-cause analysis where appropriate.
- Corrective and preventive actions, owners and due dates.
- Effectiveness checks and closure decision.
- Links to related approvals, complaints, withdrawals or audit findings.

Escalate material risks to the accountable owner and relevant independent specialist. Use G-08 for review disputes, restrictions, investigations and appeals; use G-09 for privacy incidents and personal-data handling; use G-10 for freshness and offline status consequences.

Do not close an incident merely because the immediate symptom has disappeared. Record unresolved risk and any residual limitation.

Where a credible high-consequence safety concern cannot be resolved, restrict affected reliance. If the system cannot identify the affected boundary, use a proportionate broader restriction rather than guessing.

## 7. Continuity, recovery and succession

Before production reliance, document and test a continuity plan for foreseeable disruption, including:
- Loss or unavailability of the operational owner or key decision-maker.
- Reviewer departure, illness, conflict or loss of authority.
- Credential verification source outage.
- Provider outage, contract termination or insolvency.
- Loss of access to governance records or audit evidence.
- Security incident or suspected compromise.
- Loss of signing/administrative authority in any later approved technical design.
- Funding interruption or inability to meet recurring obligations.
- Failure to meet independent review capacity.

The plan should define:
1. Who can declare a disruption and who is the authorised deputy.
2. Which functions can continue safely and which must pause.
3. How current status is communicated without overclaiming.
4. How records and evidence are preserved and recovered.
5. How access is transferred or revoked safely.
6. How users and affected parties are informed where necessary.
7. How the process returns to operation after checks and explicit approval.

Succession must not automatically transfer professional reviewer authority or private credential access. Any new person must satisfy the same verification, scope, conflict and access requirements.

Continuity of the trust process is distinct from continuity of user workout data. The latter must remain independently protected and recoverable.

## 8. Service outage, pause and orderly exit

If a required governance function becomes unavailable or unreliable:
- Do not lower evidence, credential, independence or dual-review requirements.
- Do not represent stale or unknown approvals as current.
- Pause new approvals or restrict affected reliance where necessary.
- Preserve existing records and user-owned workout history.
- Provide a clear route for status clarification when service resumes.
- Reconcile queued decisions and updates before restoring current-status claims.

An orderly exit plan should cover:
- Authority to pause or terminate the service.
- Notice and communication responsibilities.
- Export of required governance records in a usable, integrity-preserving form.
- Privacy-compliant retention, transfer and disposal under G-09.
- Provider access revocation and confirmation of deletion/retention.
- Treatment of public approvals, withdrawn/superseded records and offline snapshots.
- Handover to a successor only after independent verification and owner approval.
- Explicit closure of unsupported claims and outstanding risks.

Do not leave an unmanaged live register, orphaned credentials, inaccessible audit history or ambiguous current-status claims after exit.

## 9. Reporting and owner oversight

The operational owner should provide periodic, proportionate reporting to the accountable policy owner. The report should include:
- Active reviewer counts and scope coverage, without unnecessary personal detail.
- Upcoming and overdue credential/appointment rechecks.
- Review volumes, backlog and independent dual-review capacity.
- Conflicts, recusals and unresolved independence exceptions.
- Restrictions, suspensions, withdrawals, appeals and open investigations.
- Freshness/propagation failures and offline status incidents.
- Privacy/security incidents and access-review outcomes.
- Audit findings, overdue corrective actions and residual risks.
- Budget, provider status, continuity readiness and upcoming renewals.

Reports should distinguish confirmed facts from estimates and unresolved issues. Public reporting should use aggregated or pseudonymous information and undergo privacy/disclosure review.

The owner must be able to narrow scope, pause approvals or suspend reliance where the process is not adequately resourced, independent, secure or auditable.

## 10. Boundaries and stop conditions

This proposal does not:
- Appoint an operational owner, deputy, reviewer, investigator, auditor or service provider.
- Commit funds, establish a budget or select a commercial/funding model.
- Set final audit frequency, staffing levels, service levels or review capacity targets.
- Establish a live reviewer service, register, complaints service or production approval process.
- Authorise real credential/personal-data collection.
- Select hosting, backup, monitoring, identity, signing or key-custody technology.
- Approve production catalogue content or individual reviewer decisions.
- Authorise runtime, database, schema, backup, C3, Adaptive Brain, main, Pages or deployment changes.

Operational roles, legal duties, funding and audit arrangements require further assessment and separate approvals. Any future technical implementation must follow the approved governance decisions rather than silently resolving open questions.

A PASS approves the governance principles and requirement for a funded, independently auditable, recoverable operating process before production reliance. It does not appoint people, commit expenditure, select providers, establish a live service or authorise technical implementation.
