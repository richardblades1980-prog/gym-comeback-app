# V21.2 — G-09 privacy, access and retention

**Status:** DESIGN PROPOSAL — owner decision required. This document proposes privacy, access-control, information-security, retention and incident-governance principles for any future reviewer-trust process. It does not establish a live register, collect personal or credential data, select a vendor, determine legal compliance, or authorise technical implementation.

**Governance basis:** G-01 project-owner accountability; G-02 Australia as the initial reviewer-governance jurisdiction; G-03 qualification framework; G-04 identity/credential verification framework; G-05 reviewer lifecycle; G-06 independence/conflicts; G-07 evidence/review protocols; and G-08 decisions, withdrawal and appeals, as approved in principle in the governance decision register.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve the privacy and information-governance principles that must be satisfied before any real reviewer identity, credential, conflict, complaint, investigation or appeal information is collected or processed.

The design should minimise personal information, separate public catalogue traceability from restricted governance records, limit access by role and purpose, define retention and disposal before collection, preserve necessary audit integrity, and provide a proportionate response to access, correction, incident and service-exit events.

This is a governance design, not legal advice or a finding that any proposed processing is lawful. Appropriate Australian privacy/legal advice and a documented assessment of applicable obligations are required before operation.

## 2. Core privacy principles

1. **Purpose limitation:** collect or use information only for a defined, documented governance purpose. Do not repurpose reviewer information for marketing, unrelated analytics, profiling or public promotion.
2. **Data minimisation:** collect the least information needed to verify identity, qualification, scope, conflicts, decisions and accountability. Prefer a verification result and source reference over retaining copies of identity documents or full credential files.
3. **Accuracy and provenance:** record source, verification date, verifier, result, scope and relevant limitations. Distinguish verified facts, reviewer declarations, third-party assertions and unresolved information.
4. **Restricted by default:** identity, contact, credential, conflict, complaint and investigation material is not public catalogue data. Access requires a legitimate assigned role and recorded purpose.
5. **Transparency:** before collection, provide an understandable notice explaining purpose, required fields, access, disclosure, retention, correction/contact routes, likely service providers and relevant cross-border handling.
6. **Security throughout the lifecycle:** protect information during collection, transfer, storage, use, backup, export, archival and disposal.
7. **Accountability:** assign responsibility for each dataset, access approval, review, incident response and retention decision. Record material access and administrative actions.
8. **Individual rights and fair process:** provide a documented way to raise access/correction requests and privacy concerns, subject to applicable law and legitimate confidentiality, safety and investigation constraints.
9. **No unsupported assurance:** encryption, access logs or a signed record do not by themselves establish lawful collection, professional competence, factual correctness or absence of a breach.
10. **No impact on user-history continuity:** reviewer-trust records must remain separate from users' historical workout records. Governance restrictions, provider changes or trust-data deletion must not erase or corrupt user workout history.

## 3. Information classification and separation

Use at least these conceptual classes. Exact technical labels and storage architecture belong to a later implementation gate.

### A. Public catalogue and approval traceability

Potentially publish only the minimum information needed to explain an approval:
- A pseudonymous reviewer reference, not a person's identity or contact details.
- Approved review scope and jurisdiction, expressed narrowly.
- Approval decision and current/historical/withdrawn status.
- Exact target and version reference.
- Applicable policy/checklist version and review date.
- Public-safe evidence references or concise rationale, after redaction and disclosure review.

Do not publish identity documents, credential account identifiers, personal contact details, private declarations, complaint details, investigation evidence or unredacted correspondence.

### B. Restricted reviewer identity and eligibility records

Potential minimum fields:
- Internal reviewer identifier and necessary contact route.
- Identity-check outcome, source, date, verifier and limitations.
- Credential/registration reference, issuing authority, scope and status-check result.
- Appointment scope, jurisdiction, effective date, review/expiry date and lifecycle state.
- Conflict assessment outcome and required safeguards, without retaining unnecessary narrative detail.

Where an authoritative source can confirm a fact, prefer a source reference, result and verification metadata over a stored document copy. Retain a document copy only if a documented necessity and lawful basis have been established, with a specific access and deletion rule.

### C. Restricted governance case records

Complaint, investigation, appeal, suspension, withdrawal and reinstatement records may contain sensitive allegations or personal information. Keep them separately permissioned from routine reviewer records and public catalogue data. Link by stable internal references rather than copying full case details into catalogue records.

### D. Security and operational records

Access logs, administrative events, backup metadata and incident records should contain only what is necessary for security and accountability. Avoid logging credentials, identity-document contents, authentication secrets or unnecessary personal details.

### E. User workout and health-related records

Do not combine reviewer governance records with individual user workout histories or any user-entered injury/health information. The reviewer trust process must not become a route to inspect user health or workout records. Any future processing of user health information requires its own purpose, privacy assessment and approval.

## 4. Access roles and least privilege

The eventual operating model should define named role classes and exact permissions before access is granted. At minimum, consider separating:

- **Policy owner:** accountable for policy and governance outcomes; not automatically entitled to browse raw identity documents or all case evidence.
- **Identity/credential verifier:** sees only the identity and credential evidence needed for the assigned verification.
- **Reviewer appointment authority:** sees verification outcomes, scope and appointment information needed to make an appointment decision, not necessarily source documents.
- **Review decision-maker:** sees the evidence and review records needed for the assigned decision.
- **Investigator:** receives only the case material necessary for an assigned investigation.
- **Appeal decision-maker:** independent of the original decision/investigation and given the record necessary to decide the appeal.
- **Privacy/security administrator:** handles access, security and privacy requests within a defined remit; administrative capability does not imply authority to make professional review decisions.
- **Auditor:** read-only, time-bounded access to a defined audit sample or evidence set.
- **Public/user:** sees only approved public traceability and user-facing status information.

Controls to require in the eventual procedure:
- Unique individual accounts; no shared operator accounts for substantive decisions.
- Least-privilege access with documented approval, scope and expiry.
- Strong authentication appropriate to risk; privileged access receives stronger controls.
- Prompt access removal when a role ends, scope changes or a conflict arises.
- Periodic access recertification and event-triggered review.
- Separate approval for bulk export, exceptional access and disclosure.
- Emergency access only where necessary, time-limited, justified and reviewed afterwards.
- Read-only access for auditors wherever practicable.
- No single person should be able to alter source evidence, approve their own access, make a disputed decision and erase the audit trail.

Exact identity provider, authentication methods, encryption choices, database design, key custody and logging implementation are not selected here.

## 5. Collection, verification and disclosure controls

Before collecting any real information:
1. Identify the responsible entity/operator and the purpose for each field.
2. Document the applicable legal basis and privacy notice.
3. Identify whether the information is sensitive or otherwise subject to additional protections.
4. Determine whether direct verification can avoid collecting a copy.
5. Define who can see the information and why.
6. Define retention, correction, disclosure and secure disposal rules.
7. Assess the provider, storage location, subprocessors, access locations and cross-border transfers.
8. Test the proposed process with synthetic data only.

Verification records should state what was checked and what the result means. A record that says “verified” without source, date, scope and verifier is insufficient. A source outage or inconclusive match must not be represented as successful verification.

Do not send identity documents or credential details through ordinary email, chat, issue trackers, public repository files or user-facing application telemetry. Do not place real personal or credential data in Git history, fixtures, test logs, screenshots or support exports.

Disclosure to a professional body, regulator, law-enforcement authority, insurer, legal adviser or another party must be assessed case by case, with an appropriate authority, purpose and record. Do not promise absolute confidentiality where lawful disclosure may be required; do not disclose merely because a request is convenient.

## 6. Retention, archival and disposal

Do not choose arbitrary universal retention periods in this proposal. Before operation, approve a schedule that identifies each record class, purpose, trigger, minimum/maximum period, review owner, legal hold rules and disposal method.

The schedule should separately consider:
- Identity and credential source evidence.
- Verification outcomes and appointment history.
- Conflict declarations and assessments.
- Review evidence and approval records.
- Complaints, investigation and appeal records.
- Access/security logs and incident records.
- Backups, exports and provider-held copies.
- Public approval history and superseded status records.

Retention should be no longer than necessary for the defined purpose and applicable obligations, while preserving records genuinely required for safety, accountability, dispute resolution or legal holds. A retention period for one record class must not silently determine the period for every other class.

When information reaches its approved disposal point:
- Delete or irreversibly de-identify it from active systems as specified.
- Define how deletion propagates to backups and provider-held copies, including backup expiry where immediate removal is not technically feasible.
- Record the disposal event without retaining the deleted personal content.
- Preserve only the minimum non-identifying audit fact needed to show that disposal occurred, where justified.
- Suspend routine disposal only under a documented, authorised legal or investigation hold, and review that hold.

Corrections should not silently rewrite an established decision history. Where accountability requires retaining an earlier decision, correct it by an appended, access-controlled record and restrict the inaccurate information from future use.

## 7. Access, correction and privacy concerns

The future process should provide a safe contact route for a person to:
- Ask what personal information is held about them.
- Request access or a copy where applicable.
- Ask for inaccurate or incomplete information to be corrected.
- Raise concerns about collection, use, access, disclosure, security or retention.
- Ask how a decision or verification result was reached, subject to appropriate limits.

Requests should be identity-checked proportionately, logged, triaged and answered within applicable legal requirements. If a request is refused or limited, record the reason and any available review route. Do not promise access to confidential third-party information, protected investigation material or information whose disclosure would create a material risk. Obtain appropriate advice where the applicable legal position is uncertain.

A correction request must not automatically change a professional finding or approval. Route substantive qualification, evidence or decision disputes through the G-08 process, while handling personal-data accuracy separately.

## 8. Security, incidents and breach response

The operating plan must define:
- Security ownership and escalation contacts.
- Prevention and detection controls appropriate to the information risk.
- Secure transfer and storage requirements.
- Backup protection, restoration testing and access restrictions.
- Monitoring and review of privileged and unusual access.
- Incident containment, evidence preservation and risk assessment.
- Notification and response obligations under applicable Australian law and contracts.
- Communication with affected people and relevant authorities where required.
- Remediation, lessons learned and documented closure.

An incident may include loss, unauthorised access, alteration, disclosure, unavailability or accidental publication. Do not wait for certainty of harm before containing a credible exposure. Preserve evidence and avoid speculative public statements.

Whether an incident triggers a statutory notification scheme, and the applicable timing and recipients, must be determined by appropriately qualified Australian privacy/legal advice based on the actual facts and responsible entity. This proposal does not decide those legal questions.

## 9. Providers, location and service exit

Before selecting or using any provider, assess:
- Contracting entity and responsibility for the information.
- Storage and processing locations, including support/admin access locations.
- Subprocessors and onward disclosures.
- Security controls, independent assurance and incident commitments.
- Retention, backup and deletion capabilities.
- Export formats, audit-log access and portability.
- Contract termination, transition support and verified deletion.
- Whether provider terms permit secondary use, model training, advertising or unrelated analytics.

Do not assume that choosing an Australian-hosted service alone resolves all privacy obligations or cross-border access risks. Record the assessment and obtain advice where needed.

A service exit or provider change must include a controlled transfer, integrity check, access revocation and deletion/retention confirmation. If the governance record service becomes unavailable, do not lower review or verification standards. Preserve user workout history independently.

## 10. Privacy assessment and pre-operation gate

Before any real personal or credential information is collected, require a documented privacy impact/risk assessment proportionate to the proposed processing. It should cover:
- Purposes, data flows, entities and responsibilities.
- Data inventory, sensitivity and necessity.
- Notice, consent or other applicable authority.
- Access roles, disclosure and cross-border handling.
- Threats, misuse cases and security controls.
- Retention, correction, disposal and legal holds.
- Provider/subprocessor review.
- Incident and breach response.
- Effects on affected people and mitigation.
- Independent privacy/legal review where appropriate.
- Owner sign-off on unresolved risks and residual risk.

No real data collection may begin merely because this proposal is approved. The detailed procedure, legal assessment, safeguards and any technical controls must be completed and separately authorised.

## 11. Boundaries and stop conditions

This proposal does not:
- Determine which Australian privacy laws or regulatory schemes apply to a future operating entity.
- Provide legal advice or declare a proposed process compliant.
- Select a data controller/operator, hosting region, vendor or subprocessor.
- Set final retention periods or statutory response deadlines.
- Authorise collection, upload, storage or processing of real identity, credential, conflict, complaint or appeal data.
- Authorise a live reviewer register or operational complaints service.
- Approve production catalogue content or individual reviewers.
- Select databases, schemas, encryption, key management, authentication, APIs or logging implementation.
- Authorise changes to runtime, backup, catalogue, C3, Adaptive Brain, main, Pages or deployment.

Use synthetic data only for any future design/testing work until a separate operational and technical approval is granted.
